## 1. AffectiveVLA: Safe and Intent-Aware VLA Control for Human-Humanoid Social Interaction

- 作者：Nikita Kuzmin, Georgii Demianchuk, Mariya Lezina, Yara Mahmoud, Iana Zhura, Miguel Altamirano Cabrera, Dzmitry Tsetserukou

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T09:29:21Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07712v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07712v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Social touch interactions, such as handshakes, fist bumps, and hugs, require humanoid robots to infer human intent, generate contact-rich motions, and maintain safe physical contact. We present AffectiveVLA, an intent-gated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pipeline deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid with dexterous hands. A lightweight RGB-based model recognizes interaction intent from two onboard cameras and converts it into a language prompt for the VLA policy. Low-confidence predictions suppress motion execution, reducing unintended activations. During physical contact, interaction-specific compliance profiles adjust arm impedance, while an independent voice-triggered mechanism initiates a latched safe-stop procedure. Using VR teleoperation, we collected approximately 1,300 multimodal interaction episodes and fine-tuned four VLA models: GR00T, RLDX-1, Being-H0.5, and UnifoLM-VLA-0. On-robot evaluation identifies GR00T as the best-performing standalone model. Integrating intent recognition increases its macro-F1 score from 0.75 to 0.90, while the intent model achieves a p95 inference latency of 6.27 ms. These results demonstrate that explicit intent gating improves the reliability of VLA-based social interaction without compromising real-time operation.

## 2. Learning Object-Centric Motion Priors from Human for Robotic Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Zhengdong Hong, Guofeng Zhang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-04T05:13:03Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05979v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05979v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Manipulating diverse objects with multi-fingered dexterous hands is challenging due to the high dimensionality and complex dynamics. Human-Object Interaction (HOI) datasets provide rich knowledge about task information and embodied interactions. Instead of solely imitating the human demonstrations, our method learns to holistically predict future hand-object states by leveraging these datasets. The predicted future states of the object can serve as a general-purpose reward term for reinforcement learning, reducing reliance on task-specific reward engineering and enhancing generalization across tasks. We conduct extensive experiments on three manipulation tasks in simulation and the real world. Our approach outperforms existing SOTA methods in both success rate and generalizability on novel objects. Furthermore, we validate the cross-embodiment compatibility of our methods by successfully deploying the skills on different robot hands.

## 3. GRASP: Gradient-Guided Sampling for Contact-Rich Physics-Based Motion Retargeting

- 作者：Yang Liu, Tong Wang, Yiming Bao, Heng Yang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-03T23:53:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05754v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05754v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



We study physics-based retargeting of human demonstrations for robot learning: finding robot controls that reproduce reference motions under contact dynamics. We propose GRASP (Gradient Recovery from Archived Samples for Proposals), which combines sampling-based exploration with gradient-guided refinement without requiring a differentiable simulator. GRASP-TO extends tempered sequential Monte Carlo by fitting local quadratic cost models to archived rollouts and using their gradients to guide Markov chain Monte Carlo rejuvenation. Metropolis-Hastings correction preserves the target distribution for each fixed proposal kernel, and a population analysis connects the recovered gradients to Gaussian smoothing. GRASP-MPC scales this optimizer to long demonstrations through warm-started receding-horizon planning. Experiments on dexterous hands and humanoids show accurate retargeting with a shared tracking objective and no task-specific contact rewards, including 92.8% success on 1352 bimanual OakInk2 demonstrations. The refined references improve PPO tracking in pen spinning and humanoid-object interaction. We also demonstrate dexterous manipulation and whole-body humanoid motion on real hardware.

## 4. Dexterous Robotic Hands in the Foundation-Model Era: A Full-Stack Survey of Hardware, Data, Cross-Embodiment Transfer, Learning, and Evaluation

- 作者：Ji Pei

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-04T16:13:29Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06724v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06724v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG



Dexterous robotic hands are increasingly coupled to large-scale human data and foundation models, yet progress in hardware, representation learning and evaluation is often assessed separately. This survey reviews work from January 2024 to early November 2026, with earlier studies included to establish technical lineages. We organize the literature into five interdependent layers: hardware; data collection and curation; cross-embodiment transfer; learning and foundation models; and evaluation and applications. Human hand-object understanding and tactile sensing provide two cross-cutting perspectives. A system-level architecture, comparative tables and five case analyses connect design choices across these layers. We distinguish within-study experimental comparisons from heterogeneous corpus statistics, vendor specifications and company-reported results. Three conclusions emerge. First, the value of human data depends on kinematic, visual and contact alignment, not recording volume alone. Second, shared action representations and foundation policies do not remove embodiment-specific constraints on force, latency and contact. Third, task completion is insufficient to establish physical correctness or deployment readiness. Evidence remains limited for zero-shot transfer of contact-rich skills between morphologically different hands, long-duration reliability, and cross-sensor tactile generalization. We identify eleven open problems and propose reporting interfaces and controlled comparisons that can make full-stack progress more measurable. The review is a structured narrative synthesis rather than a pooled performance ranking or an exhaustive systematic review.

## 5. PACT: Pose-Robust Actuation Co-Design for Tendon-Driven Robotic Hands

- 作者：Prashant Kumar, Rachael Granberry, Taylor Niehues, Andrew A. Stanley, Gabe Pirie

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-03T18:13:41Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05302v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05302v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Humanoid robotic hands must provide sufficient force, speed, and backdrivability within a human-scale envelope. Tendon-driven transmissions address this challenge by relocating actuators to the palm and forearm, but routing paths, moment arms, and capstan dimensions jointly determine both force capability and joint speed, so selecting them sequentially satisfies one requirement at the expense of the other. This paper presents a pose-robust actuation co-design for tendon-driven robot hands (PACT) framework that optimizes tendon routing, moment arms, and capstan dimensions under pose-dependent force requirements and drivetrain-bandwidth constraints. A feasible-force formulation enforces actuator bounds and equilibrium at all joints, while a max-min objective maximizes task-force coverage at the weakest pose. Applied to a four-degree-of-freedom finger, the framework produces an asymmetric N + 1 tendon topology using quasi-direct-drive actuators. Across eight poses, the design achieves task-force coverage of 0.88-1.00 and a predicted 5 Hz minimum isolated-joint flexion/extension bandwidth within a 70th-percentile human-size envelope. Prototype measurements deviate from predictions by up to 24.5%, primarily due to unmodeled transmission losses. These results indicate that co-designing routing with the drivetrain can meet pose-dependent force requirements without relaxing the bandwidth constraint, paving the way for human-sized robotic hands with human-level performance.

## 6. Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Yixiao Wang, Peiyi Chen, Mustafa Mukadam, Shreekant Gayaka, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Son Tran

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T18:50:06Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08458v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08458v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging because policies must acquire both perceptual grounding and precise control, while reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation can acquire rich control behaviors from large-scale interaction with privileged information. We introduce \textbf{Latent RL Prior}, a two-stage framework that reuses this control knowledge for data-efficient visual policy learning. During privileged pretraining, we distill large-scale RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context. During visual adaptation, we freeze the decoder and learn a visual interface that maps images to its latent context using action supervision alone, without privileged labels or latent targets. The same decoder can further be shared across arms by learning coordinated visual contexts for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated tasks, our method outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of $π_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime; on door lifting and bimanual assembly, it surpasses full-data baselines using $10\times$ and $5\times$ fewer demonstrations, respectively. Further analysis shows that direct visual-to-latent conditioning improves adaptation performance and training stability over routing visual predictions through the pretrained state encoder. Robustness to unseen mass variation and greater linear accessibility of contact-force information in decoder representations further provide evidence of retained RL-acquired control knowledge. ## 7. Vortex-Induced Torque in Grasping Soft Objects: Influence of Geometry and Pneumatic Parameters

- 作者：Roman Mykhailyshyn, Tomohiro Motoda, Ivan Virgala, Ann Majewicz Fey, Yukiyasu Domae

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-04T11:21:23Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06359v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06359v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Vortex grippers are commonly integrated into automated systems for handling objects with complex geometries in packaging, sorting, and assembling. However, their interaction with compliant objects remains difficult to predict, particularly with respect to the vortex-induced torque transmitted during grasping. This study investigates vortex-induced torque during the grasping of compliant objects and quantifies the coupled effects of supply pressure, tangential nozzle diameter, surface geometry, and curvature. The results show that increasing the tangential nozzle diameter primarily enhances the sensitivity of the generated torque to supply pressure rather than producing a uniformly higher torque. Surface geometry also strongly influences the rotational interaction, with dome-concave surfaces generally producing substantially greater torque than corresponding dome-convex surfaces, whereas the difference between cylindrical concave and convex surfaces is comparatively small. The strongest and most repeatable response was observed for a dome-concave surface exhibiting favorable object-gripper geometric matching. Among the evaluated machine-learning regression methods, Gaussian Process Regression provided the best overall predictive performance under repeated cross-validation, demonstrating that vortex-induced torque can be estimated from combined pneumatic and geometric parameters. These findings provide a basis for predicting and utilizing vortex-induced torque in the dexterous manipulation of soft objects.

## 9. Robocasa-Mem: Benchmarking Environment and Process Memory in Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation

- 作者：Sihang Chen, Chen Ye, Wei Chen, Xiaohan Lei, Zhipeng Tang, Sha Zhang, Yuan Zhang, Zixuan Chen, Jiajun Deng, Houqiang Li, Yanyong Zhang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-04T13:16:32Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06475v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06475v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



In the real world, mobile manipulation robots must perform complex object interactions and make multi-step decisions, often relying on task-relevant information that is no longer observable. However, existing benchmarks provide limited evaluation of such memory-dependent decision-making in mobile manipulation. To address this gap, we introduce RoboCasa-Mem, a new benchmark comprising nine mobile manipulation tasks that require agents to use interaction history at key decision points. Specifically, the tasks are grouped into two categories of memory: One is environment memory, which captures spatial locations, object attributes, and object relationships. The other is process memory, which captures single-task progress and multi-task scheduling. Particularly, we introduce Behavior-Conditioned Evaluation. For tasks with multiple valid execution paths, instead of fixing a single target or trajectory, the evaluator derives later task constraints from the robot's earlier actions. This allows us to evaluate whether later behavior is consistent with the robot's own interaction history while reducing shortcuts based on fixed target assignments. We provide 1,800 successful demonstrations for policy training. We also develop a baseline on our benchmark, namely Hierarchical Memory-Augmented Policy(HMAP). Its high-level planner uses structured entity and process memories to generate subtasks. Its low-level VLA controller uses subgoal-conditioned queries to incorporate VGGT-Omega geometry into action generation. Together, RoboCasa-Mem and HMAP baseline support systematic evaluation of memory-dependent behavior and the development of memory-aware mobile manipulation policies.

- 分类：cs.RO



Learning robust manipulation policies for diverse everyday objects remains challenging because objects with different materials exhibit distinct deformation and contact dynamics. While diffusion policies provide a powerful framework for modeling multimodal manipulation behaviors, they typically lack explicit physical reasoning and may produce averaged actions when facing heterogeneous objects. We present a physics-aware diffusion policy that augments visual imitation learning with object physical understanding. Our framework recovers material properties from visual demonstrations by coupling 3D Gaussian Splatting with differentiable Material Point Method simulation, where video guidance and particle-level constraints enable accurate physical parameter estimation. These results show that incorporating physical knowledge is critical for scalable robot manipulation in diverse environments.

## 11. OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions

- 作者：Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Sriniket Ambatipudi, Chen Xu, Haruki Nishimura, Masha Itkina, Roberto Martín-Martín

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T18:53:16Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08462v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08462v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform a wide range of robotic manipulation tasks, but often fail during deployment. Teaching them to recover from these failures requires substantial corrective data: existing approaches learn to recover from failures by directly associating corrective actions with the observations in which they were demonstrated. For instance, DAgger-like fine-tuning can require tens to hundreds of interactions, yet the resulting policy may still fail to generalize the learned corrections beyond the demonstrated scenarios. We hypothesize that semantics can capture the underlying reasons for failures and their corrections, enabling policies to learn from only a handful of corrective demonstrations and generalize those corrections to novel objects, and scene variations. We propose OopsieRecover, a framework that uses abduction to infer the intent behind a small set of corrective demonstrations and encodes the inferred knowledge as dense spatial representations and conditional costs. These costs capture when a behavior is undesirable and steer the policy toward more desirable action regions. We experiment across 6 tasks and variations in object poses, scenes, and related tasks, and find that only a handful of corrective demonstrations can enable a VLA to overcome failures across a broad range of novel situations, without requiring new corrective data for each variation. ## 12. Failure Prediction and Classification in Vision-Language-Action Models Via Calibrated Latent Probing

- 作者：Michal P. Podolinsky, Yunhao Yang, Daniel Little, Hong Lu, Rohan Siva, Olin Goudey, Neel P. Bhatt, Matthias Scheutz, Ufuk Topcu

- 来源：arXiv

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) enable general-purpose embodied intelligence, but their performance is brittle under distribution shift, where unanticipated failures can damage equipment or endanger people. Deploying VLAs in real-world systems therefore requires a runtime monitor that is timely, calibrated, and informative while being efficient enough to run on the edge. Existing methods fail to fulfill these requirements. We present VLA-Tug, a lightweight probe over the latent layers of a VLA backbone that improves early failure detection and bounds the missed-detection and false-alarm rates, with minimal inference overhead. We show that supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory increases early failure detection at matched false-positive rates by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline. Unlike existing methods, VLA-Tug predicts the failure mode to enable corrective actions, raising top-3 accuracy on unseen tasks by up to 44% over a majority baseline. VLA-Tug enables real-time monitoring where the well-timed and informative probe supports lightweight steering that improves task success. We release a dataset of 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes to support future work on latent failure detection in VLAs at https://vla-tug.github.io.

## 13. T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

- 作者：Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen, Yingyan, Lin, Chaojian Li

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T18:17:29Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08413v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08413v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

## 14. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. A clean evaluation of all 1,060 final policies agreed with the curves: of the 728 boundary-fault runs both curve checks passed, 723 scored no more than three standard deviations below fault-free seeds, though nearly all ended on other weights and, for reward faults, logged other returns.   Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

## 15. LLMs as MDP Designers: A Dependency-Aware Agentic Framework for Automated Robot Policy Generation

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. Rather than using large language models (LLMs) as execution-time decision makers, we use them as designers that construct RL pipelines before deployment, including Markov decision process (MDP) formulation and simulator implementation. We identify a phase-wise structure in this problem: MDP formulation is dependency-structured and sequential, whereas simulator implementation becomes modular once the formulation is fixed. ## 16. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 19. Arm Robot: AR-Enhanced Embodied Control and Visualization for Intuitive Robot Arm Manipulation

- 作者：Siyou Pei, Alexander Chen, Ronak Kaoshik, Ruofei Du, Yang Zhang

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2024-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- 分类：CoRR 2024



Embodied interaction has been introduced to human-robot interaction (HRI) as a type of teleoperation, in which users control robot arms with bodily action via handheld controllers or haptic gloves. Embodied teleoperation has made robot control intuitive to non-technical users, but differences between humans' and robots' capabilities \eg ranges of motion and response time, remain challenging. In response, we present Arm Robot, an embodied robot arm teleoperation system that helps users tackle human-robot discrepancies. Specifically, Arm Robot (1) includes AR visualization as real-time feedback on temporal and spatial discrepancies, and (2) allows users to change observing perspectives and expand action space. We conducted a user study (N=18) to investigate the usability of the Arm Robot and learn how users perceive the embodiment. Our results show users could use Arm Robot's features to effectively control the robot arm, providing insights for continued work in embodied HRI.

## 20. Automated Policy Analysis

- 作者：Konstantine Arkoudas, Shoshana Loeb, Ritu Chadha, C. Jason Chiang, Keith Whittaker

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2012-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=84XY6nHYsD

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=84XY6nHYsD

- 分类：POLICY 2012



Static analysis of access-control policies is becoming increasingly important. Such analysis can reveal errors and vulnerabilities in the policies, as well as logical inconsistencies, unintended effects, and discrepancies between different policies or different versions of the same policy. In the process, it helps policy developers to better understand the effects of their policies. Policy analysis has typically been done by hand. For instance, when a bug is discovered and corrected, the resulting policy is manually inspected to ensure that the fix works and that it does not introduce any new problems. But when the policies are large or their logical structure non-trivial, performing such analysis manually is tedious and error-prone. In this paper we show how to automate a wide array of useful policy analyses. This is accomplished by representing policies as logical formulas in the SMT (satisfiability-modulo-theory) subset of first-order logic, and couching analysis questions as SMT problems, which are then solved by efficient off-the-shelf SMT solvers. Because SMT solvers can reason about arithmetic and inductive data types, in addition to Boolean constraints, our system can handle many policies that cannot be analyzed by existing policy engines. We describe the formulation of a number of useful analyses (consistency, completeness, and observational equivalence), and report experimental results on the efficiency of our implementation for analyzing policies of various sizes and kinds of logical structure.
