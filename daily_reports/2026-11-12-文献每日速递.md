## 1. GAP: Generalized Adaptation Prior for Dexterous Piano Playing

- 作者：Jinyi Chen, Hongyi Li, Lei Jiang, Xianwei Liu, Yanhong Liang, Yulin Chen, Yucheng Tao, Yongbin Jin, Hongtao Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T09:24:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12111v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12111v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous hands enable versatile manipulation, but policies trained in simulation often degrade on hardware owing to non-idealities, and the high-dimensional action space makes real-world adaptation costly. Piano playing, which requires precise and coordinated multifinger motion, offers a representative testbed. We propose GAP (Generalized Adaptation Prior for Dexterous Piano Playing), a sample-efficient adaptation framework that learns how to adapt, rather than how to act, during simulation pretraining and transfers this adaptation prior to hardware. Between repeated executions, GAP uses feedback to adjust the actions of the next one: a gradient guidance network learns from paired executions how adjusting actions affects playing performance, and its gradients guide these adjustments. In simulation, GAP yields approximately 2.4 times the F1 scores improvement of the existing fast-adaptation baseline for dexterous piano playing under the same interaction budget. The learned prior also generalizes to musical pieces held out from pretraining. On a physical dexterous hand, the same simulation-pretrained prior improves F1 scores by 3.6%-23% across three pieces of varying difficulty after approximately 7 min of adaptation each. These results suggest a practical route to reduce the real-world interaction needed to deploy dexterous hands.

## 2. Fade to... Red? Scheduling Coloured Noise Exploration Supports Efficient Robot Learning with Reduced Movement Jerk

- 作者：Francisco M. López, Francisco Cruz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T04:44:59Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11868v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11868v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Exploration in deep reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control is commonly driven by temporally uncorrelated Gaussian (white) action noise, which leads to jerky motions with locally constrained exploration. In contrast, temporally correlated coloured noise, particularly pink noise, has been shown to support more efficient exploration in RL. Motivated by these findings, we inquire whether scheduling changes to the noise colour over the course of training can outperform white noise for continuous control robotics. We perform a systematic study of 49 linear schedules between white and red noise, together with generalised state-dependent exploration (gSDE), on four sparse-reward manipulation tasks with a Fetch arm and a Shadow Dexterous Hand. We find that the noise colour at the end of training does not affect learning efficiency or end performance, whereas fading towards red noise halves the jerk of exploration in every task. Balancing learning efficiency, end performance and smoothness, red noise emerges as the preferred strategy: it learns as efficiently as pink noise with almost 2-fold less movement jerk. We therefore recommend exploration schedulers that fade towards red noise as the default for off-policy robot learning.

## 3. Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids (DASH): Latency-Agnostic Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models for Humanoid Manipulation

- 作者：Bingzhang Wang, Nathan Zhao, Jialeng Ni, Jiajin Zheng, Yin Zhang, Kehua Chen, Jian Yao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T04:23:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11854v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11854v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Vision-language-action (VLA) models pair a vision-language backbone with an action head, but in closed-loop control the backbone sits on the control-critical path: while it computes, the robot keeps moving, so each executed action is conditioned on an increasingly stale observation. A larger backbone therefore directly blocks reactivity, especially in humanoid dexterous manipulation. We present DASH, a Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids that removes this coupling. DASH splits the policy into a slow vision-language model (System 2) and a fast flow-matching action head (System 1) that run as concurrent processes over the LCM communication protocol, so the control rate is set by the action head alone, independent of backbone size. The action head fuses stale semantics from a frozen backbone with fresh vision from its own ResNet encoder, desynchronized training exposes it to the staleness it meets at deployment, and an adaptive temporal aggregator aligns overlapping action chunks under variable inference latency. On an asynchronous RoboTwin benchmark that charges inference latency in simulated time, DASH holds 20 Hz control rate from 0.8B to 8B backbones while the synchronous rate falls from 9.7 to 4.1 Hz, with higher success at every size. On a real humanoid with dexterous hands, DASH achieves clearly higher pick-and-place success than the synchronous pipeline.

## 4. DexSeed: Expanding Human Hand-Object Interactions into Robot Demonstrations for Dexterous Policy Learning

- 作者：Huaijin Pi, Zehong Shen, Yong Wang, Kechun Xu, Changpeng Wang, Dingkun Wei, Xueyang Wang, Wenjia Wang, Xing Zhu, Yujun Shen, Taku Komura

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-10T13:52:18Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14092v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14092v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



We study how to generate diverse, physically executable robot demonstrations for dexterous vision-language-action (VLA) learning. Existing demonstration-generation methods typically rely on costly teleoperated examples. Meanwhile, human hand--object datasets provide a reusable source, but existing transfer methods mainly reproduce given interactions. Turning these interactions into VLA training data further requires coverage of different objects and manipulation locations, together with executable arm and finger actions, and visual observations. To achieve this, we present DexSeed, a framework for low-cost generation of dexterous robot data from human hand--object interactions. Our key idea is to expand human sequences while preserving their grasps and realizing the resulting motions under arm-tracking dynamics in physics simulation. Specifically, we organize source sequences by grasp mode with shared language descriptions. InteractionMesh-based retargeting and arm trajectory augmentation adapt these sequences to the robot hand, different object geometries, and pick-and-place locations while preserving local hand--object relationships. An RL finger-control policy produces executable rollouts, while the arm tracks precomputed trajectories. Rendered observations and recorded actions from successful rollouts are paired with grasp descriptions to fine-tune a VLA. Experiments show improved contact fidelity in retargeting and more reliable whole-arm execution than the evaluated baselines. Simulation evaluation and real-world deployment further demonstrate that a VLA fine-tuned solely on the generated demonstrations can execute language-specified grasps. Project page: https://robbyant-research.github.io/DexSeed/.

## 5. Compact and Wide-Force-Range Magnetorheological Hand Exoskeleton for Kinesthetic Feedback in Teleoperation

- 作者：Xianlong Mai, Rui Yu, Rui Chen, Daniele Leonardis, Wenbo Li, Domenico Chiaradia, Antonio Frisoli, Shuaishuai Sun

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T09:44:12Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12130v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12130v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, eess.SY



Hand kinesthetic feedback is crucial for restoring and enhancing human perception of force and displacement in robotic hand teleoperation. Wide perception range of force feedback is vital in rendering objects of different stiffness, especially for high-stiffness rendering. However, existing approaches struggle to achieve wide range of force feedback while maintaining compact and wearable design. This paper presents a hand exoskeleton system employing magnetorheological (MR) brakes to provide a wide range kinesthetic feedback during teleoperation. The MR brake provides a braking torque ranging from 0.01 to 0.918 Nm, while maintaining a compact structure with a total mass of only 39.8 g. The proposed exoskeleton can render fingertip forces up to 21 N while maintaining back-driving force of 1.01 N. Experimental results show that the system allows users to discriminate object stiffness using kinesthetic feedback alone. In a tele-grasping task, when both conditions achieved a 100% success rate, kinesthetic feedback led to a 28.2% reduction in object contact force compared to visual feedback only, enhancing object safety and operator immersion. Questionnaire results further indicate that kinesthetic feedback improves reliability, realism, and user confidence during teleoperation.

## 6. DexForesight: Grounding Dexterous Policies with Implicit Spatial Foresight

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11166v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous manipulation requires rich spatial foresight over both the surrounding scene and the robot hand. Existing world-action models often acquire such predictive knowledge through explicit future prediction. We introduce DexForesight, a framework that learns spatial foresight implicitly by injecting dynamics-aware world representations into the latent space of a dexterous policy. During training, DexForesight learns an action-conditioned spatial world model from offline scene depth, kinematic hand geometry, and demonstration trajectories. The world model predicts 3D hand-point motion and visual feature changes, capturing both articulated hand dynamics and scene changes under demonstrated actions, including the motion of fingers occluded from the cameras. On 11 DexJoCo simulation tasks, DexForesight improves the overall success rate of the base policy from 49.4% to 60.5%. ## 7. LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning

- 作者：Xuheng Zhang, Yiming Zhang, Jialin Gao, Hao Chen, Jiaming Liu, Ji Li, Runzhong Huang, Yinxi Wang, Chenyang Gu, Zihan Yang, Yaowei Liu, Zhonghao Yan, Ziyu Guo, Renrui Zhang, Peng Jia, Pheng-Ann Heng, Shanghang Zhang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-10T13:15:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14048v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14048v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.

## 9. PCTac: Mechanochromic Photonic-Crystal Tactile Sensor for Dexterous Manipulation

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12383v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Modern visuotactile sensors primarily sense contact by imaging the deformation of a compliant interface. Using techniques such as photometric stereo, they can recover detailed contact geometry, often represented as surface normals or depth. However, force-related information typically still needs to be inferred indirectly through calibration, geometric reconstruction, or learned mappings. Mechanochromic photonic-crystal (PC) materials offer an alternative physical encoding mechanism: contact loading changes their periodic microstructure and spectral response, directly transforming local stress into a spatially resolved structural-color field. Despite this potential, PC materials have rarely been explored as visuotactile sensors for closed-loop robot learning. We present PCTac, an open-source mechanochromic PC fingertip that uses a dense hue field as its tactile observation. PCTac Hue also achieves the highest observed success rate on each evaluated task, including Perturbed Test-Tube Insertion, where it achieves 16/30 successes versus 13/30 for tactile RGB baselines and 1/30 for vision-only. These results demonstrate the potential of mechanochromic structural color as a compact, load-sensitive tactile representation for robot learning and closed-loop manipulation. ## 10. From Egocentric Human Video to Robot Manipulation in the Era of Embodied Intelligence

- 分类：cs.RO



Egocentric human video (EHV) records close-range hand-object interaction (HOI) and the temporal structure of everyday activities, offering a potentially scalable source of experience for robot manipulation. Using this experience for robot control requires recovering useful supervision without recorded robot actions and accounting for differences in morphology, viewpoint, and action space. We define Ego2Robot learning as the process of learning or adapting robot manipulation policies from EHV, organized into four learning paradigms: explicit embodiment transfer, explicit action and task supervision, egocentric video representation pretraining, and latent action and dynamics modeling. This paper links EHV data's contribution to the task information preserved and the robot-specific grounding used during training or deployment. Finally, we identify challenges in physical feasibility, agent coordination, in-context adaptation, controlled evaluation, and predictive control.

## 11. HARP-Bench: Benchmarking Robot Manipulation Around Active Humans

- 作者：Qinhong Zhou, Riley Zong, Chunru Lin, Diyi Hu, Shivraj Singh Bhatti, Hongxin Zhang, Lin Qiu, Hanqing Zeng, Chuang Gan

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-08T04:16:15Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11123v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11123v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Recent robot manipulation benchmarks have enabled systematic evaluation of general-purpose robot policies across diverse objects, scenes, instructions, and embodiments. However, most existing benchmarks still assume that the robot is the only active agent and that task semantics are determined primarily by the instruction and object state. This assumption overlooks a common source of real-world variation: humans actively sharing the workspace. In such settings, humans may reveal an implicit goal, complete part of the task, temporarily change object availability, or constrain safe robot motion while working nearby. We introduce HARP-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating robot manipulation policies in workspaces shared with active humans. HARP-Bench treats human activity as part of the manipulation problem, rather than as background scene variation. It organizes human-present manipulation into four conditions: Cueing, Contribution, Interruption, and Co-presence. These conditions are instantiated through everyday manipulation tasks, including paired human-present variants for controlled comparison with standard manipulation. HARP-Bench also provides a demonstration-generation pipeline for dynamic human-present settings. We evaluate VLA models, imitation-learning methods, and heuristic policies on HARP-Bench. Human-present variations challenge current policies, and higher task success does not consistently improve safety. Quantitative failure analysis identifies manipulation and recovery as the dominant remaining challenges. Training with HARP-Bench demonstrations improves human-present performance in simulation. A real-world study on one task family shows improved performance under human contribution, but not interruption, providing initial evidence of the data's utility and identifying limits of transfer. Code is available at https://github.com/UMass-Embodied-AGI/harp.

## 12. Tactile Sensors Are an Effective Substitute for Wrist Cameras in Contact-Rich or Occluded Manipulation

- 作者：Aijia Yang, Arsh Tangri, Sergey Zakharov, Krishna Murthy Jatavallabhula

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-07T05:33:17Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10490v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10490v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Learned manipulation policies often rely on wrist-mounted cameras for close-range visual feedback that complements external views of the workspace. However, contact-rich manipulation requires establishing and maintaining contacts that can remain hidden even from wrist cameras. Tactile sensors mounted on fingers can reliably and directly measure contact, without needing visual access. In this work, we show that this feedback enables policies to recover the performance advantage of wrist cameras using scene and egocentric vision together with touch. Our approach incorporates two low-cost tactile arrays into a visuotactile ACT policy, without wrist images during training or deployment. We evaluate six real-world task families spanning placement, insertion, occluded retrieval, and sustained-contact tool use. On three contact-rich tasks, we independently vary wrist-camera and tactile availability while keeping the demonstrations, policy architecture, and optimization settings fixed. Adding wrist cameras improves success by 18.3-28.3 percentage points over vision without wrist images. Adding tactile sensing instead improves success by 31.7-60.0 percentage points and yields higher observed success than vision-only policies with wrist cameras on all three tasks, with significant advantages on two under Holm-corrected tests. These results support finger-mounted tactile sensing as an effective alternative to wrist cameras in the evaluated contact-rich settings.

## 13. Is Your Robot's Memory Just a Clock? Temporal Shortcuts in Memory-Augmented VLA Policies

- 作者：Chubin Zhang, Bo An

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-10T18:47:57Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14488v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14488v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Memory-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) policies are meant to remember what happened. Many of them, however, fill memory by time: they sample frames uniformly across the episode and stamp each frame with its time step. Because time passes even when nothing happens, such a memory may not tell waiting from working. We show that it does not. On the counting tasks of RoboMME, we make the robot briefly pause or fidget its wrist. Such a null event changes nothing the task depends on, yet a released memory VLA counts the interval as work done and stops early. We call this failure phantom progress. It cuts success from 47 to 16 of 50 on one task and from 44 to 10 on the other. When the same frames are withheld from memory, success returns, so the cause lies in memory rather than in the interruption itself. We trace the failure to a temporal shortcut: because demonstrations proceed at a steady pace, elapsed time predicts progress. Indeed, rescaling the time stamps alone makes the policy stop early when the clock runs fast and repeat when it runs slow. Surprisingly, removing the time encoding does not cure the failure, because the frames of a null event still occupy slots of the time-sampled memory. Across RoboMME's released designs, vulnerability follows how memory is indexed. Of the fixes the diagnosis suggests, training with injected pauses does not remove the failure and impairs counting, whereas indexing memory by events recovers half or more of the loss without retraining. A memory meant to remember what happened should count events rather than clock ticks.

## 14. One Policy for 100 Household Tasks: Our Solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge

- 作者：Ilia Larchenko, Gleb Zarin, Atindra Nair, Keerti Manney, Vitaly Kleban, Mirko Kiefer, Anjana Yodaiken

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-10T18:06:16Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14432v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14432v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG



We describe our solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge: 100 long-horizon household tasks in the OmniGibson simulator. We placed 9th on the final leaderboard, the only independent team in the top 10. The core of the solution is a single vision-language-action (VLA) model, one checkpoint for all 100 tasks, built on our 1st-place 2025 solution, which is based on $π_{0.5}$. On the 50 tasks shared with 2025, its Q rises from 0.260 to 0.371; across all 100 tasks it scores 0.392. Our main conclusion from 2025 was that the model was undertrained, so most of the 2026 work went into training the model for longer, optimizing some training parameters, making each training step slightly faster and reducing the gradient noise: a migration from 50 to 100 tasks (semantic stages built from the demonstrations' skill annotations, task and stage embeddings of new tasks warm-started from similar old ones, and a 21-dimensional action space matching the teleoperation controls); variance-reduced multi-sample flow matching with a simple noise-vs-compute model that picks the number of action-expert samples per observation without ablations; and a 200k-step training run, about one pass over the 211M frames of the demonstrations. A cloud evaluation infrastructure keeps a shared record with provenance, episode diagnostics and restorable simulator states, and runs six simulators on one GPU together faster than real time. On top of the learned policy, 14 tasks are routed to a GPT-6 Astra coding agent that controls the robot through observation and motion tools, starting every episode from a frozen copy of its own practice session on training instances. Across these 14 tasks, Astra's mean Q is 0.639, against 0.108 for the learned policy. The public submission included only part of the Astra results and scored 0.446 Q with 27.3% full-task success.

## 15. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. A clean evaluation of all 1,060 final policies agreed with the curves: of the 728 boundary-fault runs both curve checks passed, 723 scored no more than three standard deviations below fault-free seeds, though nearly all ended on other weights and, for reward faults, logged other returns.   Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

## 16. LLMs as MDP Designers: A Dependency-Aware Agentic Framework for Automated Robot Policy Generation

- 作者：Hong Je-Gal, Jinwoo Kim, Chanbin Yi, Hyun-Suk Lee

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-03T14:29:36Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04974v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04974v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. Rather than using large language models (LLMs) as execution-time decision makers, we use them as designers that construct RL pipelines before deployment, including Markov decision process (MDP) formulation and simulator implementation. We identify a phase-wise structure in this problem: MDP formulation is dependency-structured and sequential, whereas simulator implementation becomes modular once the formulation is fixed. Based on this structure, we propose ARPG, a dependency-aware agentic framework that performs sequential MDP modeling, localized verification and correction, and simulator-structured implementation while externalizing intermediate artifacts. ARPG targets a key failure mode of automated policy generation: executable code may still yield non-learnable policies when state, action, reward, and termination artifacts are not semantically aligned. Experiments in Isaac Sim show that ARPG improves modeling correctness, environment executability, and policy-generation success compared with monolithic LLM baselines and off-the-shelf agentic systems. Additional demonstrations on custom robotics, standard RL, and engineering-domain MDPs further suggest that the same modeling pipeline can be reused beyond the main benchmark suite.

Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 18. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 来源：OpenReview

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.

## 19. DexUMI: Using Human Hand as the Universal Manipulation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Mengda Xu, Han Zhang, Yifan Hou, Zhenjia Xu, Linxi Fan, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2025-09-19T19:43:41Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.

## 20. Arm Robot: AR-Enhanced Embodied Control and Visualization for Intuitive Robot Arm Manipulation

- 作者：Siyou Pei, Alexander Chen, Ronak Kaoshik, Ruofei Du, Yang Zhang

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2024-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- 分类：CoRR 2024



Embodied interaction has been introduced to human-robot interaction (HRI) as a type of teleoperation, in which users control robot arms with bodily action via handheld controllers or haptic gloves. Embodied teleoperation has made robot control intuitive to non-technical users, but differences between humans' and robots' capabilities \eg ranges of motion and response time, remain challenging. In response, we present Arm Robot, an embodied robot arm teleoperation system that helps users tackle human-robot discrepancies. Specifically, Arm Robot (1) includes AR visualization as real-time feedback on temporal and spatial discrepancies, and (2) allows users to change observing perspectives and expand action space. We conducted a user study (N=18) to investigate the usability of the Arm Robot and learn how users perceive the embodiment. Our results show users could use Arm Robot's features to effectively control the robot arm, providing insights for continued work in embodied HRI.
