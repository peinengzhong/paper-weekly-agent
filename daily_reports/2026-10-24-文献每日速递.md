## 1. PiHand: Latent Hand-Object Interaction Priors for Physics-Based Policy Learning

- 作者：Niklas Berndt, Xianghui Xie, Enes Duran, Yixiao Wang, Gerard Pons-Moll

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-22T13:59:19Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31299v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31299v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Synthesizing physically grounded dexterous hand-object interactions is important for VR/AR, gaming, and robotics, yet remains challenging because subtle contact dynamics, diverse object geometries, and target trajectories must be jointly respected while maintaining natural grasps. Existing methods either track mocap trajectories with limited transfer to novel objects, or rely on hand-only generative priors that fail to capture coupled hand-object motion, sacrificing contact fidelity and sample efficiency. To tackle these challenges, we introduce PiHand, a Physically grounded Interaction-aware Hand motion synthesis framework. PiHand first trains expert policies that track mocap demonstrations in simulation, distills them into a unified hand-object interaction prior using a goal-conditioned VAE, and finally solve diverse dexterous manipulation tasks through bounded residual exploration in its latent space. With only a single reward and no complex curriculum training, this yields general tabletop manipulation policies that generalize to unseen objects and trajectories on GRAB and DexYCB. The same prior transfers to object catching and bimanual handover, two tasks that are rarely seen in its training data. Across all tasks, PiHand consistently outperforms baselines in all metrics. We will release the processed data and model checkpoints.

## 2. PHD-Hand: A Printable Humanoid Dexterous Hand with Linkage-Driven 3-DOA Digits

- 作者：Neel Adwani, Muhaiminul Islam Akash, Saffia Ouali, Ethan Liu, Cong Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-21T01:01:57Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29030v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29030v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Currently, dexterous humanoid robot hands are widely considered the final hardware bottleneck that hampers generalist robots from physically assisting people at home and work. As conventional tendon-driven designs suffer from high cost, demanding maintenance, poor reliability, and short durability, alternative linkage-driven designs have increasingly gained attention. Despite their potential to avoid the weaknesses of tendon-driven hands, integrating multiple degrees of actuation (DOA) in a single digit with linkage drivetrains is a challenge for design and fabrication. Only a few studies have managed to present functional humanoid hands that are completely linkage-driven and have at least three DOAs per digit without compromising actuator placement and other design preferences. Our work introduces a new linkage-driven hand featuring a unique flexure hinge. Together with elastic drive rods made of spring steel, the linkage drivetrains are significantly simplified. Unlike other linkage-driven designs of similar dexterity, our design does not require many miniature moving parts. As a result, the hand can be fabricated easily with a budget 3D printer. This not only significantly lowers the barrier to entry for using and developing dexterous humanoid robot hand functions, but also allows the hands to be sized much closer to regular human hands. The design offers desirable kinematic advantages as well. A prototype is evaluated for grasping and object manipulation. In addition, this paper presents our latest findings on kinematics and control of the hand.

## 3. COWBOY: A Scalable Sim-to-Real Framework for Learning Contextual Whole-Body Manipulation

- 分类：cs.RO



Mobile manipulators must adapt not only to objects, but also to the spatial context in which they appear: where to position the base, how to manipulate, and where to look while navigating surrounding scene geometry. To address this, we present COWBOY, a scalable sim-to-real framework for learning such contextual whole-body manipulation. On whole-body dexterous grasping, COWBOY transfers zero-shot to hundreds of objects and diverse in-the-wild scenes, spanning cluttered tabletops, shelves, drawers, and other unstructured settings. ## 4. Touch-JEPA: Tactile Representation Learning from Hand-Object Video with Limited Tactile Supervision

- 作者：Minseong Kweon, Qiao Feng, Yiming Huang, Lingjie Liu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T16:56:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28519v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28519v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Understanding contact and tactile magnitude in hand-object interaction is a key step toward physically intelligent systems, yet learning such representations is limited by the cost and restricted coverage of tactile measurements. We present Touch-JEPA, a framework for learning tactile representations from hand-object videos with limited tactile supervision. We first introduce a self-supervised pretraining objective that learns from video alone without tactile supervision. Using video-derived hand geometry, Touch-JEPA predicts masked visual features at corresponding hand-surface locations from spatial and temporal context, encouraging the representation to capture latent contact dynamics. We then extend this formulation to joint representation learning, where masked prediction is applied to all videos while tactile supervision is available only for a labeled subset, grounding the shared representation in measured contact and magnitude. With self-supervised pretraining alone, simple supervised readouts on frozen Touch-JEPA features outperform supervised baselines in contact discrimination and tactile magnitude estimation across three human-hand benchmarks, and transfer to robotic hands without robot tactile training data. Joint learning with limited tactile supervision further improves contact and magnitude estimation on the human-hand benchmarks. Together, these results show that hand-object video can provide scalable self-supervision for tactile representation learning, while limited tactile measurements provide complementary physical grounding.

## 5. What Novice Operators See Changes What the Policy Learns: A User Study of Keypoint Hand Visual Representation for Teleoperated Demonstration

- 作者：Amane Yamaguchi, Shohei Kamiguchi, Masashi Hamaya, Shigeo Yoshida, Takuji Narumi, Mai Nishimura

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T09:22:21Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27907v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27907v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.HC



Imitation learning increasingly relies on novice operators, whose demonstrations fall short of experts' in number and inter-trial consistency. Operators act on what they are shown, yet how the rendering of the robot affects their demonstrations, and the policies trained on them, has not been examined. We study this question with PointHand, a teleoperation interface that renders a multi-fingered robot hand as sparse keypoints at its fingertips and joints, the same keypoints a policy can observe. In a simulated mug-in-box task, 18 novices and 4 experts collected 2,200 trials under three renderings: the real shape of the hand, all keypoints emphasized, or the thumb and index finger alone emphasized. The novices shown the thumb and index finger emphasized collected 441 successful demonstrations from 600 trials, against 320 for those shown the real shape. We then trained diffusion policies on these demonstrations, replacing the scene point cloud and proprioceptive state they observe with the hand keypoints and two object keypoints. Within 400 steps these demonstrations went from the weakest of the three novice conditions under the point-cloud policy (46.3%) to the strongest under the keypoint policy (57.3%), a gain of +11.0 percentage points in all five seeds. Those collected with all keypoints emphasized changed by at most +0.7 percentage points, and in no more than three seeds. The value of a demonstration therefore depends on the rendering shown during collection and on what the policy observes together. A shared representation can pay off on both sides, in the number of successful demonstrations and in the success rate of the policy trained on them. ## 6. DeXplicit: Making Physical State Explicit in Dexterous Manipulation World Models

- 分类：cs.CV



Before acting on an object, a robot needs to anticipate how its hand motion will change the physical world and what the resulting scene will look like. Existing approaches typically entangle action-conditioned dynamics, camera motion, and visual synthesis within a video generation model, or represent dynamics as point trajectories without explicit object structure. We introduce DeXplicit, a dexterous manipulation world model that instead predicts how the world evolves explicitly in metric 3D before rendering its visual consequences. ## 8. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

End-to-end control policies trained via Reinforcement Learning (RL) have emerged as an alternative to conventional surgical robotic control approaches, with recent studies exploring state-based policies trained on privileged state observations. Vision-based RL provides an alternative by learning manipulation policies directly from RGB observations, avoiding explicit state estimation and manually engineered perception pipelines while enabling policies to acquire task-relevant visual representations. ## 11. Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success

- 作者：Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-22T17:59:51Z

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31670v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.RO



Robotic foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, which motivates cheap offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, different modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To examine this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across embodied model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones even before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to embodied models and make sensorimotor alignment empirically measurable, offering insight into why an embodied model succeeds beyond its closed-loop success rate.

- 分类：cs.RO



Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. These findings suggest that simple probes of VLA internals can be used to develop reliable runtime monitors, opening a path toward building policies that can act reliably and monitor their execution without external foundation models.

## 13. Echo: Coupled Action and Task Status Signal Denoising for Vision Language Action Models

- 作者：Yufeng Liu, Bohao Fan, Juntu Zhao, Xinyi Wang, Hang Yu, Wenzhuan Luo, Yingzhi Wang, Qinghai Li, Yingdong Hu, Junliang Guo, Richard Wang, Dequan Wang, Yang Gao, Cheng Ma

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31569v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31569v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

## 14. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

- 作者：Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T14:25:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20255v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

## 16. Reinforcement Learning from Real-Robot Interaction without High-Quality State Estimates

- 作者：Ryan Gao, Ashvin Anilkumar, Nicholas Corrado, William Cong, Josiah Hanna

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-02T21:59:06Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14042v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14042v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A longstanding goal for robot reinforcement learning (RL) is that robots learn from their own physical experience, as humans and animals do. A key obstacle to learning directly from physical experience, especially outside instrumented lab environments, is that the state of the world is usually unobservable to the learner and must be estimated from on-board sensing. Errors in that estimate corrupt both the policy input and potentially the reward signal. We study this challenge using the contact-rich task of a bipedal robot learning to score soccer goals. We estimate all key state variables on-board with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) and no external instrumentation (e.g., motion capture or overhead cameras). Building on RLPD, we introduce RSSM-RLPD and apply it to the task of a NAO robot learning to dribble a soccer ball into a goal. We show that the robot learns to score from multiple starting positions after 30 minutes of real-world interaction, reaching 100% success against 0% for a feed-forward policy under the same training budget. We complement our physical robot experiments with a simulation study and show that RSSM-RLPD learns policies that achieve a higher success rate than behavior cloning, a feed-forward, and GRU baseline. Taken together, our results show that the addition of a learned state representation to existing on-robot learning methods enables RL from real-robot data even with a high degree of partial observability.

Video diffusion models are increasingly developed into specialized models for diverse downstream tasks, and this development often includes a distillation stage, for example to accelerate sampling or to improve long-video generation. This stage is typically repeated for every specialized model. We introduce LongLive-Plug, a once-for-all distillation framework that learns reusable capabilities as LoRAs on a base model for training-free, plug-and-play deployment to compatible downstream models. These capabilities include single-pass classifier-free guidance, few-step sampling, and long-context error correction for autoregressive generation. The adapters remain reusable even when downstream models add conditioning branches, expand output channels. Despite training at a fixed guidance scale, our dedicated CFG LoRA provides text guidance control through its inference weight. Combining it with a few-step LoRA simultaneously preserves few-step generation and CFG controllability on downstream tasks. We verify training-free deployment on 54 downstream models across three backbone families and eight task categories, including world modeling, robotics, editing, and multimodal generation. The approach may support additional compatible models. Each capability can thus be distilled once per backbone family and reused without per-target retraining.

## 19. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.

## 20. DexUMI: Using Human Hand as the Universal Manipulation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Mengda Xu, Han Zhang, Yifan Hou, Zhenjia Xu, Linxi Fan, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2025-09-19T19:43:41Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.
