## 1. Catch It All!: Generalizable Dynamic Catching with a Dexterous Hand

- 作者：Arth Shukla, Kehlani Fay, Huaze Liu, Darin Djapri, Alexiy Buynitsky, Nicklas Hansen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-28T23:17:20Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39044v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39044v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Catching arbitrary objects requires generalization to novel projectiles and real-time adaptation to unpredictable contact dynamics under noisy visual observations. This paper presents Catch It All!, a sim-to-real catching framework for humanoid robots that generalizes and adapts to unseen objects with variable physical properties for underhand and overhand catches, using only one egocentric camera. We learn policies which generalize to unseen objects with varying geometry, deformability, elasticity, and physical properties. Effective grasps which adapt real-time to object poses and shapes are learned through careful grasp reward design. We provide safety-aware curricula which enable safe, high-frequency action execution near hardware limits, including wrist torque limits as low as 5 Nm. We validate our system by deploying zero-shot on a Unitree G1. With only one egocentric camera, Catch It All! successfully performs underhand and overhand catches on 9 unseen objects not seen during training, including low-rebound plushes, semi-deformable toys and chip bags, and rigid-elastic objects with high rebound on contact.

## 3. A fully articulated humanoid dexterous hand with human-level joint structure enabled by linkage-based actuation

- 作者：Jihao Li, Jianguo Wang, Haotian Guo, Yanzhe Wang, Guodong Lu, Huixu Dong

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-27T07:53:51Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36789v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36789v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Humanoid dexterous hands are a core component of embodied intelligence, enabling robots to translate high-level intent into rich and adaptive physical interaction. However, existing designs remain constrained by a persistent trade-off among anthropomorphic kinematic complexity, mechanical robustness, load capacity and control simplicity, limiting dexterous manipulation in unstructured environments. Here we present Torch-Hand, a fully articulated humanoid dexterous hand that replicates the joint structure and motion hierarchy of the human hand with 21 degrees of freedom (DOF), comprising a highly articulated five-DOF thumb and four DOF in each of the remaining fingers. The differentiated thumb integrates multi-axis CMC motion to provide broad opposition and flexible contact orientation relative to the four fingers. Unlike tendon-driven or hybrid actuation approaches, Torch-Hand adopts an entirely linkage-based transmission architecture. By integrating screw-linkage actuation, the hand achieves high grasping force and substantial payload capacity while maintaining a compact anthropomorphic form. Its actuation architecture is optimized for control tractability, with all actuators integrated within the palm and arranged in a serial-robot-like configuration that establishes a direct correspondence between motor motion and joint kinematics. This architecture mechanically decouples functional DOF to reduce unintended inter-joint motion propagation, while allowing independently addressable DOF to be actively coordinated into coupled multi-joint motion patterns during complex manipulation. Comprehensive experiments assess reachable workspace, coordinated fingertip trajectories, thumb opposition, 33-grasp taxonomy coverage, grasping force, payload capacity and task-level manipulation. These results establish Torch-Hand as a robust hardware foundation for dexterous embodied manipulation.

## 5. Kinematic-Tactile Aligned Dexterous Retargeting with Contact Wrench Space Alignment

- 作者：Xinyuan He, Ziheng Xu, Yueyuan Chen, Yufan Ye, Yuanshuo Tan, Huiming Pan, Jianjun Meng, Shuo Jiang, Peter B. Shull

- 来源：arXiv

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39582v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39582v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Human demonstrations provide a natural and scalable source of data for learning dexterous manipulation policies. However, existing retargeting methods primarily transfer human kinematics, while the tactile information underlying physical interaction is rarely preserved across embodiments. To address this limitation, we present a hierarchical three-stage framework for kinematic-tactile aligned dexterous retargeting. We use object-centric contact wrench space as a physical representation for tactile alignment between human and robot hands. The framework first aligns hand kinematics and contact geometry, and then constructs a human wrench support profile from tactile forces and contact geometry. In the real robot experiments, our method achieves a 96.7\% replay success rate and higher wrench transfer fidelity than representative retargeting baselines. The framework enables physically grounded transfer of kinematic and tactile information from human demonstrations to robot hands, providing a path toward scalable learning of contact-rich dexterous manipulation.

## 7. TouchBridge: Tactile-Conditioned Human-to-Robot Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation

- 分类：cs.RO



Bimanual dexterous manipulation requires continuous coordination of wrist motion, finger contact, and object motion. Human videos provide rich manipulation experience. Using this experience for robot control requires aligning human poses with robot commands and accounting for embodiment-specific kinematics and contact dynamics. We present AcrossDEX 1.0, a learning system that connects human motion pretraining, simulation-based expert learning, robot demonstration collection, and visual closed-loop execution. Pretraining on HOT3D Aria predicts one second of bimanual motion from five images and thirty annotated hand states over the preceding second. ## 9. Coupled Guidance for Flow Matching

- 作者：Mateo Clémente, Léo Maxime Brunswic, Rui Heng Yang, Amir Rasouli

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T14:12:07Z

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40109v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG, cs.RO



Guidance methods are essential tools for steering the generation process in diffusion models, improving the generalizability of stochastic robotic controllers and enabling high-quality controlled image synthesis. Despite strong parallels between flow-matching and diffusion, integrating guidance into flow-matching models remains quite challenging. Recent work has proposed general guidance frameworks for flow-matching, however, they are direct adaptations of diffusion guidance methods and can prove unstable. In this work, we conduct a theoretical analysis of guidance in flow-matching and show that a naive translation of diffusion-based guidance to flow-matching breaks the coupling distribution used during training, resulting in weaker control over sampling accuracy. Our findings show that our framework improves sample quality and guidance stability across all tasks, without imposing any additional overhead.

## 10. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37550v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Manipulation policies are evaluated predominantly by binary task success, which cannot tell whether the commanded gesture is the one the object's kinematics admit. We introduce Constraint-Referenced Strategy (CRS), which projects the commanded end-effector twist onto the object's joint axis, separating progress along the joint from off-constraint waste. CRS requires a joint model and a joint-following scripted controller derived from it, but no recorded demonstration or learned judge. ## 16. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 17. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

- 作者：Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T14:25:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20255v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

## 18. Evo-RL: Iterative Robot Reinforcement Learning from Selective Experience

- 作者：Kai Ye, Yifan Jin, Qianhang Luo, Hongru Zhang, Jiedong Zhang, Shuyuan Hu, Song-Yuan Wu, Jihai Xu, Jie Zhao, Bo Zhao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-09T10:19:54Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13345v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13345v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Existing real-world reinforcement learning methods improve robot policies by combining human corrections with autonomous rollouts. However, accumulated experience varies in its value for policy improvement, and even successful trajectories can contain inefficient or redundant behavior, motivating more selective experience reuse. To this end, we propose Evo-RL, an iterative reinforcement learning method that combines human interventions with advantage-guided experience selection. Evo-RL uses human corrections and experience with positive estimated advantages from successful trajectories to iteratively refine the policy. To support learning from these different experience sources, we introduce RW-RL-Dataset, which spans over 1,000 hours across four robot embodiments. On a real-world high-precision manipulation task, Evo-RL improves attempt success rate over RECAP by 30.46\%. Our code, model checkpoints, and dataset are publicly available.

## 19. vLLM-Omni Technical Report: A Unified Serving Runtime for Omni-Modality Generation

- 作者：vLLM-Omni Team

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-07T02:04:28Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09307v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09307v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.DC



Interaction with intelligent systems is expanding beyond text-centric chatbots and coding agents. Speech-native assistants, visual generation and editing, world-model environments, and robot action loops require models that emit text, audio, images, video, and actions. These models differ in execution pattern: multi-stage autoregressive omni and TTS pipelines, iterative diffusion or flow-matching generators, and longer-lived world-model or robot loops that carry state across steps. As a result, serving is no longer a single text decode loop, but a heterogeneous multi-stage workflow with cross-stage transfer, streaming, and session-shaped interaction.   Existing inference stacks are typically optimized for one architecture family. LLM servers deepen autoregressive scheduling and KV management, while diffusion stacks deepen denoising and parallel generation. Neither provides a shared control plane for pipelines that emit speech, pixels, or actions through separate generators, so production deployments often fall back to ad-hoc composition across disjoint runtimes.   We present vLLM-Omni, a unified serving runtime for omni-modality generation. vLLM-Omni organizes each workload as a multi-stage pipeline under a single orchestrator that admits requests, advances them across stages, and demultiplexes streaming outputs. Specialized engines and stage replicas provide compute; a connector carries heavy payloads on the data plane; and session-oriented control supports long-lived duplex, world-model, and robot workloads.   This report covers the architecture (stage-level KV paths, replica pools, multi-hardware platforms, and efficiency stack) and OpenAI-compatible and OpenPI APIs for omni, TTS, image/video, world-model, robot, and duplex workloads. We evaluate on the multimodal nightly CI on H100 (TTS and MiniCPM-o on H200), focused on Qwen3-Omni.

## 20. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2008-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.
