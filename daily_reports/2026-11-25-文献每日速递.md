## 1. ViTacLab: Physically Consistent Visuo-Tactile Simulation, Benchmarking, and Representation Learning for Robotic Manipulation

- 作者：Linjing You, Zeming Yang, Zhenhao Shen, Shengqiang Xu, Guanda Li, Wenxuan Li, Jiyu Guo, Jasper Lu, Kyle Xu, Shawn Xie, Junjie Zhu, Siyu Wu, Jian Liu, Changhao Zhang, Rongchong Li, Zeyi Li, Hengshuang Zhao, Ruihai Wu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T12:06:34Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32476v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32476v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Tactile sensing is essential for contact-rich robotic manipulation, but existing visuo-tactile simulators trade physical fidelity for scalability, and benchmarks largely focus on two-finger grippers. We present ViTacLab, a framework comprising ViTacSim, ViTacBench, and ViTacPolicy. ViTacSim uses a tri-modal tactile primitive: a global sparse contact-force lattice, a local dense force array, and tactile images. Anchored to PhysX contact-solver outputs, it corrects normal inconsistency through local stiffness alignment and reconstructs tangential forces under decoupled slip-stick regimes while preserving GPU parallelism. A joint projection preserves the sparse wrench and enforces local friction cones. ViTacBench spans parallel grippers, dexterous hands, and bimanual systems, with tasks ranging from vision-dominant to tactile-dominant regimes. ViTacPolicy aggregates variable numbers of heterogeneous tactile inputs using spatial-pose embeddings and a Transformer, and aligns force-array and tactile-image modalities through a two-stream VQ-VAE with a shared codebook. Controlled Hertzian-reference indentation and real-sensor sliding tests reduce normal and tangential force RMSE from 2.31 and 1.85 N to 0.42 and 0.53 N, respectively. Throughput reaches 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS at 256 parallel environments. Simulation and real-world experiments show gains on several contact-rich tasks, while low absolute success on tactile-dominant tasks highlights the remaining difficulty of purely tactile control.

## 2. Hi3R-VLA: Grounding Semantics and Actions in 3D

- 作者：Shizhe Chen, Paul Pacaud, Cordelia Schmid

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T20:44:27Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31535v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31535v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



While recent vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly incorporate 3D geometry, how to effectively exploit it for precise and generalizable action prediction remains a central challenge. We introduce Hi3R-VLA, a 3D policy that grounds heterogeneous modalities - vision, language, and action - in explicit 3D geometry for robotic manipulation. Our model integrates spatially-aligned visual features and language conditioning into point cloud representations, enriching scene geometry with fine-grained, task-relevant semantics. It then anchors low-level control commands in the same 3D coordinate frame and uses 3D-RoPE enhanced action-to-point attention over coarse-to-fine point features to model geometric relationships between action targets and observed points. Our analysis demonstrates the importance of semantic fusion within 3D and spatial alignment between observations and actions. With fewer than 250M trainable parameters, Hi3R-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa, RLBench, RoboTwin2, DexJoCo and DexSuite across diverse manipulation tasks and embodiments, from single-arm and dual-arm grippers to dexterous hands. Real-robot evaluations further demonstrate its effectiveness in the physical world. The model, code and dataset are publicly released at https://cshizhe.github.io/projects/hi3r_vla.html.

## 3. DexLatentWorld: 3D Cross-Embodiment Latent Action World Models for Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation

- 作者：Vinay Udit Mohan, Xuchuan Huang, Rolandos Alexandros Potamias

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T16:05:37Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30907v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30907v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG



World models facilitate planning and control in robotic manipulation by enabling agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions. However, they typically rely on action-labelled interaction data that is costly to acquire and difficult to scale. These models are also often tied to the specific embodiment they were trained on, limiting transferability to previously unseen robot morphologies. We introduce a 3D latent-action world model that learns continuous action representations directly from point-cloud observations of retargeted human manipulation demonstrations. A contrastive objective is used to encourage consistent representations of motion across embodiments. The resulting unified latent action space serves directly as the search space for model-predictive control, where sampled latent actions condition particle-based future predictions. Executable robot commands are then recovered from the predicted geometric motion without requiring an embodiment-specific action decoder. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that the learned latent actions exhibit controllable and compositional motion, transfer to unseen robot embodiments, and support zero-shot manipulation with unseen gripper geometries. Following task-agnostic adaptation, our model achieves 90%, 92%, and 60% success on reaching, grasping, and placing, respectively, compared with DexWM's reported 72%, 58%, and 28%. Finally, we demonstrate zero-shot real-world manipulation with an unseen dexterous hand, achieving an 86% success rate across 50 trials on five diverse tasks involving pushing, grasping, and lifting everyday objects. These results highlight the potential of geometry-grounded latent actions as a unified, transferable interface for world-model-based robotic manipulation. Project materials, including demonstration videos and source code, are available at https://vinaymohan0409.github.io/DexLatentWorld/.

## 4. Self-Sensing Compliant Anthropomorphic Hand for Speed-Dictated Shared Force Control in Teleoperation

- 作者：Huijiang Wang, Yu Zhang, Rui Chen, Tristan Bonato, Jaemin Eom, Kai Junge, Cheng Pan, Benhui Dai, Yue Xie, Josie Hughes

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T16:43:27Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32937v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32937v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



In vision-based teleoperation of robotic hands, the operator receives no haptic feedback and therefore struggles to regulate the grasp force, while adding tactile sensors to the fingers increases wiring, cost and integration complexity. Compliant hands offer an alternative, as their mechanical compliance, usually treated only as a passive property for safe and adaptive grasping, can also serve as a source of information. Here, we show that the elastic transmission of a compliant, tendon-driven anthropomorphic hand can itself act as the sensor for shared-control telemanipulation. Series springs in the tendon routing are read by potentiometers in a forearm-mounted motor box, from which the hand estimates its joint states and finger forces without any external sensors. Building on this self-sensing, we develop a contact-aware shared-control framework for VR-based teleoperation. The robot hand is controlled through a mapping from the operator's hand kinematics until a contact is detected proprioceptively. Control then switches to a finger-level force controller whose set point is determined by the operator's grasping speed, and control authority can be allocated to individual fingers. Experiments validate the system's force estimation and increased grasp success rate compared with direct teleoperation for fragile and heavy objects. These results show that compliance can serve not only for passive adaptation but also as an intrinsic sensing resource for physical human-robot collaboration.

## 6. Ego2Real: Learning Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Visuo-Tactile Demonstrations

- 作者：Ange Bao, Haishan Zhang, Lingzi Xie, Shuang Li, Pei Zhao, Ling Shi, Bidan Huang

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32101v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Learning dexterous manipulation from egocentric human demonstrations offers a scalable path toward robot skill acquisition. Vision alone, however, provides limited information about physical contacts and the demonstrator's force intent, leading to ambiguities in interpreting hand-object interactions. Capturing human touch via tactile sensing is therefore crucial for reliable human-to-robot dexterous skill transfer. We present Ego2Real, a framework that leverages egocentric demonstrations and tactile gloves to transfer bimanual dexterous manipulation skills to robotic hands. Ego2Real incorporates tactile supervision at two complementary stages: (1) tactile-guided hand-object interaction reconstruction, which uses measured tactile signals to refine hand poses and contact geometry; and (2) contact-informed reinforcement learning, which translates human contact patterns into robotic contact rewards. By combining refined motion references with physical contact rewards during training, Ego2Real guides anthropomorphic robotic hands to emulate demonstrated contact behaviors while adapting to their own morphology and actuation. Experiments show that incorporating tactile supervision into both reconstruction and policy learning yields higher manipulation success rates, underscoring the importance of enriching egocentric demonstrations with tactile observations to improve their quality and transferability.

## 8. DiffAnchor: Anchor-Space Diffusion for Monocular 3D Hand Pose Estimation

- 作者：Changlong Jiang, Youci Tang, Haohong Kuang, Hang Xu, Jinghong Zheng, Ran Wang, Zhenghong Yu, Chen Feng, Xiaofeng Yue, Yang Xiao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T12:42:03Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32513v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32513v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Accurate and efficient monocular 3D hand pose estimation is an important perception capability for embodied human interaction and dexterous manipulation. Recent diffusion-based methods model pose uncertainty by iteratively denoising 3D joint coordinates. However, directly diffusing the final joints requires the generative process to simultaneously explore plausible poses and recover precise coordinates, often relying on additional denoising steps or multiple hypotheses and consequently increasing inference cost. In this work, we propose DiffAnchor, a few-step anchor-space diffusion framework that diffuses anchors rather than joints. Our key insight is that stochastic exploration does not need to operate directly on the final output. Instead, diffusion searches for informative intermediate anchors that serve as local 3D regressors, while anchor-to-joint offset prediction and weighted aggregation complete precise joint localization. Specifically, fixed context anchors provide stable global references, whereas noisy dynamic anchors are iteratively refined toward informative joint regions. An importance-guided anchor selection and replenishment mechanism further retains useful anchors, removes ineffective ones, and explores insufficiently covered regions. This design reduces the precision burden of the diffusion process and enables accurate prediction using only a few denoising steps and a single hypothesis. Extensive experiments on HO-3D V2, NYU, ICVL, and InterHand2.6M demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on the first three benchmarks and competitive performance on challenging interacting-hand estimation, while maintaining inference efficiency comparable to deterministic regression baselines.

## 9. OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments

- 作者：Xiang Fan, Yuheng Wang, Jiayuan Yan, Bohan Fang, Wisdom Oluchi Ikezogwo, Ainaz Eftekhar, Max Argus, Jason Ren, Jiafei Duan, Ranjay Krishna

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33158v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG



A single policy that controls diverse robots must not only transfer task knowledge across embodiments, but also adapt its behavior to each robot's capabilities. Existing approaches facilitate transfer through shared action representations, but these representations do not necessarily capture how differences in robot morphology should shape manipulation strategies. We introduce OmniAct, a cross-embodiment manipulation policy that conditions directly on each robot's kinematics in a specific scene to produce embodiment-specific behavior. This kinematic graph represents the robot's joint positions, motion axes, and connectivity to its visual observation, allowing the policy to account for how its embodiment related to objects and surfaces in the scene. A flow-matching policy predicts relative joint motions for each node, enabling control across robots with different degrees of freedom and kinematic configurations. Beyond task success, we observe that OmniAct learns distinct manipulation strategies that exploit the capabilities and accommodate the limitations of different morphologies. ## 10. Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies

- 作者：Xuanchen Liu, Hejun Zhang, Boxuan Dong, Ying Wu, Heshuai Li, Wen Gao, Yonghong Tian, Weihao Yu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T17:32:00Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33010v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33010v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.RO



Can a simple mirror expose the limitations of today's robotic manipulation policies? Inspired by the mirror self-recognition test in animal cognition, we investigate whether robot policies can reliably manipulate physical objects in scenes containing their reflections. We introduce RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on RoboDojo that preserves task objectives, success criteria, and policy checkpoints while comparing a mirror-free baseline with single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions. Across five policies spanning Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, and agentic policies, every mirror condition reduces aggregate success rates: averaged over the three conditions, the drop ranges from 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points across policies, and it reaches 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Because a mirror is also a physical obstacle, we compare it against geometrically matched non-reflective panels; 50--88\% of mirror-specific failures occur without recorded robot--panel contact, so direct collisions alone do not explain the degradation. Trajectory and contact analyses instead reveal recurring behaviors consistent with phantom targeting of reflected objects and spatial mislocalization of real ones. Targeted diagnostics further characterize grasping near reflections, policy-dependent pushing progress, and physical-target interaction under main-camera occlusion. Real-robot experiments with $π_{0.5}$ and FastWAM also show lower overall success rates under the upright-mirror and mirrored-tabletop conditions. These findings reveal a shared vulnerability to reflective environments across diverse robot policies and motivate reflection-aware evaluation of embodied intelligence. Code is available at https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror.

## 12. What Does a Skill Vocabulary Cost? A Closed-Loop Decomposition over Demonstrated Motions

- 作者：Maximilian Xiling Li, Nils Blank, Paul Mattes, Pankhuri Vanjani, Rudolf Lioutikov

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T15:58:53Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32858v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32858v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Discrete skill policies execute motions drawn from a finite vocabulary. In most current methods, this vocabulary is a latent codebook learned jointly with a selector and a decoder. When such a policy fails in closed loop, the failure cannot be traced to the vocabulary, to the choice of skill or to its execution. We present Movement Prototype Quantization (MPQ), which places a library of demonstrated motions after a trained policy instead of inside it. MPQ replaces each predicted action chunk with its nearest library element, corrects that element toward the prediction within a bound, and trains no parameters. Across six architectures, including a 7B vision-language-action model, the wrapped policy is statistically equivalent to the original. A reference policy executed through the library is therefore a baseline for any selector over the same library. Substituting a selector's components one at a time attributes its shortfall to them. On LIBERO-90, with selectors on three backbones, quantization costs nothing and the choice of skill costs at most 2 points under the reference's placement and correction. The selectors' own placement and correction account for nearly all of the remaining gap, up to 12 points. Offline reconstruction predicts none of these differences and misjudges library size, design and transfer across benchmark suites. Project page: https://intuitive-robots.github.io/mpq.

## 13. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. ## 15. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.

## 19. Arm Robot: AR-Enhanced Embodied Control and Visualization for Intuitive Robot Arm Manipulation

- 作者：Siyou Pei, Alexander Chen, Ronak Kaoshik, Ruofei Du, Yang Zhang

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2024-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- 分类：CoRR 2024



Embodied interaction has been introduced to human-robot interaction (HRI) as a type of teleoperation, in which users control robot arms with bodily action via handheld controllers or haptic gloves. Embodied teleoperation has made robot control intuitive to non-technical users, but differences between humans' and robots' capabilities \eg ranges of motion and response time, remain challenging. In response, we present Arm Robot, an embodied robot arm teleoperation system that helps users tackle human-robot discrepancies. Specifically, Arm Robot (1) includes AR visualization as real-time feedback on temporal and spatial discrepancies, and (2) allows users to change observing perspectives and expand action space. We conducted a user study (N=18) to investigate the usability of the Arm Robot and learn how users perceive the embodiment. Our results show users could use Arm Robot's features to effectively control the robot arm, providing insights for continued work in embodied HRI.

## 20. Automated Policy Analysis

- 作者：Konstantine Arkoudas, Shoshana Loeb, Ritu Chadha, C. Jason Chiang, Keith Whittaker

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2012-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=84XY6nHYsD

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=84XY6nHYsD

- 分类：POLICY 2012



Static analysis of access-control policies is becoming increasingly important. Such analysis can reveal errors and vulnerabilities in the policies, as well as logical inconsistencies, unintended effects, and discrepancies between different policies or different versions of the same policy. In the process, it helps policy developers to better understand the effects of their policies. Policy analysis has typically been done by hand. For instance, when a bug is discovered and corrected, the resulting policy is manually inspected to ensure that the fix works and that it does not introduce any new problems. But when the policies are large or their logical structure non-trivial, performing such analysis manually is tedious and error-prone. In this paper we show how to automate a wide array of useful policy analyses. This is accomplished by representing policies as logical formulas in the SMT (satisfiability-modulo-theory) subset of first-order logic, and couching analysis questions as SMT problems, which are then solved by efficient off-the-shelf SMT solvers. Because SMT solvers can reason about arithmetic and inductive data types, in addition to Boolean constraints, our system can handle many policies that cannot be analyzed by existing policy engines. We describe the formulation of a number of useful analyses (consistency, completeness, and observational equivalence), and report experimental results on the efficiency of our implementation for analyzing policies of various sizes and kinds of logical structure.
