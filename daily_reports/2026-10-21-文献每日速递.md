## 1. Plausible Future, Wrong Choice: Steering World-Action Models toward Intended Future

- 作者：Zhuohao Li, Jiawei He, Yuxuan Yan, Lang Zhou, Yinghao Li, Yanrui Li, Tianyu Zhang, Yi-Yang Song, Xirui Wu andYi-Lin Wei, Fating Hong, Qing Zhang, Zhilin Zhao, Jingdong Wang, Wei-Shi Zheng

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T15:58:45Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28431v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28431v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for embodied generalist policies by jointly modeling future world evolution and robot actions across diverse manipulation tasks. However, representing diverse futures is only the first step: a capable WAM must also generate futures that match the requirements of the current task and execution progress. In long-horizon manipulation where similar observation-action segments occur across different tasks and stages, existing approaches may generate locally plausible yet misdirected rollouts to drift toward wrong task stage, i.e., a phenomenon we term Stage Confusion. To address this problem, we propose TaskWAM, a closed-loop, stage-aware framework that represents fine-grained task requirements with Mode Tokens and tracks execution progress with State Tokens, forming a structured task state to steer world--action generation toward intended future. We further introduce StageBench, a controlled multi-stage manipulation benchmark with parameterized stage variations and automated evaluation.Extensive experiments on both simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks demonstrate that TaskWAM effectively reduces confusion and achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art methods. Moreover, TaskWAM provides strong plug-and-play transfer across heterogeneous WAM architectures and robot embodiments, including single-arm robots, dual-arm grippers, dexterous hands, and humanoid robots.

## 2. Frontier LLMs Enter the World of Robotics: A Cross-Model Evaluation on RoboColiseum

- 作者：Da Huang, Jichao Wang, Junhui Wu, Tong Ran, Linting Guo, Siying Zhang, Chuang Men, Sukai Wang, Yue Zhou, Maoqing Yao, Mo Wu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T08:46:24Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27865v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27865v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Recent studies have explored the potential of multimodal large language models for robot control, yet the models' embodied capabilities remain insufficiently characterized across models, tasks, and execution settings. We conduct a multidimensional comparative evaluation on RoboColiseum across four tracks: Instruction Following, Spatial Reasoning, General Manipulation, and Robustness. We implement Agent2Act, a control harness that integrates GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, and Gemini 4 Argon into the evaluation environment through shared observation, action execution, and feedback interfaces. Under common task settings and scoring criteria, all four models exhibit a consistent capability profile: high instruction-following scores, with three approaching perfect performance; strong spatial reasoning; and general manipulation scores below those of the other three tracks. Within the evaluated configurations, Astra leads instruction following and robustness, while Opus leads spatial reasoning, general manipulation, and overall performance. These results reveal complementary strengths across models and a persistent gap between task understanding and reliable physical execution. Component comparisons on selected tasks further show that harness design affects task success, planning cost, and execution time. Additionally, case studies with Astra examine whole-body coordination, manipulation across robot embodiments, precision manipulation, dexterous-hand manipulation, and real-robot tasks, providing further insight into the model's capabilities and limitations across embodied tasks.

## 3. RoboJudge: Multimodal Language Models as Judges for Embodied Video Generation

- 作者：Siyuan Ma, Yu Qi, Cheng Ye, Ruotian Peng, Xinyi Xu, Zitiantao Lin, Zedong Cai, Yijian Huang, Weiyang Liu, Siyuan Wang, Yandong Wen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T07:48:41Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27782v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27782v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV, cs.RO



Video generation models are promising foundations for embodied world models, but only if their generated rollouts are physically valid and faithful to task instructions. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are increasingly used to judge these properties, yet their agreement with human judgments on embodied videos has not been systematically evaluated. To address this gap, we introduce RoboJudge-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating MLLMs as judges of embodied video generation. The benchmark measures two fundamental dimensions, Physical Adherence and Instruction Alignment, across 800 human-annotated videos from 8 source corpora and 10 video generators, spanning grippers, human hands, and dexterous hands. Experiments reveal uneven alignment between existing judges and human ratings: general-purpose MLLMs show lower agreement on physical adherence, while specialized video judges designed for general-domain physical reasoning do not consistently transfer to embodied scenarios. Motivated by these findings, we construct RoboJudge-Train, comprising 12K videos annotated with 25K human-verified scores and model-assisted rationales for Physical Adherence and Instruction Alignment. We fine-tune a 9B MLLM on this dataset with supervised learning followed by reinforcement learning, yielding RoboJudge-9B. Among the model judges evaluated on RoboJudge-Bench, RoboJudge-9B achieves the highest overall agreement, reaching a Pearson correlation of 0.719 with a statistically significant 0.089 gain over Gemini-3.7-Flash, the best-performing proprietary baseline in our evaluation. Training on RoboJudge-Train also yields gains on external benchmarks, including VideoPhy2 and Physion-Eval. We further use RoboJudge-9B as a downstream evaluator to benchmark video generation models according to Physical Adherence and Instruction Alignment.

## 4. Touch-JEPA: Tactile Representation Learning from Hand-Object Video with Limited Tactile Supervision

- 作者：Minseong Kweon, Qiao Feng, Yiming Huang, Lingjie Liu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T16:56:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28519v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28519v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Understanding contact and tactile magnitude in hand-object interaction is a key step toward physically intelligent systems, yet learning such representations is limited by the cost and restricted coverage of tactile measurements. We present Touch-JEPA, a framework for learning tactile representations from hand-object videos with limited tactile supervision. We first introduce a self-supervised pretraining objective that learns from video alone without tactile supervision. Using video-derived hand geometry, Touch-JEPA predicts masked visual features at corresponding hand-surface locations from spatial and temporal context, encouraging the representation to capture latent contact dynamics. We then extend this formulation to joint representation learning, where masked prediction is applied to all videos while tactile supervision is available only for a labeled subset, grounding the shared representation in measured contact and magnitude. With self-supervised pretraining alone, simple supervised readouts on frozen Touch-JEPA features outperform supervised baselines in contact discrimination and tactile magnitude estimation across three human-hand benchmarks, and transfer to robotic hands without robot tactile training data. Joint learning with limited tactile supervision further improves contact and magnitude estimation on the human-hand benchmarks. Together, these results show that hand-object video can provide scalable self-supervision for tactile representation learning, while limited tactile measurements provide complementary physical grounding.

## 5. What Novice Operators See Changes What the Policy Learns: A User Study of Keypoint Hand Visual Representation for Teleoperated Demonstration

- 作者：Amane Yamaguchi, Shohei Kamiguchi, Masashi Hamaya, Shigeo Yoshida, Takuji Narumi, Mai Nishimura

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T09:22:21Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27907v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27907v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.HC



Imitation learning increasingly relies on novice operators, whose demonstrations fall short of experts' in number and inter-trial consistency. Operators act on what they are shown, yet how the rendering of the robot affects their demonstrations, and the policies trained on them, has not been examined. We study this question with PointHand, a teleoperation interface that renders a multi-fingered robot hand as sparse keypoints at its fingertips and joints, the same keypoints a policy can observe. In a simulated mug-in-box task, 18 novices and 4 experts collected 2,200 trials under three renderings: the real shape of the hand, all keypoints emphasized, or the thumb and index finger alone emphasized. The novices shown the thumb and index finger emphasized collected 441 successful demonstrations from 600 trials, against 320 for those shown the real shape. We then trained diffusion policies on these demonstrations, replacing the scene point cloud and proprioceptive state they observe with the hand keypoints and two object keypoints. Within 400 steps these demonstrations went from the weakest of the three novice conditions under the point-cloud policy (46.3%) to the strongest under the keypoint policy (57.3%), a gain of +11.0 percentage points in all five seeds. Those collected with all keypoints emphasized changed by at most +0.7 percentage points, and in no more than three seeds. The value of a demonstration therefore depends on the rendering shown during collection and on what the policy observes together. A shared representation can pay off on both sides, in the number of successful demonstrations and in the success rate of the policy trained on them. ## 6. DexOAK: Object-Aware Kinematic Retargeting

- 作者：Federica Spinola, Robert Katzschmann, Cordelia Schmid

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-19T17:20:10Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26762v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26762v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



We introduce DexOAK, a learning-based object-aware kinematic retargeting framework for generating contact-consistent robot hand references from human motion-capture data. DexOAK first minimizes an interaction-graph Laplacian objective over a joint hand-object graph, so the robot reproduces the demonstrated object-relative interaction geometry rather than the human hand pose alone. These per-frame solutions form seed retargeted references, from which a feed-forward network conditioned on human keypoints and object point clouds learns the retargeting function. Object-aware finetuning then refines the network under contact, non-penetration, and self-collision losses that position the fingers on the demonstrated contacts and reduce artifacts destabilizing physics-based refinement. Once trained, the network transfers to unseen data by inference alone, without per-frame optimization. DexOAK references improve dynamic retargeting over the state-of-the-art across bimanual tasks with rigid and articulated objects. On the Inspire hand, success rises from 39.5% to 48.4% on OakInk-v2, and ADD-AUC rises from 62.7% to 77.9% on ARCTIC. Ablations trace the gains to object-aware kinematic retargeting and the amortized network refined with contact losses. Trained on OakInk-v2, it transfers to ARCTIC zero-shot and nears in-domain performance after fine-tuning. On hardware, 46/56 open-loop replays succeed across five tasks, enabling dexterous robot data generation from human data. Visualizations and interactive results are at: https://fspinola.github.io/dexoak/.

Tactile representation learning for dexterous manipulation often requires substantial robot-side data collection, limiting its scalability across different hands and sensors. ## 8. EmbodyHOI: Embodiment-Aware Generation of Hand-Object Interactions for Humanoid Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Chaitanya Chawla, Kartik Sharma, Ananya Bal, Laszlo A. Jeni, Guanya Shi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-19T20:08:32Z

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27242v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Humanoid dexterous manipulation needs reference motions that are both semantically rich and kinematically feasible for the target robot. Text-conditioned hand-object interaction (HOI) generators supply diverse, language-specified demonstrations, but they synthesize motion in human (MANO) space. The embodiment gap is therefore deferred to post-hoc retargeting, where trajectories deployed on the robot can fail to maintain contact with objects and also degrade policy learning. Our key idea is that robot reachability should constrain the interaction trajectories while they are being generated. We present EmbodyHOI, which runs a differentiable inverse-kinematics (IK) solver for a specific humanoid embodiment inside the sampling loop of a frozen flow-matching HOI trajectory generator. We feed back gradients from the embodiment-aware IK solver into the HOI sampler and jointly optimize the position of the humanoid with respect to the object. Critically, cheaper alternatives for bridging the embodiment gap fail to beat EmbodyHOI. Post-hoc wrist refinement and compute-matched best-of-N selection both reach lower IK energy than our guidance yet train worse policies. For simulation results, see our website: https://embodyhoi.github.io/

## 10. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

## 11. In-Context Guidance: Learning Inter-Task Synergies via Numerical Foundational Models for Few-Shot Multitask Optimization

- 作者：Tingyang Wei, Haofeng Wu, Jiao Liu, Zhao Wei, Puay Siew Tan, Yew-Soon Ong

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-09-22T08:01:54Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25836v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25836v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.NE



Multi-task optimization (MTO) addresses a set of optimization tasks simultaneously, often suffering from inaccurate inter-task relationship estimation under limited evaluation budgets, leading to negative transfer. This paper introduces In-Context Guidance Multitask Optimization (ICG-MTO), a novel framework that leverages numerical foundational models to improve inter-task coupling estimation in few-shot scenarios. Unlike conventional methods that rely solely on scarce observed data, ICG-MTO employs a frozen foundational model to infer auxiliary guidance through in-context learning. The framework operates through three stages: constructing an algorithm-specific in-context query from evaluated solutions, using the foundational model to infer a guidance signal characterizing predictive relationships among tasks, and translating this signal into algorithm-specific guidance for maximum-a-posteriori coupling estimation. This approach provides regularization during the early, data-scarce stages of optimization and gradually relinquishes control as task-specific observations accumulate. We instantiate the framework in multitask Bayesian optimization as ICG-MTBO, using directional fitness-class queries to guide inter-task coupling estimation, and further instantiate it in MFEA-II using decision-space-overlap queries to guide random mating probability estimation. Experiments across synthetic benchmarks and a real-world robot arm control problem, together with evaluations under different acquisition functions and evolutionary multitasking, demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of ICG-MTO for few-shot multitask optimization.

## 13. AWLS: Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering for One-Step Generative Robotic Manipulation Policies

- 作者：Jiahong Tang, Jiaju He, Zhiyuan Yang, Ruyang Han, Haiyue Zhu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T06:58:12Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27736v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27736v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Human-collected real-world robotic manipulation demonstrations often contain mixed-quality trajectories, including hesitant, indirect, or inefficient action segments. While diffusion- and flow-based policies can model such multimodal data, standard behavior cloning (BC) assigns equal likelihood to all demonstrated actions and may therefore reproduce suboptimal modes. Offline RL value estimates offer a natural signal for biasing policies toward higher-value behavioral modes, but directly fine-tuning the generative policy or using critic gradients for policy steering can amplify critic errors and induce prior drift. We propose Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering (AWLS), an offline post-training method for improving frozen one-step generative manipulation policies without updating their decoders. AWLS learns a residual noise shaper, an observation-conditioned residual correction to the policy's latent noise, and trains it with an advantage-weighted action reconstruction objective. The advantages are detached, scale-normalized, and used only as supervised weights on demonstrated actions, avoiding critic-gradient policy updates while preserving fast single-step inference. We further introduce a rollout-free decoder-sensitivity criterion for selecting pretrained checkpoints that remain responsive to latent-noise perturbations. Experiments on simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks with clean and mixed-quality demonstrations show that AWLS improves over the frozen base policy while limiting drift from the pretrained behavior prior. ## 14. ALIGN: Aligning Condition Estimation and Expression in Advantage-Conditioned Behavior Cloning

- 作者：Zechen Wang, Zhenqi Qiu, Houlong Xiong, Suohang Zhang, Yiyu Ren, Wanting Xu, Hongfei Niu, Chengyang He, Ge Sun, Ran Cheng, Qian Zhu

- 来源：arXiv

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Human-collected robot manipulation data often mix successful behavior with retries, pauses, recoveries, and failures. Advantage-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (AC-BC) can exploit such mixed-quality data, but only if the conditioning signal is both accurately constructed from offline trajectories and effectively incorporated into the policy's action-vector-field prediction. Existing pipelines can weaken this signal by thresholding estimator-derived advantage scores into noisy conditions, and by routing conditions through prompts that weakly affect the action vector field. We introduce ALIGN, a condition-aligned AC-BC framework for vision-language-action policies. ALIGN directly predicts categorical pairwise conditions in the policy-facing label space and injects them into a DiT action expert through AdaRMS modulation fused with flow time. These results indicate that aligning conditions from estimation to action prediction is an effective way to extract robust manipulation policies from data in which success, recovery and error coexist.

## 15. Perceives but Doesn't Brake: A Perception-Action Binding Gap in Reasoning Driving VLAs

- 作者：Matteo Cercola, Katie Z Luo, Simone Formentin, Marco Pavone

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T13:21:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28184v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28184v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Reasoning-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) driving models can perceive and reason about safety-critical hazards yet fail to translate that evidence into safe actions. We characterize this failure as a perception-action binding gap: hazard information is available inside the model but exerts insufficient control over trajectory generation. Using matched simulated counterfactuals and logged driving replay, we show that in-path hazards are actually encoded in the visual representation, but longitudinal plans are dominated by ego-speed history and respond only weakly to hazard proximity or the model's own reasoning trace. Further analysis shows that the trajectory generator is capable of braking but sometimes fails to do so due to the perception-action binding gap. Without retraining or modifying any weights, we inject a hazard-conditioned energy into the frozen flow-matching sampler of Alpamayo-1.5, restoring the missing coupling at generation time. With exact obstacle geometry, guidance cuts footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5% and recovers safe braking whenever sufficient stopping distance remains, eliminating collisions entirely in our braking-relevant closed-loop battery. It also reverses the diagnosed dependence of the plan, from tracking ego speed to responding to obstacle range. These results separate hazard perception from hazard control in driving VLAs, show that unsafe behavior can arise even when the model has both the evidence and the ability to act on it, and propose a simple fix for bridging this binding gap.

## 16. Reasoning as a Distribution: Generative Plan Latents for Vision-Language-Action Models

- 作者：Matteo Cercola, Katie Z Luo, Milan Ganai, John Alora, Simone Formentin, Marco Pavone

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T13:08:37Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28166v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28166v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies increasingly reason before acting, but existing reasoning models compress this intermediate decision into a single representation. When several plans are valid, this commitment hides alternatives that could otherwise be selected or revised. We introduce the Generative Plan Latent (GPL), which represents the reasoning state as a low-dimensional conditional distribution over plans, such as which object, grasp, or route to use. This makes the reasoning state addressable: without retraining the VLA, plans can be sampled (coverage), selected on request (steering), and reweighted by deployment outcomes (outcome adaptation). Steering the plan raises the fraction of executions that follow the request from 0.48 to 0.94, while the same guidance on the native action distribution shows no reliable gain. Finally, we show that an explicit plan is most useful when it carries decision information not already determined by the observation.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have achieved strong performance on standard robotic manipulation benchmarks. However, their reliability can degrade substantially when task conditions deviate from the nominal setting. Perturbations in object positions and task instructions may lead to complete task failure. Existing VLA policies generally lack an explicit runtime mechanism for distinguishing these failure modes and selecting an appropriate recovery strategy. To systematically mitigate this issue, we propose FIT-MAC, a failure-aware, closed-loop multi-agent control framework for improving the robustness of VLA policies at inference time. FIT-MAC augments a VLA policy with an inference-time control layer. First, the Planner Agent parses the language instruction into a structured task specification that captures the manipulation goal and spatial constraints, and the Grounder Agent resolves each instruction-level reference to a visual target. Next, the Verifier Agent tracks phase-specific execution progress, distinguishes target-binding errors from local execution failures, and determines whether intervention is warranted. The Correction Agent then routes permitted interventions to target rebinding or bounded grasping or placement recovery, depending on the failure type. When no intervention is required, the pretrained VLA remains the default action generator. Extensive experiments on LIBERO-PRO demonstrate that FIT-MAC improves robustness across multiple VLA backbones, consistently increasing success rates and achieving state-of-the-art performance under diverse perturbations.

## 18. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

- 作者：Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T14:25:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20255v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

Existing real-world reinforcement learning methods improve robot policies by combining human corrections with autonomous rollouts. ## 20. Reinforcement Learning from Real-Robot Interaction without High-Quality State Estimates

- 作者：Ryan Gao, Ashvin Anilkumar, Nicholas Corrado, William Cong, Josiah Hanna

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-02T21:59:06Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14042v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14042v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A longstanding goal for robot reinforcement learning (RL) is that robots learn from their own physical experience, as humans and animals do. A key obstacle to learning directly from physical experience, especially outside instrumented lab environments, is that the state of the world is usually unobservable to the learner and must be estimated from on-board sensing. Errors in that estimate corrupt both the policy input and potentially the reward signal. We study this challenge using the contact-rich task of a bipedal robot learning to score soccer goals. We estimate all key state variables on-board with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) and no external instrumentation (e.g., motion capture or overhead cameras). Because the UKF may incorrectly estimate state when the ball is unobserved, we learn a new state representation with a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) that, critically, only corrects the RSSM's latent state when the ball is observed. Building on RLPD, we introduce RSSM-RLPD and apply it to the task of a NAO robot learning to dribble a soccer ball into a goal. We show that the robot learns to score from multiple starting positions after 30 minutes of real-world interaction, reaching 100% success against 0% for a feed-forward policy under the same training budget. We complement our physical robot experiments with a simulation study and show that RSSM-RLPD learns policies that achieve a higher success rate than behavior cloning, a feed-forward, and GRU baseline. Taken together, our results show that the addition of a learned state representation to existing on-robot learning methods enables RL from real-robot data even with a high degree of partial observability.
