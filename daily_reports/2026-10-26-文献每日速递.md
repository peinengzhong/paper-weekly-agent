## 1. DexPort: A Hand-Held Skill Interface for Dexterous Hand Data Collection

- 作者：Amber Xie, Jiawei Fu, Xiaofeng Guo, Carmelo Sferrazza, Guanya Shi, Haozhi Qi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-23T17:56:28Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32969v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32969v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG



Collecting high-quality demonstrations remains a central bottleneck for learning dexterous manipulation. Teleoperation records executable robot actions, but it requires a complete robot setup and places the burden of coordinating many finger joints on the operator. Human demonstrations are easier to collect, but their motions must be estimated and retargeted to the robot, and they do not directly provide robot actions. In this paper, we introduce DexPort, a portable data-collection interface for high-DoF robot hands. It uses the same hand hardware for collection and deployment, avoiding a hand embodiment gap, and records both commanded joint targets and measured joint states. Our key observation is that a small set of skills can support a wide range of dexterous tasks. During collection, the operator moves the hand-held interface to specify the robot wrist motion and selects or adjusts skills that coordinate the finger motion. This removes the need to control each joint independently while retaining behaviors such as precise grasping and in-hand manipulation. We collect demonstrations across several real-world tasks and in unstructured environments without a robot arm. We then use behavior cloning to train visuomotor policies directly on the recorded wrist motions and hand commands, without skill labels. We evaluate across 4 tasks: pick-and-place with diverse objects, screwing with in-hand tool use, drawer stowage which requires simultaneous grasp and pulling, and a bimanual scanning task. We collect over 2,500 demonstrations with our portable device, in both laboratory and in-the-wild settings. Across our tasks, we see an improvement from 11% to 64% when using recorded robot commands instead of states, and an improvement from 36% to 78% for task success when using in-the-wild data.

## 2. PiHand: Latent Hand-Object Interaction Priors for Physics-Based Policy Learning

- 作者：Niklas Berndt, Xianghui Xie, Enes Duran, Yixiao Wang, Gerard Pons-Moll

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-22T13:59:19Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31299v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31299v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Synthesizing physically grounded dexterous hand-object interactions is important for VR/AR, gaming, and robotics, yet remains challenging because subtle contact dynamics, diverse object geometries, and target trajectories must be jointly respected while maintaining natural grasps. Existing methods either track mocap trajectories with limited transfer to novel objects, or rely on hand-only generative priors that fail to capture coupled hand-object motion, sacrificing contact fidelity and sample efficiency. To tackle these challenges, we introduce PiHand, a Physically grounded Interaction-aware Hand motion synthesis framework. PiHand first trains expert policies that track mocap demonstrations in simulation, distills them into a unified hand-object interaction prior using a goal-conditioned VAE, and finally solve diverse dexterous manipulation tasks through bounded residual exploration in its latent space. With only a single reward and no complex curriculum training, this yields general tabletop manipulation policies that generalize to unseen objects and trajectories on GRAB and DexYCB. The same prior transfers to object catching and bimanual handover, two tasks that are rarely seen in its training data. Across all tasks, PiHand consistently outperforms baselines in all metrics. We will release the processed data and model checkpoints.

## 3. PHD-Hand: A Printable Humanoid Dexterous Hand with Linkage-Driven 3-DOA Digits

- 作者：Neel Adwani, Muhaiminul Islam Akash, Saffia Ouali, Ethan Liu, Cong Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-21T01:01:57Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29030v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29030v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Currently, dexterous humanoid robot hands are widely considered the final hardware bottleneck that hampers generalist robots from physically assisting people at home and work. As conventional tendon-driven designs suffer from high cost, demanding maintenance, poor reliability, and short durability, alternative linkage-driven designs have increasingly gained attention. Despite their potential to avoid the weaknesses of tendon-driven hands, integrating multiple degrees of actuation (DOA) in a single digit with linkage drivetrains is a challenge for design and fabrication. Only a few studies have managed to present functional humanoid hands that are completely linkage-driven and have at least three DOAs per digit without compromising actuator placement and other design preferences. Our work introduces a new linkage-driven hand featuring a unique flexure hinge. Together with elastic drive rods made of spring steel, the linkage drivetrains are significantly simplified. Unlike other linkage-driven designs of similar dexterity, our design does not require many miniature moving parts. As a result, the hand can be fabricated easily with a budget 3D printer. This not only significantly lowers the barrier to entry for using and developing dexterous humanoid robot hand functions, but also allows the hands to be sized much closer to regular human hands. The design offers desirable kinematic advantages as well. A prototype is evaluated for grasping and object manipulation. In addition, this paper presents our latest findings on kinematics and control of the hand.

## 4. Touch-JEPA: Tactile Representation Learning from Hand-Object Video with Limited Tactile Supervision

- 作者：Minseong Kweon, Qiao Feng, Yiming Huang, Lingjie Liu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T16:56:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28519v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28519v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Understanding contact and tactile magnitude in hand-object interaction is a key step toward physically intelligent systems, yet learning such representations is limited by the cost and restricted coverage of tactile measurements. We present Touch-JEPA, a framework for learning tactile representations from hand-object videos with limited tactile supervision. We first introduce a self-supervised pretraining objective that learns from video alone without tactile supervision. Using video-derived hand geometry, Touch-JEPA predicts masked visual features at corresponding hand-surface locations from spatial and temporal context, encouraging the representation to capture latent contact dynamics. We then extend this formulation to joint representation learning, where masked prediction is applied to all videos while tactile supervision is available only for a labeled subset, grounding the shared representation in measured contact and magnitude. With self-supervised pretraining alone, simple supervised readouts on frozen Touch-JEPA features outperform supervised baselines in contact discrimination and tactile magnitude estimation across three human-hand benchmarks, and transfer to robotic hands without robot tactile training data. Joint learning with limited tactile supervision further improves contact and magnitude estimation on the human-hand benchmarks. Together, these results show that hand-object video can provide scalable self-supervision for tactile representation learning, while limited tactile measurements provide complementary physical grounding.

## 5. EgoForesight: Effectively Scaling Policy Learning with Noisy Egocentric Actions

- 作者：Chubin Zhang, Changyuan Wang, Yirui Wang, Ao Li, Shilin Ma, Yue Su, Cai Zhou, Xiuwei Xu, Tianle Zhang, Yansong Tang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-22T19:28:43Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31845v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31845v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Scaling policy learning requires broad experience and reliable supervision. Robot demonstrations provide high-quality trajectory supervision but are costly to collect. Egocentric videos broaden task and environment coverage, yet motion reconstruction and retargeting introduce noisy action labels. To address this challenge, we introduce EgoForesight, a vision-language-action framework that learns jointly from both sources by reducing the influence of unreliable action labels and exploiting demonstrated outcomes as complementary supervision. A joint vision-action flow couples future visual representation prediction with action generation, unifying reliability-aware action learning with outcome-informed representation transfer. Specifically, reliability-conditioned corruption allocates action supervision across noise levels according to source reliability, while reliability-weighted regression regulates each source's contribution. Complementing this treatment of noisy labels, privileged-future representation distillation draws supervision from demonstrated outcomes. An exponential-moving-average teacher observes the current scene and clean future to encode action-relevant structure from the resulting scene change. Its deeper representations supervise an earlier student layer receiving a corrupted future, providing outcome-informed structure beyond noisy action annotations. Together, these mechanisms support effective scaling with noisy egocentric actions. Over the tested range, EgoForesight exhibits steeper scaling slopes than the baseline, reducing real-world open-loop action MSE by 20.5% and improving RoboCasa few-shot success by 8.1 percentage points at 30,000 hours of pretraining data. After post-training, it also achieves strong absolute performance in both simulated and real-world dexterous manipulation.

## 6. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

## 7. RoboCode: Learning Reusable Action Primitives for Robot Manipulation

- 作者：Xianyue Peng, Devi Amarsaikhan, Iman Soltani

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-23T06:32:09Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32225v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32225v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Recent robot manipulation policies commonly predict continuous action chunks directly from observations, without explicitly modeling reusable structure in the action space. However, manipulation demonstrations often contain recurring short-horizon motion patterns that are shared across tasks. We introduce \textsc{RoboCode}, which learns these patterns through a shared soft-gated primitive action interface. Rather than assigning each action chunk to a single discrete code, \textsc{RoboCode} represents it as a soft composition of multiple learned primitives, providing a flexible intermediate representation of short-horizon actions. The primitive interface is learned from action chunks pooled across multiple tasks and then frozen while task-specific state encoders are trained to map current state inputs into the shared latent space.   Experiments on five ManiSkill manipulation tasks address three questions. First, the learned primitive interface reconstructs action chunks with an average reconstruction MSE of 0.0187. Second, analysis of code activations reveals structured code reuse across tasks. Third, \textsc{RoboCode} achieves the highest or tied closed-loop success rate on all evaluated tasks, with an average success rate of 75.6%, compared with 68.8% for ACT and 41.6% for the larger Diffusion Policy baseline. These results suggest that soft-gated primitive codebooks provide an effective and interpretable intermediate action representation for robot manipulation.

## 10. If at First You Don't Succeed, Backtrack: Lightweight Recovery for Frozen VLAs

- 作者：Thomas Landeg, Thierry Tambe

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-23T17:35:33Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32941v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32941v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models perform well on robotic manipulation, yet remain brittle once their own actions carry them into states their training data barely covers. Recovering from such failures usually requires recovery demonstrations, policy retraining, or a separate model at inference time. We introduce Backtrack, a lightweight failure detection and recovery mechanism for frozen VLAs that needs none of these. A small failure prediction head, trained only on outcome-labeled rollouts of the frozen policy, reads the VLA's existing internal representations to estimate whether the current episode will fail. When the predicted failure probability exceeds a threshold, a controller pauses the policy, retraces the arm's recently achieved motion, and hands control back to the unchanged policy so it can retry from an earlier configuration. The head adds only 0.48-0.88% to the policy's parameter count. We evaluate Backtrack on LIBERO with three VLAs spanning two action-generation families: SmolVLA and $π$0.5, which use flow-matching action experts, and the autoregressive $π$0-FAST. On LIBERO-Long, the suite of long-horizon multi-step tasks and the one on which all three policies fail most often, Backtrack reduces each policy's failure rate by 19-48%, raising success by up to 7.4 percentage points; on the three shorter suites, where control success is already 85-99%, its effect is smaller. On MetaWorld, all twelve controller configurations tested with SmolVLA improve on the unassisted policy.

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies can adapt to local human intervention, while federated learning can combine distributed robot experience without pooling raw records. ## 12. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

- 作者：Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T14:25:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20255v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

## 14. Reinforcement Learning from Real-Robot Interaction without High-Quality State Estimates

- 作者：Ryan Gao, Ashvin Anilkumar, Nicholas Corrado, William Cong, Josiah Hanna

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-02T21:59:06Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14042v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14042v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A longstanding goal for robot reinforcement learning (RL) is that robots learn from their own physical experience, as humans and animals do. A key obstacle to learning directly from physical experience, especially outside instrumented lab environments, is that the state of the world is usually unobservable to the learner and must be estimated from on-board sensing. Errors in that estimate corrupt both the policy input and potentially the reward signal. We study this challenge using the contact-rich task of a bipedal robot learning to score soccer goals. We estimate all key state variables on-board with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) and no external instrumentation (e.g., motion capture or overhead cameras). Because the UKF may incorrectly estimate state when the ball is unobserved, we learn a new state representation with a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) that, critically, only corrects the RSSM's latent state when the ball is observed. Building on RLPD, we introduce RSSM-RLPD and apply it to the task of a NAO robot learning to dribble a soccer ball into a goal. We show that the robot learns to score from multiple starting positions after 30 minutes of real-world interaction, reaching 100% success against 0% for a feed-forward policy under the same training budget. We complement our physical robot experiments with a simulation study and show that RSSM-RLPD learns policies that achieve a higher success rate than behavior cloning, a feed-forward, and GRU baseline. Taken together, our results show that the addition of a learned state representation to existing on-robot learning methods enables RL from real-robot data even with a high degree of partial observability.

## 19. Arm Robot: AR-Enhanced Embodied Control and Visualization for Intuitive Robot Arm Manipulation

- 作者：Siyou Pei, Alexander Chen, Ronak Kaoshik, Ruofei Du, Yang Zhang

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2024-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- 分类：CoRR 2024



Embodied interaction has been introduced to human-robot interaction (HRI) as a type of teleoperation, in which users control robot arms with bodily action via handheld controllers or haptic gloves. Embodied teleoperation has made robot control intuitive to non-technical users, but differences between humans' and robots' capabilities \eg ranges of motion and response time, remain challenging. In response, we present Arm Robot, an embodied robot arm teleoperation system that helps users tackle human-robot discrepancies. Specifically, Arm Robot (1) includes AR visualization as real-time feedback on temporal and spatial discrepancies, and (2) allows users to change observing perspectives and expand action space. We conducted a user study (N=18) to investigate the usability of the Arm Robot and learn how users perceive the embodiment. Our results show users could use Arm Robot's features to effectively control the robot arm, providing insights for continued work in embodied HRI.

## 20. Automated Policy Analysis

- 作者：Konstantine Arkoudas, Shoshana Loeb, Ritu Chadha, C. Jason Chiang, Keith Whittaker

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2012-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=84XY6nHYsD

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=84XY6nHYsD

- 分类：POLICY 2012



Static analysis of access-control policies is becoming increasingly important. Such analysis can reveal errors and vulnerabilities in the policies, as well as logical inconsistencies, unintended effects, and discrepancies between different policies or different versions of the same policy. In the process, it helps policy developers to better understand the effects of their policies. Policy analysis has typically been done by hand. For instance, when a bug is discovered and corrected, the resulting policy is manually inspected to ensure that the fix works and that it does not introduce any new problems. But when the policies are large or their logical structure non-trivial, performing such analysis manually is tedious and error-prone. In this paper we show how to automate a wide array of useful policy analyses. This is accomplished by representing policies as logical formulas in the SMT (satisfiability-modulo-theory) subset of first-order logic, and couching analysis questions as SMT problems, which are then solved by efficient off-the-shelf SMT solvers. Because SMT solvers can reason about arithmetic and inductive data types, in addition to Boolean constraints, our system can handle many policies that cannot be analyzed by existing policy engines. We describe the formulation of a number of useful analyses (consistency, completeness, and observational equivalence), and report experimental results on the efficiency of our implementation for analyzing policies of various sizes and kinds of logical structure.
