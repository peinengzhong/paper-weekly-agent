## 1. DexEngine: Scaling Human Demonstrations into Physically Executable Whole-Robot Data

- 作者：Wongyun Yu, Honggeun Kim, Donguk Lee, Minchan Kim, Kyoung Whan Choe, Minsu Cho

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T00:46:23Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33579v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33579v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A growing body of recent work addresses the retargeting of human hand-object demonstrations to robotic hands. To train whole-robot policies, however, the retargeted motions must be kinematically feasible and physically executable, and be produced efficiently at scale. We present DexEngine, a simulation pipeline that turns human manipulation data into train-ready, physically executable arm-hand trajectories. DexEngine first places each clip's hand-object motion where the arms can reach it, finds an arm motion that follows the human hands, and then corrects the motion in physics simulation with residual reinforcement learning. From nine human hand-object datasets, we generate about 5,200 clips for each of four dexterous hands, augmented into 65,000 trajectories (116 h) and 325,000 episodes (578 h) in total. Even with arms attached, DexEngine tracks objects more accurately than prior floating-hand methods and runs over 10x faster than RL-based ones. Policies trained on our data far outperform those trained on the same clips without physics correction or with the arms attached after a floating-hand correction, showing that fine-tuning data need physics correction. Mid-training on our data further improves them at equal fine-tuning budget, in simulation and on a real robot.

## 2. ViTacLab: Physically Consistent Visuo-Tactile Simulation, Benchmarking, and Representation Learning for Robotic Manipulation

- 作者：Linjing You, Zeming Yang, Zhenhao Shen, Shengqiang Xu, Guanda Li, Wenxuan Li, Jiyu Guo, Jasper Lu, Kyle Xu, Shawn Xie, Junjie Zhu, Siyu Wu, Jian Liu, Changhao Zhang, Rongchong Li, Zeyi Li, Hengshuang Zhao, Ruihai Wu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T12:06:34Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32476v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32476v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Tactile sensing is essential for contact-rich robotic manipulation, but existing visuo-tactile simulators trade physical fidelity for scalability, and benchmarks largely focus on two-finger grippers. We present ViTacLab, a framework comprising ViTacSim, ViTacBench, and ViTacPolicy. ViTacSim uses a tri-modal tactile primitive: a global sparse contact-force lattice, a local dense force array, and tactile images. Anchored to PhysX contact-solver outputs, it corrects normal inconsistency through local stiffness alignment and reconstructs tangential forces under decoupled slip-stick regimes while preserving GPU parallelism. A joint projection preserves the sparse wrench and enforces local friction cones. ViTacBench spans parallel grippers, dexterous hands, and bimanual systems, with tasks ranging from vision-dominant to tactile-dominant regimes. ViTacPolicy aggregates variable numbers of heterogeneous tactile inputs using spatial-pose embeddings and a Transformer, and aligns force-array and tactile-image modalities through a two-stream VQ-VAE with a shared codebook. Controlled Hertzian-reference indentation and real-sensor sliding tests reduce normal and tangential force RMSE from 2.31 and 1.85 N to 0.42 and 0.53 N, respectively. Throughput reaches 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS at 256 parallel environments. Simulation and real-world experiments show gains on several contact-rich tasks, while low absolute success on tactile-dominant tasks highlights the remaining difficulty of purely tactile control.

## 3. Hi3R-VLA: Grounding Semantics and Actions in 3D

- 作者：Shizhe Chen, Paul Pacaud, Cordelia Schmid

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T20:44:27Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31535v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31535v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



While recent vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly incorporate 3D geometry, how to effectively exploit it for precise and generalizable action prediction remains a central challenge. We introduce Hi3R-VLA, a 3D policy that grounds heterogeneous modalities - vision, language, and action - in explicit 3D geometry for robotic manipulation. Our model integrates spatially-aligned visual features and language conditioning into point cloud representations, enriching scene geometry with fine-grained, task-relevant semantics. It then anchors low-level control commands in the same 3D coordinate frame and uses 3D-RoPE enhanced action-to-point attention over coarse-to-fine point features to model geometric relationships between action targets and observed points. Our analysis demonstrates the importance of semantic fusion within 3D and spatial alignment between observations and actions. With fewer than 250M trainable parameters, Hi3R-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa, RLBench, RoboTwin2, DexJoCo and DexSuite across diverse manipulation tasks and embodiments, from single-arm and dual-arm grippers to dexterous hands. Real-robot evaluations further demonstrate its effectiveness in the physical world. The model, code and dataset are publicly released at https://cshizhe.github.io/projects/hi3r_vla.html.

## 4. ArtKit: Motion-Driven Re-Segmentation and Link Re-Generation for Articulated Objects from RGB-D Video

- 作者：Sylvia Yuan, Dan Wang, Vivian Yu, Ravi Ramamoorthi, Xinrui Cui

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T20:24:16Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33403v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33403v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



An RGB-D camera that films an object being moved, often from one viewpoint, sees one side of it and how its parts move. We present ArtKit, which takes such a video as input to create an articulated model with complete parts, a kinematic tree, and typed joints. Our central idea is generating the hidden geometry and verifying it against what the camera sees. A generative part prior proposes complete, part-decomposed geometry based on the first frame. Point tracks, grouped into rigid trajectories, split and merge the generated parts into links. We then fit revolute and prismatic joints, with a visibility-aware energy ensuring that the articulated model explains the depth observed across the video. A rule checks against the video to choose between synchronous group fits and per-frame chain fits, so that one pipeline serves a diverse range of articulated objects, such as household items and robotic hands. Finally, we regenerate each link of the chosen fit as one part and keep it wherever it agrees better with the observations. We evaluate on Articulate-100 and on HArt, a new benchmark of 60 PartNet-Mobility objects and 8 robotic hands and grippers rendered as RGB-D videos with ground-truth parts and joints. ArtKit reduces the axis and part motion errors of the strongest baseline by more than half on the objects, as well as the axis error on the hands. ArtKit also runs on real recordings and generated videos.

## 5. Self-Sensing Compliant Anthropomorphic Hand for Speed-Dictated Shared Force Control in Teleoperation

- 作者：Huijiang Wang, Yu Zhang, Rui Chen, Tristan Bonato, Jaemin Eom, Kai Junge, Cheng Pan, Benhui Dai, Yue Xie, Josie Hughes

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T16:43:27Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32937v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32937v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



In vision-based teleoperation of robotic hands, the operator receives no haptic feedback and therefore struggles to regulate the grasp force, while adding tactile sensors to the fingers increases wiring, cost and integration complexity. Compliant hands offer an alternative, as their mechanical compliance, usually treated only as a passive property for safe and adaptive grasping, can also serve as a source of information. Here, we show that the elastic transmission of a compliant, tendon-driven anthropomorphic hand can itself act as the sensor for shared-control telemanipulation. Series springs in the tendon routing are read by potentiometers in a forearm-mounted motor box, from which the hand estimates its joint states and finger forces without any external sensors. Building on this self-sensing, we develop a contact-aware shared-control framework for VR-based teleoperation. The robot hand is controlled through a mapping from the operator's hand kinematics until a contact is detected proprioceptively. Control then switches to a finger-level force controller whose set point is determined by the operator's grasping speed, and control authority can be allocated to individual fingers. Experiments validate the system's force estimation and increased grasp success rate compared with direct teleoperation for fragile and heavy objects. These results show that compliance can serve not only for passive adaptation but also as an intrinsic sensing resource for physical human-robot collaboration.

## 6. DexLink: Synthesizing Dexterous Behaviors from Chained Hand-object Poses

- 作者：Meenal Parakh, Deep Patel, Beining Han, Jia Deng

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T18:59:36Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34936v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34936v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Synthesizing diverse dexterous manipulation policies is challenging: existing simulation-based approaches rely on human demonstrations which are difficult to scale, or use generic reward functions, which provide limited control over the hand-object interactions. We propose DexLink, which addresses these limitations by synthesizing diverse dexterous behaviors from sparse, reusable hand-object interaction templates. Given key task waypoints, DexLink retargets static interaction templates to target objects using an optimization that preserves spatial hand-object relationships. It then links the retargeted poses into an approximate reference trajectory and uses residual reinforcement learning to refine this reference into a closed-loop policy in simulation. Experiments spanning single-hand, bimanual, articulated, and dynamic tasks demonstrate that DexLink provides fine-grained control over task timing, grasp types, and high-level execution. The modular interaction templates transfer reliably across object scales, instances, and categories; without object-specific human demonstrations, the framework also matches the performance of dense reference-tracking methods. Finally, zero-shot deployment of policies trained purely in simulation shows promise for the simulation-based framework.

## 7. FineDex: Bridging Fine-Grained Human-Object Interaction Generation to Dexterous Humanoid Manipulation

- 作者：Yujia Zeng, Yuxin Chen, Yuzhen Song, Runhan Huang, Chensheng Peng, Yiyang Shao, Koushil Sreenath, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Yilun Du

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34884v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34884v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Dexterity is, at its core, finger-level control, yet existing dexterous manipulation and human-object interaction (HOI) generation methods rarely let one specify how an interaction is performed: five-fingered hands are often reduced to grippers, or finger usage is copied from individual human demonstrations. We present FineDex, a framework that makes finger-level intent both expressible and executable. FineDex learns coordinated human body, hand, and object motions that follow fine-grained instructions on hand selection, individual fingers, and contacts with articulated objects. To bridge generation and execution, FineDex leverages generated interactions as actionable priors for an agent that discovers physically feasible contact strategies through practice in a physics-based digital twin. By adapting at test time without per-instance retraining, FineDex generalizes across fine-grained instructions and object configurations. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art HOI generation quality and fine-grained controllability, substantial improvements in dexterous manipulation success, and successful deployment on a real dexterous humanoid robot.

## 8. Nav4Manip: From Navigation to Dexterous Mobile Manipulation through Co-Evolution

- 作者：Yusen Feng, Zixi Kang, Zhijun Yin, Changxiao Liu, Tianhong Gao, Jiazhao Zhang, Jiahang Liu, Libin Liu, Zhizheng Zhang, He Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T16:29:17Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34655v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34655v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous mobile manipulation is essential for robots to perform useful tasks in complex, unstructured environments. However, existing systems face two challenges. First, navigation remains too coarse for downstream manipulation, which often requires a precise base position and approach orientation rather than merely reaching a meter-scale goal region. Second, navigation and manipulation are commonly trained in isolation, lacking a feedback loop that enables the two capabilities to co-evolve. These limitations prevent current systems from reliably executing long-horizon dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks in real-world environments. To address them, we introduce Nav4Manip, a bidirectionally coupled co-evolution system for long-horizon dexterous mobile manipulation. It comprises two complementary architectures: Nav4Manip-N learns long-horizon navigation and refines its policy through manipulation feedback, while Nav4Manip-M trains a world-action model (WAM) for downstream dexterous manipulation. We train Nav4Manip-N in three stages: multi-task co-tuning learns navigation behavior and manipulation-aware affordance prediction; rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) rejects low-quality policy-generated paths and fine-tunes the model on retained trajectories; and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) uses counterfactual preferences to further improve navigation decisions. To support this framework, we establish the Nav-N-Manip benchmark and construct Nav4Manip-Pairs from benchmark rollouts, providing large-scale paired navigation--manipulation trajectories and affordance supervision. Extensive experiments on public vision-language navigation (VLN) benchmarks and Nav-N-Manip demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, while real-world evaluations show that Nav4Manip completes diverse dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks. Project website: https://nav4manip.github.io/.

## 9. Targeted Adaptation of World-Action Models via Interaction and Motion Heads

- 作者：Yuze Zhu, Jacky Li, Yusen Luo, Ken Jiankun Zheng, Chancharik Mitra, Trevor Darrell, Roei Herzig

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T08:28:00Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33963v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33963v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



World-Action Models (WAMs) are a promising new paradigm of robot manipulation policies that jointly generate future video and actions. Successful manipulation requires both identifying where to interact and modeling how the robot and objects move. Yet standard adaptation methods, such as full fine-tuning and LoRA, do not explicitly select parameters aligned with these manipulation-specific capabilities. Motivated by this challenge, we discover two functionally specialized families of attention heads: Next-Interaction Heads, which localize upcoming grasp-and-release targets, and Motion Heads, which track movement across frames. Furthermore, we introduce HeadLight, a parameter-efficient refinement method that jointly adapts Next-Interaction Heads and Motion Heads. On RoboTwin tasks unseen during fine-tuning, HeadLight achieves 62.28% success, compared with 57.89% for full fine-tuning and 58.66% for LoRA, while updating only 1.1% of parameters. Inference-time ablations further validate the functional importance of these heads: ablating Motion Heads reduces manipulation success from 57.89% to 0.94%, while ablating Next-Interaction Heads lowers it to 40.61%, both substantially more than ablating equally many random heads. Finally, we show that our approach is effective across multiple WAM architectures and real-robot manipulation settings.

## 10. Why Does Sensor Depth Fail Learned Manipulation Policies? Finding the Failure Mode with ACT

- 作者：Inayat Rasool, Chulwoo Pack, Shabbir Ahmed

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-24T19:38:42Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33352v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33352v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Learned manipulation policies, from task-specific imitation policies to vision-language-action (VLA) models, rely primarily on RGB images. While some studies report gains from sensor depth, others avoid depth cameras because of measurement noise. Depth cameras introduce both noise and missing depth values (holes), whose relative effects remain unclear. In this study, we calibrate a sensor model using a real wrist-mounted stereo camera and separately evaluate noise and holes on RGB-D imitation policies with three visual encoders in simulation. We found that complete depth improves success by approximately ten percentage points, and most of this gain persists even at twenty times the camera's noise level. In contrast, holes covering about one-sixth of the wrist image eliminate the detectable benefit of depth. The validity mask strongly influences depth reliance: marking a small fraction of pixels as invalid reduces reliance, while filling holes and marking them valid restores most of the benefit. Policies trained on clean depth also lose performance when holes appear only at deployment, sometimes falling below RGB-only performance. These findings suggest that depth cameras for learned policies should therefore be judged by depth coverage more than measurement precision.

## 11. Cairn: Object-Centric 3D Trajectory Priors for Steering Frozen VLAs

- 作者：Agelos Kratimenos, Kostas Daniilidis, Evangelos Chatzipantazis

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T18:57:29Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34914v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34914v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Vision-language-action models (VLAs) are capable low-level controllers, but they tie where objects go to the layouts they were trained on: when objects are moved or obstacles block the way, they carry them to the wrong place or into collisions. Fine-tuning on new layouts needs robot data for each one and erodes skills the model already has. We introduce Cairn, which keeps the VLA frozen and steers it at test time along learned 3D trajectories of the object in its gripper. A small prior reads point clouds of the scene and the held object, along with its pose when grasped and the instruction, and samples SE(3) trajectories up to its release that lead it around obstacles and turn it to fit through gaps. The prior and the VLA form the two levels of a hierarchical planner in which feedback runs both ways: the VLA's intended motion chooses and reshapes the trajectories, and the chosen one guides the VLA's denoising. Its predicted actions, mapped to the held object's poses through the grasp, are pulled toward the next waypoint, while the VLA's weights stay untouched. Across 1,941 held-out LIBERO scenes with displaced objects and obstacles, Cairn raises the success rate of a frozen π0.5 from 24.5% to 95.1%, above that of a π0.5 fine-tuned on our training layouts, without hurting standard LIBERO. On the third-party LIBERO-PRO benchmark, it raises success from 27.3% to 38.0%, and from 54.4% to 81.1% on the episodes it steers. On a real Franka, with a prior trained purely in simulation, it guides the frozen DROID π0.5 around an obstacle and to a placement at the limit of the arm's reach, where it otherwise fails. All code, models and data will be made public.

## 12. RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies

- 作者：Insup Lee, Hojin Jeon, Sunguk Shin, Seoungbin Bae, Taekwan Kim, Jinwoo Shin, Dongsu Han

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T18:30:43Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34864v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34864v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Since vision-language-action (VLA) policies can inherit the slow pace of human demonstrations, accelerating these demonstrations before fine-tuning can lead to faster VLA policies without an additional speed-selection module at deployment. Several offline speedup methods have focused on identifying segments that require less precision and assigning predefined speedup factors to the segments. However, even within a single segment, a speedup factor suitable for steady motion may be too high when precise alignment is required. To this end, this paper presents RePace, an offline reasoning framework that determines how much to accelerate each part of a demonstration. For the same recorded movement, RePace measures how much each candidate speedup factor shortens the sequence and compares the resulting observation-action pairs used for training. Given the task instruction, a pretrained language model uses the visual context and original motion of successive windows to interpret these candidate retiming effects and select a speedup profile for the complete episode. Experimental results demonstrate that RePace outperforms the baselines in overall task execution efficiency on both LIBERO and RoboCasa-Kitchen. On all three tasks with a gripper-equipped Franka Research 3 arm, RePace matches or exceeds the acceleration baselines in success rate and completes successful trials faster.

## 13. Toward Agentic Robotics: A Large-Scale Study of Harness Design for Robot-Use Agents

- 作者：Yuxiang Lai, Jike Zhong, Yu-Jhe Li, Lan Wang, Chengzhi Liu, Youbao Tang, Ming Li, Xi Xiao, Xiaofeng Yang, Liang Zhao, Zheda Mai, Ruixi Fan

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T17:39:10Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34778v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34778v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Recent vision-language models (VLMs) can act as robot-use agents that understand diverse scenes and control robots through policy code and tools. However, they predict 3D gripper poses poorly even with extended reasoning, struggling with precise manipulation. Vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action (WAM) models are the opposite: they execute fast, smooth control on trained tasks, yet fail under domain shifts or when tasks require long-horizon memory and reasoning. Agentic robots therefore combine VLM reasoning and perception with precise VLA/WAM control through a policy harness. Yet harness design spans thousands of configurations, shifting success by tens of points with the same models. Moreover, testing even one design takes thousands of rollouts at high API and GPU cost, so prior works often commit to a single design chosen from few alternatives. To guide harness design, we evaluate over 470,000 rollouts across eight simulation benchmarks, six VLMs, and 15 VLA/WAM checkpoints. Seven controlled studies show effective harnesses must adapt to both VLM and VLA/WAM capabilities. Our findings specify what structure the VLM's policy code needs, where VLA manipulation needs help from tools and policy code, and when to involve the VLM at test time. The reference recipe X-Harness shows that these findings compose without weight updates, lifting base policies up to $+42.8$ points across memory, reasoning, and domain shifts.

## 14. WISER-RL: A Wake-Sleep Learning Mechanism for Robot Reinforcement Learning from One Demonstration

- 作者：Kwunhang Wong, Yang Bai, Qingyang Bao, Liudi Yang, Chenzhi Liu, Jiehong Lin, Wei Huang, Lin Li, Ziyuan Liu, Yujie Yang, Gitta Kutyniok, Zhongrui Wang, Xiaojuan Qi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-25T09:22:42Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34043v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34043v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



A robot learning from one human demonstration may rarely succeed on its own, leaving reinforcement learning (RL) with little reward feedback. We introduce WISER-RL ({W}ake--sleep policy improvement loop, {I}mitation, and {S}elective {E}xperience {R}eplay), a composite reinforcement learning framework that turns assisted practice into autonomous control. During wake, GPTDAgger monitors disagreement among actions sampled by a diffusion policy. When disagreement crosses a threshold, a frozen GPT controller uses retrieved experience to continue the episode. During sleep, Conflict-Aware Experience Reweighting (CAER) compares visually matched successful trajectories and downweights conflicting action windows with more steps remaining to success. The policy imitates the reweighted successes and learns from both successes and failures through offline advantage-guided RL before collecting the next round. After final online PPO refinement, the autonomous policy succeeded in 100% evaluations on each of six LIBERO tasks and 100% trials on each of two real-robot tasks.

## 15. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. A clean evaluation of all 1,060 final policies agreed with the curves: of the 728 boundary-fault runs both curve checks passed, 723 scored no more than three standard deviations below fault-free seeds, though nearly all ended on other weights and, for reward faults, logged other returns.   Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

## 16. LLMs as MDP Designers: A Dependency-Aware Agentic Framework for Automated Robot Policy Generation

- 作者：Hong Je-Gal, Jinwoo Kim, Chanbin Yi, Hyun-Suk Lee

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-03T14:29:36Z

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04974v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. Rather than using large language models (LLMs) as execution-time decision makers, we use them as designers that construct RL pipelines before deployment, including Markov decision process (MDP) formulation and simulator implementation. We identify a phase-wise structure in this problem: MDP formulation is dependency-structured and sequential, whereas simulator implementation becomes modular once the formulation is fixed. Based on this structure, we propose ARPG, a dependency-aware agentic framework that performs sequential MDP modeling, localized verification and correction, and simulator-structured implementation while externalizing intermediate artifacts. ARPG targets a key failure mode of automated policy generation: executable code may still yield non-learnable policies when state, action, reward, and termination artifacts are not semantically aligned. Experiments in Isaac Sim show that ARPG improves modeling correctness, environment executability, and policy-generation success compared with monolithic LLM baselines and off-the-shelf agentic systems. Additional demonstrations on custom robotics, standard RL, and engineering-domain MDPs further suggest that the same modeling pipeline can be reused beyond the main benchmark suite.

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.

## 20. Automated Policy Analysis

- 作者：Konstantine Arkoudas, Shoshana Loeb, Ritu Chadha, C. Jason Chiang, Keith Whittaker

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2012-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=84XY6nHYsD

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=84XY6nHYsD

- 分类：POLICY 2012



Static analysis of access-control policies is becoming increasingly important. Such analysis can reveal errors and vulnerabilities in the policies, as well as logical inconsistencies, unintended effects, and discrepancies between different policies or different versions of the same policy. In the process, it helps policy developers to better understand the effects of their policies. Policy analysis has typically been done by hand. For instance, when a bug is discovered and corrected, the resulting policy is manually inspected to ensure that the fix works and that it does not introduce any new problems. But when the policies are large or their logical structure non-trivial, performing such analysis manually is tedious and error-prone. In this paper we show how to automate a wide array of useful policy analyses. This is accomplished by representing policies as logical formulas in the SMT (satisfiability-modulo-theory) subset of first-order logic, and couching analysis questions as SMT problems, which are then solved by efficient off-the-shelf SMT solvers. Because SMT solvers can reason about arithmetic and inductive data types, in addition to Boolean constraints, our system can handle many policies that cannot be analyzed by existing policy engines. We describe the formulation of a number of useful analyses (consistency, completeness, and observational equivalence), and report experimental results on the efficiency of our implementation for analyzing policies of various sizes and kinds of logical structure.
