## 1. GAP: Generalized Adaptation Prior for Dexterous Piano Playing

- 作者：Jinyi Chen, Hongyi Li, Lei Jiang, Xianwei Liu, Yanhong Liang, Yulin Chen, Yucheng Tao, Yongbin Jin, Hongtao Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T09:24:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12111v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12111v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous hands enable versatile manipulation, but policies trained in simulation often degrade on hardware owing to non-idealities, and the high-dimensional action space makes real-world adaptation costly. Piano playing, which requires precise and coordinated multifinger motion, offers a representative testbed. We propose GAP (Generalized Adaptation Prior for Dexterous Piano Playing), a sample-efficient adaptation framework that learns how to adapt, rather than how to act, during simulation pretraining and transfers this adaptation prior to hardware. Between repeated executions, GAP uses feedback to adjust the actions of the next one: a gradient guidance network learns from paired executions how adjusting actions affects playing performance, and its gradients guide these adjustments. In simulation, GAP yields approximately 2.4 times the F1 scores improvement of the existing fast-adaptation baseline for dexterous piano playing under the same interaction budget. The learned prior also generalizes to musical pieces held out from pretraining. On a physical dexterous hand, the same simulation-pretrained prior improves F1 scores by 3.6%-23% across three pieces of varying difficulty after approximately 7 min of adaptation each. These results suggest a practical route to reduce the real-world interaction needed to deploy dexterous hands.

## 2. Fade to... Red? Scheduling Coloured Noise Exploration Supports Efficient Robot Learning with Reduced Movement Jerk

- 作者：Francisco M. López, Francisco Cruz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T04:44:59Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11868v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11868v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Exploration in deep reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control is commonly driven by temporally uncorrelated Gaussian (white) action noise, which leads to jerky motions with locally constrained exploration. In contrast, temporally correlated coloured noise, particularly pink noise, has been shown to support more efficient exploration in RL. Motivated by these findings, we inquire whether scheduling changes to the noise colour over the course of training can outperform white noise for continuous control robotics. We perform a systematic study of 49 linear schedules between white and red noise, together with generalised state-dependent exploration (gSDE), on four sparse-reward manipulation tasks with a Fetch arm and a Shadow Dexterous Hand. We find that the noise colour at the end of training does not affect learning efficiency or end performance, whereas fading towards red noise halves the jerk of exploration in every task. Balancing learning efficiency, end performance and smoothness, red noise emerges as the preferred strategy: it learns as efficiently as pink noise with almost 2-fold less movement jerk. We therefore recommend exploration schedulers that fade towards red noise as the default for off-policy robot learning.

## 3. Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids (DASH): Latency-Agnostic Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models for Humanoid Manipulation

- 作者：Bingzhang Wang, Nathan Zhao, Jialeng Ni, Jiajin Zheng, Yin Zhang, Kehua Chen, Jian Yao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T04:23:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11854v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11854v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Vision-language-action (VLA) models pair a vision-language backbone with an action head, but in closed-loop control the backbone sits on the control-critical path: while it computes, the robot keeps moving, so each executed action is conditioned on an increasingly stale observation. A larger backbone therefore directly blocks reactivity, especially in humanoid dexterous manipulation. We present DASH, a Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids that removes this coupling. DASH splits the policy into a slow vision-language model (System 2) and a fast flow-matching action head (System 1) that run as concurrent processes over the LCM communication protocol, so the control rate is set by the action head alone, independent of backbone size. The action head fuses stale semantics from a frozen backbone with fresh vision from its own ResNet encoder, desynchronized training exposes it to the staleness it meets at deployment, and an adaptive temporal aggregator aligns overlapping action chunks under variable inference latency. On an asynchronous RoboTwin benchmark that charges inference latency in simulated time, DASH holds 20 Hz control rate from 0.8B to 8B backbones while the synchronous rate falls from 9.7 to 4.1 Hz, with higher success at every size. On a real humanoid with dexterous hands, DASH achieves clearly higher pick-and-place success than the synchronous pipeline.

## 4. Compact and Wide-Force-Range Magnetorheological Hand Exoskeleton for Kinesthetic Feedback in Teleoperation

- 作者：Xianlong Mai, Rui Yu, Rui Chen, Daniele Leonardis, Wenbo Li, Domenico Chiaradia, Antonio Frisoli, Shuaishuai Sun

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T09:44:12Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12130v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12130v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, eess.SY



Hand kinesthetic feedback is crucial for restoring and enhancing human perception of force and displacement in robotic hand teleoperation. Wide perception range of force feedback is vital in rendering objects of different stiffness, especially for high-stiffness rendering. However, existing approaches struggle to achieve wide range of force feedback while maintaining compact and wearable design. This paper presents a hand exoskeleton system employing magnetorheological (MR) brakes to provide a wide range kinesthetic feedback during teleoperation. The MR brake provides a braking torque ranging from 0.01 to 0.918 Nm, while maintaining a compact structure with a total mass of only 39.8 g. The proposed exoskeleton can render fingertip forces up to 21 N while maintaining back-driving force of 1.01 N. Experimental results show that the system allows users to discriminate object stiffness using kinesthetic feedback alone. In a tele-grasping task, when both conditions achieved a 100% success rate, kinesthetic feedback led to a 28.2% reduction in object contact force compared to visual feedback only, enhancing object safety and operator immersion. Questionnaire results further indicate that kinesthetic feedback improves reliability, realism, and user confidence during teleoperation.

## 5. DexForesight: Grounding Dexterous Policies with Implicit Spatial Foresight

- 作者：Zhongxi Chen, Yi Yang, Xinyi Chen, Mingzhe Ye, Yaping Li, Zhihong Liu, Congsheng Xu, Haonan Wang, Zhijie Deng

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11166v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous manipulation requires rich spatial foresight over both the surrounding scene and the robot hand. Existing world-action models often acquire such predictive knowledge through explicit future prediction. We introduce DexForesight, a framework that learns spatial foresight implicitly by injecting dynamics-aware world representations into the latent space of a dexterous policy. During training, DexForesight learns an action-conditioned spatial world model from offline scene depth, kinematic hand geometry, and demonstration trajectories. The world model predicts 3D hand-point motion and visual feature changes, capturing both articulated hand dynamics and scene changes under demonstrated actions, including the motion of fingers occluded from the cameras. At deployment, only the policy is retained, operating on RGB observations, language, and robot state without depth input or explicit future prediction. On 11 DexJoCo simulation tasks, DexForesight improves the overall success rate of the base policy from 49.4% to 60.5%. ## 6. Which Taxels, Not How Many: Tactile Sensor Placement for Dexterous Policies

- 作者：Taeyun Kim, Jinsoo Shin, Taehwa Kim, Taeyoon Kim, Jinyoung Choi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-07T10:37:42Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10647v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10647v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Policies for dexterous manipulation depend on design choices made before training: which sensors enter the observation, where they are placed, and how their readings are arranged for the encoder. These choices are rarely examined. For tactile sensing, simulation studies place taxels uniformly over the hand, and this placement has become a default without being tested. We test this default under a single controlled protocol. The protocol holds the teacher, the training procedure, and the candidate taxels fixed and varies only the sensor configuration and the representation. We evaluate it on in-palm rotation and grasping with three robot hands. A small set of taxels selected from contact statistics matches a far larger uniform set. Taxels at rarely contacted sites are equivalent to no tactile input. With all candidate taxels, their layout in the input tensor does not affect performance, even for a convolutional encoder. Uniform placement therefore overestimates the number of taxels a task requires. We distill these results into lessons for designing tactile policies and into a minimal set of design choices that a tactile policy study should report.

## 7. Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation

## 8. PCTac: Mechanochromic Photonic-Crystal Tactile Sensor for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Ruilin Zhang, Xuanshuo Liu, Junzhe Dai, Kefei Wu, Xuhao Qin, Ziyuan Jiao, Wanlin Li, Yurui Qu, Chenxi Xiao

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12383v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Modern visuotactile sensors primarily sense contact by imaging the deformation of a compliant interface. Using techniques such as photometric stereo, they can recover detailed contact geometry, often represented as surface normals or depth. However, force-related information typically still needs to be inferred indirectly through calibration, geometric reconstruction, or learned mappings. Mechanochromic photonic-crystal (PC) materials offer an alternative physical encoding mechanism: contact loading changes their periodic microstructure and spectral response, directly transforming local stress into a spatially resolved structural-color field. Despite this potential, PC materials have rarely been explored as visuotactile sensors for closed-loop robot learning. We present PCTac, an open-source mechanochromic PC fingertip that uses a dense hue field as its tactile observation. Across 90 physical rollouts, PCTac Hue achieves 68 successes (75.6\%), compared with 60 (66.7\%) for PCTac RGB, 57 (63.3\%) for Wedge RGB, and 29 (32.2\%) for vision-only. PCTac Hue also achieves the highest observed success rate on each evaluated task, including Perturbed Test-Tube Insertion, where it achieves 16/30 successes versus 13/30 for tactile RGB baselines and 1/30 for vision-only. These results demonstrate the potential of mechanochromic structural color as a compact, load-sensitive tactile representation for robot learning and closed-loop manipulation. ## 10. HARP-Bench: Benchmarking Robot Manipulation Around Active Humans

- 作者：Qinhong Zhou, Riley Zong, Chunru Lin, Diyi Hu, Shivraj Singh Bhatti, Hongxin Zhang, Lin Qiu, Hanqing Zeng, Chuang Gan

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-08T04:16:15Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11123v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11123v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Recent robot manipulation benchmarks have enabled systematic evaluation of general-purpose robot policies across diverse objects, scenes, instructions, and embodiments. However, most existing benchmarks still assume that the robot is the only active agent and that task semantics are determined primarily by the instruction and object state. This assumption overlooks a common source of real-world variation: humans actively sharing the workspace. In such settings, humans may reveal an implicit goal, complete part of the task, temporarily change object availability, or constrain safe robot motion while working nearby. We introduce HARP-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating robot manipulation policies in workspaces shared with active humans. HARP-Bench treats human activity as part of the manipulation problem, rather than as background scene variation. It organizes human-present manipulation into four conditions: Cueing, Contribution, Interruption, and Co-presence. These conditions are instantiated through everyday manipulation tasks, including paired human-present variants for controlled comparison with standard manipulation. HARP-Bench also provides a demonstration-generation pipeline for dynamic human-present settings. We evaluate VLA models, imitation-learning methods, and heuristic policies on HARP-Bench. Human-present variations challenge current policies, and higher task success does not consistently improve safety. Quantitative failure analysis identifies manipulation and recovery as the dominant remaining challenges. Training with HARP-Bench demonstrations improves human-present performance in simulation. A real-world study on one task family shows improved performance under human contribution, but not interruption, providing initial evidence of the data's utility and identifying limits of transfer. Code is available at https://github.com/UMass-Embodied-AGI/harp.

## 11. Tactile Sensors Are an Effective Substitute for Wrist Cameras in Contact-Rich or Occluded Manipulation

- 作者：Aijia Yang, Arsh Tangri, Sergey Zakharov, Krishna Murthy Jatavallabhula

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-07T05:33:17Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10490v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10490v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Learned manipulation policies often rely on wrist-mounted cameras for close-range visual feedback that complements external views of the workspace. However, contact-rich manipulation requires establishing and maintaining contacts that can remain hidden even from wrist cameras. Tactile sensors mounted on fingers can reliably and directly measure contact, without needing visual access. In this work, we show that this feedback enables policies to recover the performance advantage of wrist cameras using scene and egocentric vision together with touch. Our approach incorporates two low-cost tactile arrays into a visuotactile ACT policy, without wrist images during training or deployment. We evaluate six real-world task families spanning placement, insertion, occluded retrieval, and sustained-contact tool use. On three contact-rich tasks, we independently vary wrist-camera and tactile availability while keeping the demonstrations, policy architecture, and optimization settings fixed. Adding wrist cameras improves success by 18.3-28.3 percentage points over vision without wrist images. Adding tactile sensing instead improves success by 31.7-60.0 percentage points and yields higher observed success than vision-only policies with wrist cameras on all three tasks, with significant advantages on two under Holm-corrected tests. These results support finger-mounted tactile sensing as an effective alternative to wrist cameras in the evaluated contact-rich settings.

## 12. On Agentic Manipulation Policies

- 作者：Chen Chen, Omid Bagherieh, Naveen Nikamanth Appiah Balaji, Jesse T. Gonzalez, Negin Heravi, Fahad Islam, Xueting Li, Jean-Baptiste Regli, Kejia Ren, Petr Švec, David J. Yoon, Matthias Müller, Vladlen Koltun

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-06T18:59:34Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09892v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG



Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6\% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2\% and 42.9\% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The agent succeeds zero-shot in 104 of 108 real-world trials spanning twelve tasks and three robot instances, reducing failure rates by 20x and 15x relative to strong VLA and WAM baselines. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation.

## 13. VLA Adaptation with Real and Simulated Data: A Case Study on Robotic Chess

- 作者：Matěj Kripner, Milan Straka

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T16:43:53Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12676v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12676v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained as general-purpose foundation models for robot learning, and require fine-tuning for each downstream task and robot. This raises practical questions such as how much data and training updates are needed, how checkpoints should be selected, and whether simulation can be leveraged to alleviate the cost of real data collection. We study these questions on a task that requires both precise manipulation and spatial understanding of the scene. In this task, an SO-101 arm moves a chess king between two squares that are given only by their names, such as e4 and a5. Partial results indicate that pretraining on the simulated demonstrations helps substantially, most clearly when real data is scarce. We also present a negative result, in which training on the policy's own rollouts with filtered behavior cloning, RECAP-style advantage conditioning, corrections from an automatic coach, or residual reinforcement learning did not yield a measurable improvement over plain behavior cloning in our setting. We release the simulator, the expert and the real demonstrations.

## 14. Be My Eye: Learning to See Beyond a Robot's Own Perspective

- 作者：Baifu Huang, Shilin Fang, Jianan Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T12:19:15Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12295v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12295v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Robots operating in the real world are inherently subject to partial observability, particularly in multi-robot collaboration, where individual observations may be insufficient for effective task execution. A key challenge is leveraging complementary observations from partner robots to support collaborative decision-making. We argue that information sharing should go beyond passively transmitting raw observations or generic visual features. Instead, robots should selectively encode task-relevant information according to task objectives and execution progress. To this end, we propose a task-oriented active context sharing framework that transforms robot observations into compact representations, preserving critical information under constrained communication budgets. The framework adopts a two-stage training paradigm. In the first stage, onboard observations and task instructions are jointly encoded into compact context representations. Task-oriented future prediction and soft task-progress correspondence learning guide this collaboration-agnostic compression, preserving information about task objectives and execution progress. In the second stage, the learned representations are integrated into a pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, enabling bidirectional context exchange. Each robot combines complementary partner information with local observations to guide action generation. Action supervision jointly refines the context representations and policy, aligning information sharing with collaborative decision-making. Controlled experiments demonstrate substantial performance gains under constrained communication budgets, while three real-world experiments validate the framework in complex dual-robot tasks. These results highlight a shift from passive visual transmission toward active, task-oriented context sharing that directly supports collaborative decision-making.

## 15. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

## 16. LLMs as MDP Designers: A Dependency-Aware Agentic Framework for Automated Robot Policy Generation

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. Rather than using large language models (LLMs) as execution-time decision makers, we use them as designers that construct RL pipelines before deployment, including Markov decision process (MDP) formulation and simulator implementation. We identify a phase-wise structure in this problem: MDP formulation is dependency-structured and sequential, whereas simulator implementation becomes modular once the formulation is fixed. ## 17. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 18. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. ## 19. DexUMI: Using Human Hand as the Universal Manipulation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Mengda Xu, Han Zhang, Yifan Hou, Zhenjia Xu, Linxi Fan, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2025-09-19T19:43:41Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.

## 20. Arm Robot: AR-Enhanced Embodied Control and Visualization for Intuitive Robot Arm Manipulation

- 作者：Siyou Pei, Alexander Chen, Ronak Kaoshik, Ruofei Du, Yang Zhang

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2024-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- 分类：CoRR 2024



Embodied interaction has been introduced to human-robot interaction (HRI) as a type of teleoperation, in which users control robot arms with bodily action via handheld controllers or haptic gloves. Embodied teleoperation has made robot control intuitive to non-technical users, but differences between humans' and robots' capabilities \eg ranges of motion and response time, remain challenging. In response, we present Arm Robot, an embodied robot arm teleoperation system that helps users tackle human-robot discrepancies. Specifically, Arm Robot (1) includes AR visualization as real-time feedback on temporal and spatial discrepancies, and (2) allows users to change observing perspectives and expand action space. We conducted a user study (N=18) to investigate the usability of the Arm Robot and learn how users perceive the embodiment. Our results show users could use Arm Robot's features to effectively control the robot arm, providing insights for continued work in embodied HRI.
