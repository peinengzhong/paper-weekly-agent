## 1. Task2Hand: A Contact-Grounded Skill for Parametric Dexterous-Hand Morphology Design

- 作者：Haoyang Wang, Weibo Gao, Zhengxiong Li, Lingfeng Tao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-20T18:48:12Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27967v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27967v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous hand design demands repeated expert work across geometry, robot-description code, collision debugging, grasp initialization, and policy training. We present Task2Hand, a Skill: a specification package that turns a functional manipulation brief into a parameterized hand model and routes every candidate through cost-ordered contact, dynamics, and learning gates. Each gate returns a status and an evidence record; on failure, the Skill records the earliest blocker, revises one bounded parameter group, and reruns the cheaper gates. The agent generates, validates, and trains autonomously within declared bounds, and escalates trade-offs, plateaus, and conflicting evidence to a human who sets the next design direction. An evidence-driven design trajectory for bidirectional in-hand manipulation yielded SAGE Hand (Skill-Aware Generative Embodiment): four fingers, 16 actuated DoF, an integrated guiding palm, compact roots, cylindrical tips, and a collision-cleared opposable thumb. Under a matched training protocol, SAGE Hand reaches 45.6% task success on a hexagonal prism against 37.3% for the LEAP hand, and 70.8% on a bicolor cylinder against 51.6%. Dense simulated tactile input more than halves the cylinder drop rate. In a controlled iteration study, the Skill path preserves capture at 164/165 trials while the path without the Skill degrades to 14/165, confirming that the gates act as a regression guard during refinement. Accepted candidates pack with URDF/MJCF, metrics, checkpoints, and a provenance manifest.

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV



Computer-use agents now solve most desktop tasks through direct software input, but a robot using the same computer must turn GUI knowledge into physical contact with a keyboard and mouse. We introduce ROSWorld, a benchmark that couples a physically simulated robot workstation to a live OSWorld desktop, so that a robot and a desktop agent pursue the same goals under the same official evaluators. ## 3. Latent Jacobian Steer: Eliciting Contact-Rich Out-of-Distribution Behaviors from Pretrained Humanoid Controllers

- 作者：S K Swaminathan, Shivam Sood, Shreyansh Kansal, Chengyang He, Guillaume Adrien Sartoretti

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-20T09:14:25Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27338v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27338v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Given a pretrained general latent-conditioned whole-body humanoid controller, say a behavioral foundation model (BFM), how can we elicit complex and contact-rich out-of-distribution behaviors? Raw joint-action exploration enables direct, task-specific adjustments but can disrupt coordination learned by the pretrained controller, whereas latent exploration retains that coordination but is difficult to steer toward desired physical effects. To combine the complementary strengths of these exploration spaces, we propose Latent Jacobian Steer (LJS), which couples sampling in direct joint-action space with the semantics of the learned frozen controller's input latent space. For a task specified through desired physical effects, we sample task-relevant joint-action perturbations and map them through a damped local inverse of the policy Jacobian to obtain candidate latent ($z$) commands. These commands are executed through the frozen pretrained policy, while exploration is directed toward the physical outcomes required by the new task. Candidate outcomes are evaluated in simulation and composed into latent-command sequences that accomplish the task. Ablations show that neither action-space nor latent-space exploration alone provides the same capability as their coupling. Across diverse out-of-distribution tasks, simulation-based searching using this method produces contact-rich behaviors, including hurdling, manipulating articulated objects, and opening doors through non-prehensile contact. Over ten seeds, LJS completes more of each humanoid task than when searching in either space alone. Beyond humanoids, it also steers a goal-conditioned dexterous hand policy through four assembly tasks better than searching in goal space directly.

## 4. TapTacTouch: Shape Completion from Sparse Tactile Contacts with Geometric Priors

- 作者：Yoobin Shin, Jaeyoung Lee, Jieun Kang, Hyun Song, Woojin Kim, Andrew Jaeyong Choi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T10:42:58Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24207v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24207v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



3D object reconstruction is essential for robotic perception and manipulation, yet conventional approaches predominantly rely on visual observations that can be limited by occlusion or unavailable in visually constrained environments. In this work, we propose a vision-free, training-free, and category-agnostic framework for reconstructing 3D object meshes from sparse tactile contacts without relying on pretrained shape models. A 6-DoF robotic arm equipped with a 20-DoF multi-finger robotic hand collects tactile observations using 3x3 pressure sensor arrays mounted on the finger pads. The acquired tactile measurements are transformed into 3D contact constraints through robot kinematics and integrated into a multi-scale Gaussian Process Implicit Surface (GPIS). To address sparse and incomplete surface observations, the proposed method incorporates contact confidence weighting, weak pseudo-surface constraints, and an observation-driven geometric prior to preserve local surface geometry and provide structural guidance in unobserved regions. Real-world experiments on six objects achieve a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 1.610, an Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) of 7.738, and an F-Score of 95.00, with reconstruction completed in an average of 49.99 s per object. These results demonstrate that sparse tactile sensing can provide sufficient geometric information for continuous 3D object reconstruction without visual or semantic information.

## 5. Porosification of the dielectric layer in alternating-current electroluminescence devices enables high-resolution tactile imaging

- 作者：Mingwei Gu, Licheng Li, Zeyuan Zhou, Ying Yang, Zixiang Nie, Xingyu Ma, Junshuai Chen, Lin Zheng, Jinhui Gu, Jia-Sen Xie, Ziling Wei, Mengxing Huang, Jin Ge

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-14T12:47:44Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19384v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19384v1.pdf

- 分类：cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.optics



Vision-based tactile sensing enables high-resolution contact perception, yet bulky optical architectures consume valuable space and constrain the functional design of dexterous robotic hands. Here we introduce a self-emissive tactile imaging strategy based on alternating-current electroluminescence (ACEL) devices, in which a compressible porous dielectric encodes contact pressure into spatial emission patterns without external illumination or discrete sensing pixels. We identify a nonlinear capacitance-dependent emission window that provides a basis for tunable pressure-to-light conversion. Pore-size engineering further suppresses local emission deficiencies, reducing spatial intensity variation by nearly ninefold and enabling faithful imaging of contact features down to 100 μm. The continuous electroluminescent interface also reproduces complex contact patterns without computational reconstruction of electrical fields. Direct lamination of the ACEL device onto a complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) image sensor is expected to enable compact contact imaging in an assembly less than 5 mm thick, substantially thinner than conventional vision-based tactile sensors. This approach could reduce the space required for high-resolution tactile imaging, offering greater design freedom for dexterous robotic hands.

## 8. Coherent Moment Propagation: Epistemic Uncertainty for Pre-trained Robot Policies

- 作者：Miguel Saavedra-Ruiz, Anqi Li, Claudia D'Arpino, Alperen Degirmenci, Tucker Hermans, Fabio Ramos

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-19T22:17:33Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26855v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26855v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Flow matching policies underpin the state-of-the-art in robot learning, with impressive results ranging from long-horizon planning to dexterous manipulation. However, they provide no measure of whether their predicted commands can be trusted, particularly in novel situations outside their training set. Epistemic uncertainty reflects uncertainty about the model's weights and quantifies this ignorance, but calculating it at inference time from a flow matching policy is not trivial. Actions are produced by an ODE solver, requiring uncertainty to be propagated through the entire integration process. BayesDiff addresses this problem for diffusion models by propagating the mean and variance of the state through the denoising chain under a last-layer Laplace posterior approximation, but it severely underestimates the epistemic uncertainty. In this paper we: i) prove that, for flow matching and diffusion ODEs, BayesDiff's total variance decays at rate $O(1/T)$ as the number of Euler steps $T$ used by the solver increases; ii) introduce Coherent Moment Propagation (CoMP), two recursions inspired by the extended and unscented Kalman filters that fix this problem by carrying the cross-covariance between state and weights; and iii) widen the last-layer posterior with observation embeddings the policy already computes, improving uncertainty estimates without retraining for CoMP and several test-time estimators alike. We demonstrate these benefits on three manipulation benchmarks with applications in failure detection and demonstration selection for fine-tuning, where CoMP corrects BayesDiff's variance collapse while matching or outperforming several uncertainty estimators.

## 12. Learning under Bounded Manipulation for Quadratic Incentive Design

- 作者：Arda Güçlü, Subhonmesh Bose, Tamer Başar

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-19T19:29:39Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26736v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26736v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.GT, stat.ML



We study a repeated quadratic incentive design game in which a leader learns a follower's private type from noisy observations of the follower's actions and uses her estimates in an affine incentive rule. The follower knows the leader's learning policy, which incorporates past observations to produce estimates of the follower's type, and can deviate from his myopic best response to influence the leader's future estimates. We ask how the follower's ability to manipulate these observations affects the leader's performance. Under a manipulation budget $C_T$ constraining the expected cumulative squared deviation of the follower's actions from his myopic best response, we establish a minimax regret rate of order $\max\{\log T, \min\{C_T,T\}\}$ for approximately rational followers. Our information-theoretic lower bound applies to a general class of causal estimation and manipulation policies for the leader and the follower. We provide a simple learning policy that achieves the lower bound. Finally, we show that under a fixed learning rule, increasing the manipulation budget of the follower can counterintuitively reduce the leader's regret.

Single-modality action-expert pretraining controls competition from visual shortcuts before vision--language--action (VLA) adaptation. ## 15. Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance

- 作者：Sunli Chen, Yun Zong, Qinhong Zhou, Xiaowen Qiu, Jiaben Chen, Zeyuan Yang, Chuang Gan

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-20T06:12:51Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27182v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27182v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Agent harnesses are turn-based: the agent acts, waits for the result, then reasons. When the action invokes a pretrained robot policy, such as a vision-language-action (VLA) or world action model (WAM), the policy may react continuously to observations, but the agent cannot revisit its decision until the call returns. Meanwhile, a person may step into the robot's path or move the object it is about to grasp. Invoking the agent more often narrows this blind interval, but a reasoning model takes seconds per decision and most control steps need no new one. We propose TRIP, a training-free, event-driven harness in which the agent programs its own interrupts. With each call, the agent writes a tripwire: a lightweight, stateful Python program that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM (for example, to locate a person and measure their distance), and decides whether the call continues or control returns to the agent. When it fires, the harness halts the policy and returns control with the reason; the agent then reassesses, adapts, and continues under a rewritten tripwire. We evaluate TRIP with three VLAs and one WAM on RoboHD, a benchmark of 168 RoboCasa365 scenes in which a simulated person disturbs the robot and any human-robot contact counts as failure. Compared with a turn-based harness using the same agent and tools, TRIP halves the rate of human contact with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, without sacrificing task success (38.7% vs. 31.0%). Real-robot experiments show that TRIP completes two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.

## 16. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v2

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. ## 17. LLMs as MDP Designers: A Dependency-Aware Agentic Framework for Automated Robot Policy Generation

- 作者：Hong Je-Gal, Jinwoo Kim, Chanbin Yi, Hyun-Suk Lee

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-03T14:29:36Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04974v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04974v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. Rather than using large language models (LLMs) as execution-time decision makers, we use them as designers that construct RL pipelines before deployment, including Markov decision process (MDP) formulation and simulator implementation. We identify a phase-wise structure in this problem: MDP formulation is dependency-structured and sequential, whereas simulator implementation becomes modular once the formulation is fixed. Based on this structure, we propose ARPG, a dependency-aware agentic framework that performs sequential MDP modeling, localized verification and correction, and simulator-structured implementation while externalizing intermediate artifacts. ARPG targets a key failure mode of automated policy generation: executable code may still yield non-learnable policies when state, action, reward, and termination artifacts are not semantically aligned. ## 18. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 19. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints.
