## 1. ManiLadder: Benchmarking Robot Manipulation Through a Categorized and Multi-Difficulty Level Task Ladder

- 作者：Chongkai Gao, Jieao Shi, Zhaoyu Mu, Yudi Lin, Xuanwei Liu, Wei Ken Ng, Baiye Cheng, Anqi Chen, Kerun Liu, Zhuoran Li, Xuchuan Huang, Haoyang Li, Shengjia Zhu, Nga Teng Chan, Yuxuan Zhang, Xingyu Chen, Lin Shao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-12T06:42:31Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16374v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16374v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Recent advances in robotic manipulation have enabled increasingly complex and dexterous behaviors, yet existing benchmarks still focus on simple pick-and-place tasks and lack a systematic framework for measuring progress in robot learning along the axis of task difficulty. We present ManiLadder, a large-scale and organized simulation benchmark for robotic manipulation. ManiLadder contains 112 carefully designed tasks stratified into 4 difficulty levels, with 8 categories in each level, spanning rigid-body and deformable-object manipulation, single-arm and dual-arm settings, and gripper and dexterous-hand end-effectors. To calibrate difficulty, we fix the data amount the same for every task and use a unified flow- matching imitation learning policy under the same training and evaluation protocols, then use its evaluation success rate as an operational empirical difficulty signal. All tasks are iteratively refined until they fall into target difficulty ranges, which are then verified across different random seeds, policies, and agentic learning systems. We further analyze the task properties, compare different policy performances and training settings, analyze task transfer and data scaling behaviors, and conduct real-world experiments showing that the designed tasks preserve their difficulty ordering beyond simulation. ManiLad- der provides a calibrated ladder for measuring, comparing, and advancing robotic manipulation capabilities. More results are available at https://maniladder-benchmark.github.io/.

## 2. GAP: Generalized Adaptation Prior for Dexterous Piano Playing

- 作者：Jinyi Chen, Hongyi Li, Lei Jiang, Xianwei Liu, Yanhong Liang, Yulin Chen, Yucheng Tao, Yongbin Jin, Hongtao Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T09:24:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12111v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12111v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous hands enable versatile manipulation, but policies trained in simulation often degrade on hardware owing to non-idealities, and the high-dimensional action space makes real-world adaptation costly. Piano playing, which requires precise and coordinated multifinger motion, offers a representative testbed. We propose GAP (Generalized Adaptation Prior for Dexterous Piano Playing), a sample-efficient adaptation framework that learns how to adapt, rather than how to act, during simulation pretraining and transfers this adaptation prior to hardware. Between repeated executions, GAP uses feedback to adjust the actions of the next one: a gradient guidance network learns from paired executions how adjusting actions affects playing performance, and its gradients guide these adjustments. In simulation, GAP yields approximately 2.4 times the F1 scores improvement of the existing fast-adaptation baseline for dexterous piano playing under the same interaction budget. The learned prior also generalizes to musical pieces held out from pretraining. On a physical dexterous hand, the same simulation-pretrained prior improves F1 scores by 3.6%-23% across three pieces of varying difficulty after approximately 7 min of adaptation each. These results suggest a practical route to reduce the real-world interaction needed to deploy dexterous hands.

## 3. Fade to... Red? Scheduling Coloured Noise Exploration Supports Efficient Robot Learning with Reduced Movement Jerk

- 作者：Francisco M. López, Francisco Cruz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-09T04:44:59Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11868v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11868v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Exploration in deep reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control is commonly driven by temporally uncorrelated Gaussian (white) action noise, which leads to jerky motions with locally constrained exploration. In contrast, temporally correlated coloured noise, particularly pink noise, has been shown to support more efficient exploration in RL. Motivated by these findings, we inquire whether scheduling changes to the noise colour over the course of training can outperform white noise for continuous control robotics. We perform a systematic study of 49 linear schedules between white and red noise, together with generalised state-dependent exploration (gSDE), on four sparse-reward manipulation tasks with a Fetch arm and a Shadow Dexterous Hand. We find that the noise colour at the end of training does not affect learning efficiency or end performance, whereas fading towards red noise halves the jerk of exploration in every task. Balancing learning efficiency, end performance and smoothness, red noise emerges as the preferred strategy: it learns as efficiently as pink noise with almost 2-fold less movement jerk. We therefore recommend exploration schedulers that fade towards red noise as the default for off-policy robot learning.

## 4. DexRemo: A Force-Sensing Hand Platform and Task Suite for Contact-Rich Removal Manipulation

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15981v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Many manipulation tasks require separating adhered, embedded, contaminated, or mechanically constrained targets. We study this setting as removal manipulation, where progress depends on evolving contact states that are only partially observable from vision and kinematics. We introduce DexRemo, a three-finger robotic hand with high-force actuation, modular fingertips, and three-axis fingertip force sensing, together with a nine-scenario real-robot task suite spanning adhesive detachment, surface cleaning and self-cleaning, and occluded retrieval. ## 5. DexSeed: Expanding Human Hand-Object Interactions into Robot Demonstrations for Dexterous Policy Learning

- 作者：Huaijin Pi, Zehong Shen, Yong Wang, Kechun Xu, Changpeng Wang, Dingkun Wei, Xueyang Wang, Wenjia Wang, Xing Zhu, Yujun Shen, Taku Komura

- 来源：arXiv

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14092v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14092v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



We study how to generate diverse, physically executable robot demonstrations for dexterous vision-language-action (VLA) learning. Existing demonstration-generation methods typically rely on costly teleoperated examples. Meanwhile, human hand--object datasets provide a reusable source, but existing transfer methods mainly reproduce given interactions. Turning these interactions into VLA training data further requires coverage of different objects and manipulation locations, together with executable arm and finger actions, and visual observations. To achieve this, we present DexSeed, a framework for low-cost generation of dexterous robot data from human hand--object interactions. Our key idea is to expand human sequences while preserving their grasps and realizing the resulting motions under arm-tracking dynamics in physics simulation. Specifically, we organize source sequences by grasp mode with shared language descriptions. InteractionMesh-based retargeting and arm trajectory augmentation adapt these sequences to the robot hand, different object geometries, and pick-and-place locations while preserving local hand--object relationships. An RL finger-control policy produces executable rollouts, while the arm tracks precomputed trajectories. Rendered observations and recorded actions from successful rollouts are paired with grasp descriptions to fine-tune a VLA. Experiments show improved contact fidelity in retargeting and more reliable whole-arm execution than the evaluated baselines. Simulation evaluation and real-world deployment further demonstrate that a VLA fine-tuned solely on the generated demonstrations can execute language-specified grasps. Hand kinesthetic feedback is crucial for restoring and enhancing human perception of force and displacement in robotic hand teleoperation. Wide perception range of force feedback is vital in rendering objects of different stiffness, especially for high-stiffness rendering. This paper presents a hand exoskeleton system employing magnetorheological (MR) brakes to provide a wide range kinesthetic feedback during teleoperation. ## 7. LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning

- 作者：Xuheng Zhang, Yiming Zhang, Jialin Gao, Hao Chen, Jiaming Liu, Ji Li, Runzhong Huang, Yinxi Wang, Chenyang Gu, Zihan Yang, Yaowei Liu, Zhonghao Yan, Ziyu Guo, Renrui Zhang, Peng Jia, Pheng-Ann Heng, Shanghang Zhang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-10T13:15:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14048v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14048v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.

## 9. PCTac: Mechanochromic Photonic-Crystal Tactile Sensor for Dexterous Manipulation

- 分类：cs.RO



Modern visuotactile sensors primarily sense contact by imaging the deformation of a compliant interface. Using techniques such as photometric stereo, they can recover detailed contact geometry, often represented as surface normals or depth. However, force-related information typically still needs to be inferred indirectly through calibration, geometric reconstruction, or learned mappings. Mechanochromic photonic-crystal (PC) materials offer an alternative physical encoding mechanism: contact loading changes their periodic microstructure and spectral response, directly transforming local stress into a spatially resolved structural-color field. Despite this potential, PC materials have rarely been explored as visuotactile sensors for closed-loop robot learning. ## 15. What Can a VLA Learn from the Agent That Calls It? Task-Level Experience Distillation for Embodied Navigation

- 作者：Jinze Tao, Yuxiao Han, Kangkang Dong, Yancong Wei, Xiaojun Zhu, Bokui Chen, Houde Liu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-12T03:42:58Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16246v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16246v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Navigation agents use a large language model (LLM) planner to call a vision-language-action (VLA) model. Each episode needs the LLM, adding cost and delay. We distill successful agent experience into the VLA instead of distilling the planner. The VLA then navigates alone at test time. Agent sessions are not VLA demonstrations. They include motions driven by planner tools and sub-instructions, along with detours. We present H2E, a task-level experience distillation method that converts these sessions into VLA training data. On all 3,189 REVERIE-CE val-unseen episodes, H2E raises the success rate of an 8B VLA from 35.0% to 44.9%.

## 16. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

We investigate the automated generation of executable robot reinforcement learning (RL) policies from natural-language task descriptions. We identify a phase-wise structure in this problem: MDP formulation is dependency-structured and sequential, whereas simulator implementation becomes modular once the formulation is fixed. Based on this structure, we propose ARPG, a dependency-aware agentic framework that performs sequential MDP modeling, localized verification and correction, and simulator-structured implementation while externalizing intermediate artifacts. ARPG targets a key failure mode of automated policy generation: executable code may still yield non-learnable policies when state, action, reward, and termination artifacts are not semantically aligned. Experiments in Isaac Sim show that ARPG improves modeling correctness, environment executability, and policy-generation success compared with monolithic LLM baselines and off-the-shelf agentic systems. Additional demonstrations on custom robotics, standard RL, and engineering-domain MDPs further suggest that the same modeling pipeline can be reused beyond the main benchmark suite.

## 18. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 19. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.

## 20. DexUMI: Using Human Hand as the Universal Manipulation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Mengda Xu, Han Zhang, Yifan Hou, Zhenjia Xu, Linxi Fan, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2025-09-19T19:43:41Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.
