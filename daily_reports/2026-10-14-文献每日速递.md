## 1. EgoMatrix: Physically Faithful Dexterous Robot Demonstrations from Egocentric Human Videos

- 作者：Ziheng Wang, Yanming Shao, Ting Mao, Kaihan Chen, Yiqun Wang, Xuanye Wu, Yao Mu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T17:51:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18822v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18822v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Egocentric human videos offer a scalable alternative to robot teleoperation for collecting dexterous manipulation data. However, two challenges limit their use as reliable training data: accurate hand-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction from monocular RGB and high-quality robot demonstrations, especially visual observations. We present EgoMatrix, an end-to-end data engine that converts monocular egocentric human videos into bimanual dexterous trajectories and paired robot observation videos, with the trajectories directly replayable on real robots. It reconstructs HOI with emphasis on relative geometry and contact rather than exact metric scene recovery, then uses retargeting and reinforcement learning (RL) to reproduce the demonstrated tasks in simulation. We jointly render robot hands and objects from these simulated trajectories to produce physically faithful observations synchronized with state and control records. Rendering the same trajectories in diverse, task-compatible backgrounds expands paired training data while preserving robot-object motion and contact. Across TACO, OakInk2, and self-collected data, EgoMatrix achieves 54.84% end-to-end success in simulation versus 14.52% for the strongest baseline. Reconstruction reaches state-of-the-art performance on most metrics of the two public benchmarks. Qualitative results show more faithful hand-object interactions than prior methods, while feature-space analysis shows closer alignment with real-robot observations than GPT-6 Astra.Our project page is available at https://spikew726.github.io/EgoMatrix/.

## 2. EgoCPT: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from a Single Ego-Centric Video with Contact-Aware Optimization

- 作者：Zhixin Sun, Kairui Shi, Tianao Ren, Ming Yang, Mark Cutkosky, James Jingxi Xu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T01:45:01Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17572v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17572v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



While expert demonstrations on real robots are difficult to collect, human ego-centric videos are easy to collect and abundant on the internet. However, learning dexterous manipulation policies from human videos remains challenging---one of the most important challenges being the difficulty of reconstructing physically grounded robot trajectories due to the large embodiment gap. In this paper, we propose EgoCPT, a novel paradigm for learning high-quality retargeted dexterous hand trajectories directly from an ego-centric video, which can then be deployed directly on real robots. The only inputs to our method are an ego-centric RGB video and a reference hand pose trajectory, which can be easily obtained through off-the-shelf software or specialized cameras during recording. Our method is particularly robust to noisy hand poses attributed to our novel contact-aware optimization framework, which iteratively optimizes the contact points between the robot hands and objects. Across five manipulation tasks, our method achieves overall success rates of 67.3% on noisy EgoDex hand-pose reconstructions and 92.0% on high-quality TACO reconstructions collected using motion-capture hardware, outperforming multiple baselines on both benchmarks.

## 3. DexNinja: Learning Robust Dexterous Cutting Policy with a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Data Engine

- 作者：Haozhe Lou, Runyi Yang, Wanqi Zhong, Caiyun Liu, Yicheng Liu, Wenhao Liu, Wenlong Ma, Jiawei Xia, Kanzhi Wu, Danda Pani Paudel, Luc Van Gool, Hang Zhao, Yiming Li

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-12T17:59:51Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17208v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17208v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Cutting a piece of food looks effortless in human hands. For a robot, however, every cut is a high-contact interaction with a deformable, elasto-plastic object whose behavior changes with pose, contact mode, and cutting stage. Collecting real-world data is expensive and wasteful: each trial typically contaminates or damages the food, so obtaining scalable real world data for dexterous cutting is expensive and difficult. Simulation is an appealing alternative, but a large sim-to-real gap exists in both physics and perception. This gap is especially severe for topology-changing interactions like cutting, where small errors in contact, friction, or material and elasticity responses quickly compound. We propose \textsc{DexNinja}, a real2sim2real framework that turns a handful of real demonstrations into a dense and realistic training distribution. \textsc{DexNinja} (i) reconstructs object instances from real captures, (ii) randomizes physically meaningful parameters under category-level constraints, and (iii) produces augmented dexterous manipulation episodes using a custom differentiable simulator that couples robot dynamics, tactile contact, and deformable cutting. We evaluate \textsc{DexNinja} on a dexterous food-slicing task and show that (1) with tens of real demonstrations and hundreds of augmented simulator data, the success rate could reach more than 60\%. (2) the augmented data consistently improves success rates when only using real data with more than 20\% via sim-to-real, and (3) the resulting policies generalize to out-of-distribution objects with varying shapes and sizes.

## 4. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

## 5. In-Context Guidance: Learning Inter-Task Synergies via Numerical Foundational Models for Few-Shot Multitask Optimization

- 作者：Tingyang Wei, Haofeng Wu, Jiao Liu, Zhao Wei, Puay Siew Tan, Yew-Soon Ong

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-09-22T08:01:54Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25836v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25836v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.NE



Multi-task optimization (MTO) addresses a set of optimization tasks simultaneously, often suffering from inaccurate inter-task relationship estimation under limited evaluation budgets, leading to negative transfer. This paper introduces In-Context Guidance Multitask Optimization (ICG-MTO), a novel framework that leverages numerical foundational models to improve inter-task coupling estimation in few-shot scenarios. Unlike conventional methods that rely solely on scarce observed data, ICG-MTO employs a frozen foundational model to infer auxiliary guidance through in-context learning. The framework operates through three stages: constructing an algorithm-specific in-context query from evaluated solutions, using the foundational model to infer a guidance signal characterizing predictive relationships among tasks, and translating this signal into algorithm-specific guidance for maximum-a-posteriori coupling estimation. This approach provides regularization during the early, data-scarce stages of optimization and gradually relinquishes control as task-specific observations accumulate. We instantiate the framework in multitask Bayesian optimization as ICG-MTBO, using directional fitness-class queries to guide inter-task coupling estimation, and further instantiate it in MFEA-II using decision-space-overlap queries to guide random mating probability estimation. Experiments across synthetic benchmarks and a real-world robot arm control problem, together with evaluations under different acquisition functions and evolutionary multitasking, demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of ICG-MTO for few-shot multitask optimization.

## 7. RESTA: Time-Scaling Augmentation for Data-Efficient Robot Imitation Learning

- 作者：Yunpeng Jiang, Jianshu Hu, Han Fang, Zhiyuan Guan, Paul Weng, Yutong Ban

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T11:07:43Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18163v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18163v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Robot manipulation policies trained through imitation learning commonly rely on costly demonstrations recorded in one task direction and at one temporal resolution. We propose Reversal and Stride-based Trajectory Augmentation (RESTA), which exploits time-scaling data augmentation to improve data efficiency through trajectory reversal and temporal resampling. Trajectory-reversal augmentation reverses and relabels demonstrations between paired tasks with opposite objectives, reusing recorded rollouts without additional environment interaction. Because contact-rich manipulation is not exactly reversible, we treat reversal as an approximate paired-task prior rather than a physical symmetry. Temporal-stride augmentation varies observation and action sampling strides and conditions the policy on the resulting offsets, enabling fixed or randomized temporal schedules. Experiments across 16 paired bimanual manipulation tasks show that trajectory reversal is particularly effective in the low-data regime and that temporal-stride augmentation improves success under randomized temporal schedules. With 25 and 50 independently collected demonstrations per task, RESTA achieves 78.4% and 85.2% average success, respectively, matching or exceeding baselines trained with twice as many demonstrations (77.3% and 85.4%). These results show that RESTA can improve data efficiency in robot imitation learning.

## 9. ChunkAct: Action Chunking for Agentic Robotics

- 作者：Bingxin Xu, Wenrui Bao, Xinxin Liu, Jiawei Zhou, Yuzhang Shang, Emilio Ferrara

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T17:59:57Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18874v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18874v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CV



Agentic robotics places a large language model (LLM) agent in the robot control loop, where it composes perception, motion, and vision-language-action (VLA) tools under the guidance of a memory and thereby generalizes to new tasks without retraining. This generality comes at a steep price in efficiency. Every primitive requires a round of LLM reasoning over the full interaction history, so the cost of an episode grows with the number of primitives. We find this reasoning largely redundant: once a sub-goal is chosen, its primitives follow a routine already prescribed by memory. We present ChunkAct, which introduces action chunking to agentic robotics. Online, a single call launches a chunk of chained primitives, which executes autonomously and returns control only upon failure. Offline, ChunkAct compiles the memory into these chunks, so execution is memory-free at test time and the planner is reserved for decisions that require reasoning. LLM computation therefore scales with the number of decisions rather than the number of primitives. On LIBERO-Pro, ChunkAct improves task success by 5.0 points over the state of the art in agentic robotics while requiring 2.9x fewer LLM calls, 4.4x fewer input tokens, and 2.3x fewer output tokens.

## 13. Reinforcement Learning from Real-Robot Interaction without High-Quality State Estimates

- 作者：Ryan Gao, Ashvin Anilkumar, Nicholas Corrado, William Cong, Josiah Hanna

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-02T21:59:06Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14042v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14042v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A longstanding goal for robot reinforcement learning (RL) is that robots learn from their own physical experience, as humans and animals do. A key obstacle to learning directly from physical experience, especially outside instrumented lab environments, is that the state of the world is usually unobservable to the learner and must be estimated from on-board sensing. Errors in that estimate corrupt both the policy input and potentially the reward signal. We study this challenge using the contact-rich task of a bipedal robot learning to score soccer goals. We estimate all key state variables on-board with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) and no external instrumentation (e.g., motion capture or overhead cameras). Because the UKF may incorrectly estimate state when the ball is unobserved, we learn a new state representation with a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) that, critically, only corrects the RSSM's latent state when the ball is observed. Building on RLPD, we introduce RSSM-RLPD and apply it to the task of a NAO robot learning to dribble a soccer ball into a goal. We show that the robot learns to score from multiple starting positions after 30 minutes of real-world interaction, reaching 100% success against 0% for a feed-forward policy under the same training budget. We complement our physical robot experiments with a simulation study and show that RSSM-RLPD learns policies that achieve a higher success rate than behavior cloning, a feed-forward, and GRU baseline. Taken together, our results show that the addition of a learned state representation to existing on-robot learning methods enables RL from real-robot data even with a high degree of partial observability.

## 14. Skill Composition for Legged Robot Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Daniel Gigliotti, Flavio Maiorana, Fabio Patrizi, Luca Iocchi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-09-13T16:32:17Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14647v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14647v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Robots, and humanoid robots in particular, are increasingly competent at individual behaviors, each obtained by training a specialized controller. A specialized skill is quick to train, converges reliably because the problem it faces is narrow, and can be validated on its own, none of which is true of a single end-to-end policy asked to cover everything. What remains fragile is the transition between them. We argue that the composition of independent sub-policies deserves to be treated as a research problem in its own right, rather than as an implementation detail left to whatever mechanism happens to be at hand. Reliable composition is what turns a collection of separate skills into a repertoire that can be used, extended and shared. More fundamentally, if control can be passed between specialized policies safely, and at any moment, the choice of what the robot should do next can be delegated to a component of an entirely different nature, such as a planner, an automaton or a symbolic controller, whose behavior can be inspected in advance. The policies would then only ever have to act, and what the robot can be trusted to do would become verifiable.

## 17. DexStitch: Learning and Composing Dexterous Skills from Human Videos via Contact-Aware Robotic Inpainting

- 作者：Byeonggyeol Choi, Seongmin Jung, Unghui Nam, Youngbo Song, Sunhong Min, MinHyung Lee, Byonghyo Shim, Yongseok Lee

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T06:18:15Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17789v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17789v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



A long-horizon manipulation task consists of primitive skills and the transitions connecting them. Learning skills that can be executed in sequence requires supervision for both, which is costly to collect on a robot. Human videos offer a scalable source of demonstrations, but transferring them to robots requires extracting robot-compatible observations and actions together with their skill structure. We present DexStitch, a framework that cuts long-horizon human videos into skills and transitions, converts them into aligned robot observation-action-skill data, and stitches the learned skills into a requested sequence. A skill classifier first segments the videos into manipulation skills and transitions. DexStitch then converts the human demonstrations into robot observations and actions while retaining their corresponding skill labels. To stitch the learned skills into a sequence, a shared skill-conditioned policy is pretrained on the resulting synthetic data and finetuned on a mixture of synthetic data and a small set of single-skill teleoperation episodes. Conditioning on the current and next skills guides each transition toward the following skill, while predicted skill progression enables closed-loop switching. On a held-out session, the classifier agrees with manual annotations on 92.4% of frames. Across four household skills, DexStitch achieves 80.0% single-skill success and 77.5% sequence success.

## 18. OATH: Object-Agnostic Tracking for Dexterous Hands, Enabling Rapid Physics-based Retargeting

- 作者：Solvin Sigurdson, Prasanna Sriganesh, Juan Borbon, Joel Burdick, Dingqi Zhang, Jiuguang Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-09T22:55:25Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14379v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14379v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Retargeting human demonstrations into dynamically feasible dexterous hand trajectories requires resolving complex contact physics, yet current methods either omit physical constraints or rely on computationally expensive sampling-based optimization in simulation. Reinforcement learning has previously been used to achieve planning and control through contact dynamics; however, standard policies require object-specific training and fail to generalize across arbitrary geometries. This work reframes physics-based retargeting as a residual learning problem, where we demonstrate that zero-shot generalization across objects relies on structured state representations. Specifically, formulating the observation space to include a localized contact-error signal guiding each robot link to its target coordinate on the object surface enables a single, unified residual policy to successfully grasp novel, unseen object geometries. Rolling out this policy in simulation on a kinematic reference trajectory yields a physics-based retargeter, OATH. Operating at 0.4 seconds of compute per second of reference, OATH achieves 2.4x faster-than-real-time performance and a 60-350x reduction in computational cost compared to sampling-based methods. The system recovers 58% of held-out and 54% of unseen-object DexYCB trajectories while maintaining a sub-2 cm tracking error.

## 19. PACT: Posture-Aligned Co-Design Technique for Hand Configuration and Retargeting in Multi-Finger Imitation Learning

- 作者：Koyo Sasazaki, Satoshi Funabashi, Pranav Ponnivalavan, Alexander Schmitz, Tetsuya Ogata, Shigeki Sugano

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T14:16:45Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18443v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18443v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



The fidelity of teleoperated demonstrations used for imitation learning is determined by how faithfully a robot hand can replicate the operator's posture. The posture retargeting law that the robot will execute is typically selected only once the hardware has been finalized, and conventional hand designs often neglect human-like grasping postures. We introduce PACT, an optimization based on human grasping postures that jointly optimizes the mechanical parameters of a 17-servo robot hand (link lengths, joint offsets, mounting angles, and base placements) and its nonlinear posture retargeter (a linear human-to-robot joint map, per-joint posture-IK weights, and damping). The optimization enforces full-finger correspondence (MCP, PIP, DIP, and tip of each finger) to human poses from the DexYCB dataset, and is constrained by servo interference, assembly clearances, and flat-hand protrusion, so the resulting design can be physically assembled. We fabricate the PACT Hand and validate it through teleoperation experiments and an imitation-learning demonstration. The hand reproduces all human hand keypoints with a kinematic RMSE of 3.9 mm, compared to 12.8 mm for the LEAP Hand baseline, and keeps all fingertips within 10 mm on every grasping posture, while the baseline achieves this for only 21.6% of the poses. Under matched per-joint torque limits, it lifts twice the baseline payload, 2 kg against 1 kg, and deforms a held paper cup seven times less, reaching a deformation level comparable to a human hand. It also achieves an 86% grasping success rate on YCB daily objects, compared to 64% for the baseline. Finally, as a demonstration, an imitation-learning policy trained on demonstrations collected with the PACT Hand achieved a 75% success rate at held-out placements in a can pick-and-place task. ## 20. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2008-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.
