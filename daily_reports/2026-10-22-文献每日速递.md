## 1. PHD-Hand: A Printable Humanoid Dexterous Hand with Linkage-Driven 3-DOA Digits

- 作者：Neel Adwani, Muhaiminul Islam Akash, Saffia Ouali, Ethan Liu, Cong Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-21T01:01:57Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29030v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29030v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Currently, dexterous humanoid robot hands are widely considered the final hardware bottleneck that hampers generalist robots from physically assisting people at home and work. As conventional tendon-driven designs suffer from high cost, demanding maintenance, poor reliability, and short durability, alternative linkage-driven designs have increasingly gained attention. Despite their potential to avoid the weaknesses of tendon-driven hands, integrating multiple degrees of actuation (DOA) in a single digit with linkage drivetrains is a challenge for design and fabrication. Only a few studies have managed to present functional humanoid hands that are completely linkage-driven and have at least three DOAs per digit without compromising actuator placement and other design preferences. Our work introduces a new linkage-driven hand featuring a unique flexure hinge. Together with elastic drive rods made of spring steel, the linkage drivetrains are significantly simplified. Unlike other linkage-driven designs of similar dexterity, our design does not require many miniature moving parts. As a result, the hand can be fabricated easily with a budget 3D printer. This not only significantly lowers the barrier to entry for using and developing dexterous humanoid robot hand functions, but also allows the hands to be sized much closer to regular human hands. The design offers desirable kinematic advantages as well. A prototype is evaluated for grasping and object manipulation. In addition, this paper presents our latest findings on kinematics and control of the hand.

## 2. COWBOY: A Scalable Sim-to-Real Framework for Learning Contextual Whole-Body Manipulation

- 作者：Jiahui Yang, Jason Jingzhou Liu, Ray Muxin Liu, Andrew E. Wang, Kenneth Shaw, Deepak Pathak, Ruslan Salakhutdinov

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T21:59:10Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28948v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28948v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Mobile manipulators must adapt not only to objects, but also to the spatial context in which they appear: where to position the base, how to manipulate, and where to look while navigating surrounding scene geometry. To address this, we present COWBOY, a scalable sim-to-real framework for learning such contextual whole-body manipulation. On whole-body dexterous grasping, COWBOY transfers zero-shot to hundreds of objects and diverse in-the-wild scenes, spanning cluttered tabletops, shelves, drawers, and other unstructured settings. Rather than learning scene adaptation directly with RL in simulation, a vectorized environment-aware whole-body controller (WBC) handles reaching in thousands of scenes by coordinating base-arm motion, collision avoidance, and active vision. Then, local RL experts are trained in local scenes for object interaction and can be reused across thousands of full-scene layouts. We distill these composed WBC-RL teachers into a single vision policy that controls the mobile base, arm, dexterous hand, and actuated camera from point-cloud observations. The resulting policy exhibits contextual whole-body behavior, automatically adapting base placement, camera viewpoint, arm trajectory, and grasp strategy to observed scene and object geometry. ## 3. Plausible Future, Wrong Choice: Steering World-Action Models toward Intended Future

- 作者：Zhuohao Li, Jiawei He, Yuxuan Yan, Lang Zhou, Yinghao Li, Yanrui Li, Tianyu Zhang, Yi-Yang Song, Xirui Wu andYi-Lin Wei, Fating Hong, Qing Zhang, Zhilin Zhao, Jingdong Wang, Wei-Shi Zheng

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T15:58:45Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28431v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28431v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for embodied generalist policies by jointly modeling future world evolution and robot actions across diverse manipulation tasks. However, representing diverse futures is only the first step: a capable WAM must also generate futures that match the requirements of the current task and execution progress. In long-horizon manipulation where similar observation-action segments occur across different tasks and stages, existing approaches may generate locally plausible yet misdirected rollouts to drift toward wrong task stage, i.e., a phenomenon we term Stage Confusion. To address this problem, we propose TaskWAM, a closed-loop, stage-aware framework that represents fine-grained task requirements with Mode Tokens and tracks execution progress with State Tokens, forming a structured task state to steer world--action generation toward intended future. We further introduce StageBench, a controlled multi-stage manipulation benchmark with parameterized stage variations and automated evaluation.Extensive experiments on both simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks demonstrate that TaskWAM effectively reduces confusion and achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art methods. Moreover, TaskWAM provides strong plug-and-play transfer across heterogeneous WAM architectures and robot embodiments, including single-arm robots, dual-arm grippers, dexterous hands, and humanoid robots.

## 4. Touch-JEPA: Tactile Representation Learning from Hand-Object Video with Limited Tactile Supervision

- 作者：Minseong Kweon, Qiao Feng, Yiming Huang, Lingjie Liu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T16:56:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28519v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28519v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Understanding contact and tactile magnitude in hand-object interaction is a key step toward physically intelligent systems, yet learning such representations is limited by the cost and restricted coverage of tactile measurements. We present Touch-JEPA, a framework for learning tactile representations from hand-object videos with limited tactile supervision. We first introduce a self-supervised pretraining objective that learns from video alone without tactile supervision. Using video-derived hand geometry, Touch-JEPA predicts masked visual features at corresponding hand-surface locations from spatial and temporal context, encouraging the representation to capture latent contact dynamics. We then extend this formulation to joint representation learning, where masked prediction is applied to all videos while tactile supervision is available only for a labeled subset, grounding the shared representation in measured contact and magnitude. With self-supervised pretraining alone, simple supervised readouts on frozen Touch-JEPA features outperform supervised baselines in contact discrimination and tactile magnitude estimation across three human-hand benchmarks, and transfer to robotic hands without robot tactile training data. Joint learning with limited tactile supervision further improves contact and magnitude estimation on the human-hand benchmarks. Together, these results show that hand-object video can provide scalable self-supervision for tactile representation learning, while limited tactile measurements provide complementary physical grounding.

## 5. What Novice Operators See Changes What the Policy Learns: A User Study of Keypoint Hand Visual Representation for Teleoperated Demonstration

- 作者：Amane Yamaguchi, Shohei Kamiguchi, Masashi Hamaya, Shigeo Yoshida, Takuji Narumi, Mai Nishimura

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T09:22:21Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27907v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27907v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.HC



Imitation learning increasingly relies on novice operators, whose demonstrations fall short of experts' in number and inter-trial consistency. Operators act on what they are shown, yet how the rendering of the robot affects their demonstrations, and the policies trained on them, has not been examined. We study this question with PointHand, a teleoperation interface that renders a multi-fingered robot hand as sparse keypoints at its fingertips and joints, the same keypoints a policy can observe. In a simulated mug-in-box task, 18 novices and 4 experts collected 2,200 trials under three renderings: the real shape of the hand, all keypoints emphasized, or the thumb and index finger alone emphasized. The novices shown the thumb and index finger emphasized collected 441 successful demonstrations from 600 trials, against 320 for those shown the real shape. We then trained diffusion policies on these demonstrations, replacing the scene point cloud and proprioceptive state they observe with the hand keypoints and two object keypoints. Within 400 steps these demonstrations went from the weakest of the three novice conditions under the point-cloud policy (46.3%) to the strongest under the keypoint policy (57.3%), a gain of +11.0 percentage points in all five seeds. Those collected with all keypoints emphasized changed by at most +0.7 percentage points, and in no more than three seeds. The value of a demonstration therefore depends on the rendering shown during collection and on what the policy observes together. A shared representation can pay off on both sides, in the number of successful demonstrations and in the success rate of the policy trained on them. Project page: https://omron-sinicx.github.io/point-hand/

## 6. T-WLA: Bridging Slow Vision and Fast Touch

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28814v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



In contact-rich manipulation, contact conditions can change before a visual policy updates its action plan. Recomputing the plan through the visual policy adds latency, while tactile feedback alone does not specify how the ongoing motion should be revised for the task. We introduce T-WLA, a world-language-action architecture that uses shared visual memory to guide both action generation and tactile correction. At each visual update, the visual branch integrates the current image, task instruction, and retrieved history into a visual memory that conditions base-action generation. The same memory is cached for a lightweight tactile editor, which combines it with the active action chunk and incoming touch to predict residual corrections to unexecuted actions without rerunning the visual branch. - 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Tactile representation learning for dexterous manipulation often requires substantial robot-side data collection, limiting its scalability across different hands and sensors. We explore whether large-scale human hand-object interaction (HOI) data can provide a useful prior for robotic tactile learning. To this end, we propose HuTact, a framework that pretrains tactile-aware hand representations from human HOI data without requiring real tactile signals during pretraining. HuTact transfers the interaction structure learned from human hand motions to robotic tactile observations and can be further adapted with a small amount of unlabeled robot interaction data. ## 8. EmbodyHOI: Embodiment-Aware Generation of Hand-Object Interactions for Humanoid Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Chaitanya Chawla, Kartik Sharma, Ananya Bal, Laszlo A. Jeni, Guanya Shi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-19T20:08:32Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27242v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27242v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Humanoid dexterous manipulation needs reference motions that are both semantically rich and kinematically feasible for the target robot. Text-conditioned hand-object interaction (HOI) generators supply diverse, language-specified demonstrations, but they synthesize motion in human (MANO) space. The embodiment gap is therefore deferred to post-hoc retargeting, where trajectories deployed on the robot can fail to maintain contact with objects and also degrade policy learning. Our key idea is that robot reachability should constrain the interaction trajectories while they are being generated. We present EmbodyHOI, which runs a differentiable inverse-kinematics (IK) solver for a specific humanoid embodiment inside the sampling loop of a frozen flow-matching HOI trajectory generator. We feed back gradients from the embodiment-aware IK solver into the HOI sampler and jointly optimize the position of the humanoid with respect to the object. Because guidance acts only at inference, one HOI trajectory generator trained on human hand poses transfers across humanoid embodiments without retraining. On ARCTIC trajectories with two contrasting humanoids (Unitree G1 and H1-2), our proposed guidance-based method cuts the retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while leaving text-to-HOI generation quality intact, and raises task success from 68% to 79% on 50 random G1 tasks and from 62% to 76% on a four-task H1-2 suite. Critically, cheaper alternatives for bridging the embodiment gap fail to beat EmbodyHOI. Post-hoc wrist refinement and compute-matched best-of-N selection both reach lower IK energy than our guidance yet train worse policies. For simulation results, see our website: https://embodyhoi.github.io/

## 9. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

## 10. In-Context Guidance: Learning Inter-Task Synergies via Numerical Foundational Models for Few-Shot Multitask Optimization

- 作者：Tingyang Wei, Haofeng Wu, Jiao Liu, Zhao Wei, Puay Siew Tan, Yew-Soon Ong

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-09-22T08:01:54Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25836v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25836v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.NE



Multi-task optimization (MTO) addresses a set of optimization tasks simultaneously, often suffering from inaccurate inter-task relationship estimation under limited evaluation budgets, leading to negative transfer. This paper introduces In-Context Guidance Multitask Optimization (ICG-MTO), a novel framework that leverages numerical foundational models to improve inter-task coupling estimation in few-shot scenarios. Unlike conventional methods that rely solely on scarce observed data, ICG-MTO employs a frozen foundational model to infer auxiliary guidance through in-context learning. The framework operates through three stages: constructing an algorithm-specific in-context query from evaluated solutions, using the foundational model to infer a guidance signal characterizing predictive relationships among tasks, and translating this signal into algorithm-specific guidance for maximum-a-posteriori coupling estimation. This approach provides regularization during the early, data-scarce stages of optimization and gradually relinquishes control as task-specific observations accumulate. We instantiate the framework in multitask Bayesian optimization as ICG-MTBO, using directional fitness-class queries to guide inter-task coupling estimation, and further instantiate it in MFEA-II using decision-space-overlap queries to guide random mating probability estimation. Experiments across synthetic benchmarks and a real-world robot arm control problem, together with evaluations under different acquisition functions and evolutionary multitasking, demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of ICG-MTO for few-shot multitask optimization.

## 12. AWLS: Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering for One-Step Generative Robotic Manipulation Policies

- 作者：Jiahong Tang, Jiaju He, Zhiyuan Yang, Ruyang Han, Haiyue Zhu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-20T06:58:12Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27736v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27736v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Human-collected real-world robotic manipulation demonstrations often contain mixed-quality trajectories, including hesitant, indirect, or inefficient action segments. While diffusion- and flow-based policies can model such multimodal data, standard behavior cloning (BC) assigns equal likelihood to all demonstrated actions and may therefore reproduce suboptimal modes. Offline RL value estimates offer a natural signal for biasing policies toward higher-value behavioral modes, but directly fine-tuning the generative policy or using critic gradients for policy steering can amplify critic errors and induce prior drift. We propose Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering (AWLS), an offline post-training method for improving frozen one-step generative manipulation policies without updating their decoders. AWLS learns a residual noise shaper, an observation-conditioned residual correction to the policy's latent noise, and trains it with an advantage-weighted action reconstruction objective. The advantages are detached, scale-normalized, and used only as supervised weights on demonstrated actions, avoiding critic-gradient policy updates while preserving fast single-step inference. We further introduce a rollout-free decoder-sensitivity criterion for selecting pretrained checkpoints that remain responsive to latent-noise perturbations. Experiments on simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks with clean and mixed-quality demonstrations show that AWLS improves over the frozen base policy while limiting drift from the pretrained behavior prior. ## 13. ALIGN: Aligning Condition Estimation and Expression in Advantage-Conditioned Behavior Cloning

- 作者：Zechen Wang, Zhenqi Qiu, Houlong Xiong, Suohang Zhang, Yiyu Ren, Wanting Xu, Hongfei Niu, Chengyang He, Ge Sun, Ran Cheng, Qian Zhu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-19T14:10:26Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26455v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26455v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Human-collected robot manipulation data often mix successful behavior with retries, pauses, recoveries, and failures. Advantage-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (AC-BC) can exploit such mixed-quality data, but only if the conditioning signal is both accurately constructed from offline trajectories and effectively incorporated into the policy's action-vector-field prediction. Existing pipelines can weaken this signal by thresholding estimator-derived advantage scores into noisy conditions, and by routing conditions through prompts that weakly affect the action vector field. We introduce ALIGN, a condition-aligned AC-BC framework for vision-language-action policies. ALIGN directly predicts categorical pairwise conditions in the policy-facing label space and injects them into a DiT action expert through AdaRMS modulation fused with flow time. We evaluate ALIGN on deliberately mixed real-robot datasets for ARX X5 bowl stacking and Franka key hanging, each combining standard demonstrations with retry-heavy trajectories. ALIGN improves condition fidelity, strengthens conditional vector-field separability, and achieves 90.0% and 56.7% closed-loop success, compared with 56.7% and 33.3% for the strongest AC-BC baseline. These results indicate that aligning conditions from estimation to action prediction is an effective way to extract robust manipulation policies from data in which success, recovery and error coexist.

## 14. AtomicRL: Atomic Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

Reinforcement learning (RL) offers an effective avenue for further improving VLA policies. However, existing VLA-RL methods typically suffer from delayed and biased credit assignment due to trajectory-level sparse rewards, while imbalanced subtask mastery creates bottlenecks that limit overall performance. To address these challenges, we propose AtomicRL, an RL post-training framework that leverages atomic task structures for reliable credit assignment and adaptive exploration. These results demonstrate that AtomicRL effectively improves the learning efficiency and manipulation performance of VLA policies on long-horizon tasks.

## 16. GeoMem-VLA: Internalizing 3D Scene Understanding into Temporal Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models

- 作者：Qinfan Zhang, Yifan Luo, Haoyuan Shi, Guanlin Liu, Han Dong, Haozhe Shan, Yong Dai, Xiangcong Ren, Xiaozhu Ju

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29866v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Geometric structure is essential for robotic manipulation and understanding the 3D world. Most VLA models rely on 2D representations of current observations, while geometry-aware approaches often require additional 3D information. However, geometry inferred independently from individual observations is often discarded rather than accumulated into a persistent task representation. Our key insight is that action generation needs a compact, task-relevant geometric abstraction that can be continuously updated, rather than a complete, renderable 3D reconstruction at every step. We introduce GeoMem-VLA, a VLA framework that internalizes explicit 3D supervision into dedicated geometric tokens and maintains them as temporal memory for action generation, without directly aligning the VLM's native visual embeddings with the teacher feature space. After benchmark-specific downstream adaptation, GeoMem-VLA achieves 98.9% average success on LIBERO and 91.10% / 91.24% on RoboTwin's clean/randomized settings. Across five real-world tasks, GeoMem-VLA achieves 78.0% average success. These results suggest that internalizing and remembering 3D scene understanding is a promising approach to robot control.

Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

## 18. Evo-RL: Iterative Robot Reinforcement Learning from Selective Experience

- 作者：Kai Ye, Yifan Jin, Qianhang Luo, Hongru Zhang, Jiedong Zhang, Shuyuan Hu, Song-Yuan Wu, Jihai Xu, Jie Zhao, Bo Zhao

- 发布时间：2026-10-09T10:19:54Z

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13345v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Existing real-world reinforcement learning methods improve robot policies by combining human corrections with autonomous rollouts. However, accumulated experience varies in its value for policy improvement, and even successful trajectories can contain inefficient or redundant behavior, motivating more selective experience reuse. To this end, we propose Evo-RL, an iterative reinforcement learning method that combines human interventions with advantage-guided experience selection. Evo-RL uses human corrections and experience with positive estimated advantages from successful trajectories to iteratively refine the policy. On a real-world high-precision manipulation task, Evo-RL improves attempt success rate over RECAP by 30.46\%. Our code, model checkpoints, and dataset are publicly available.

## 19. Reinforcement Learning from Real-Robot Interaction without High-Quality State Estimates

- 作者：Ryan Gao, Ashvin Anilkumar, Nicholas Corrado, William Cong, Josiah Hanna

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-02T21:59:06Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14042v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14042v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A longstanding goal for robot reinforcement learning (RL) is that robots learn from their own physical experience, as humans and animals do. A key obstacle to learning directly from physical experience, especially outside instrumented lab environments, is that the state of the world is usually unobservable to the learner and must be estimated from on-board sensing. Errors in that estimate corrupt both the policy input and potentially the reward signal. We study this challenge using the contact-rich task of a bipedal robot learning to score soccer goals. We estimate all key state variables on-board with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) and no external instrumentation (e.g., motion capture or overhead cameras). Because the UKF may incorrectly estimate state when the ball is unobserved, we learn a new state representation with a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) that, critically, only corrects the RSSM's latent state when the ball is observed. Building on RLPD, we introduce RSSM-RLPD and apply it to the task of a NAO robot learning to dribble a soccer ball into a goal. We show that the robot learns to score from multiple starting positions after 30 minutes of real-world interaction, reaching 100% success against 0% for a feed-forward policy under the same training budget. We complement our physical robot experiments with a simulation study and show that RSSM-RLPD learns policies that achieve a higher success rate than behavior cloning, a feed-forward, and GRU baseline. Taken together, our results show that the addition of a learned state representation to existing on-robot learning methods enables RL from real-robot data even with a high degree of partial observability.

## 20. vLLM-Omni Technical Report: A Unified Serving Runtime for Omni-Modality Generation

- 作者：vLLM-Omni Team

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-07T02:04:28Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09307v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09307v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.DC



Interaction with intelligent systems is expanding beyond text-centric chatbots and coding agents. Speech-native assistants, visual generation and editing, world-model environments, and robot action loops require models that emit text, audio, images, video, and actions. These models differ in execution pattern: multi-stage autoregressive omni and TTS pipelines, iterative diffusion or flow-matching generators, and longer-lived world-model or robot loops that carry state across steps. As a result, serving is no longer a single text decode loop, but a heterogeneous multi-stage workflow with cross-stage transfer, streaming, and session-shaped interaction.   Existing inference stacks are typically optimized for one architecture family. LLM servers deepen autoregressive scheduling and KV management, while diffusion stacks deepen denoising and parallel generation. Neither provides a shared control plane for pipelines that emit speech, pixels, or actions through separate generators, so production deployments often fall back to ad-hoc composition across disjoint runtimes.   We present vLLM-Omni, a unified serving runtime for omni-modality generation. vLLM-Omni organizes each workload as a multi-stage pipeline under a single orchestrator that admits requests, advances them across stages, and demultiplexes streaming outputs. Specialized engines and stage replicas provide compute; a connector carries heavy payloads on the data plane; and session-oriented control supports long-lived duplex, world-model, and robot workloads.   This report covers the architecture (stage-level KV paths, replica pools, multi-hardware platforms, and efficiency stack) and OpenAI-compatible and OpenPI APIs for omni, TTS, image/video, world-model, robot, and duplex workloads. We evaluate on the multimodal nightly CI on H100 (TTS and MiniCPM-o on H200), focused on Qwen3-Omni.
