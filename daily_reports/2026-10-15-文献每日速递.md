## 1. KinoGen: Customizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation References via Kinematically Grounded Video Generation

- 作者：Qi Wu, Lulu Zhao, Sanghyun Hahn, Rishi Yennu, Emmy Yang, Dingqi Zhang, Tao Pang, Jiuguang Wang, Kuan Fang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T17:43:32Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20552v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20552v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



We present KinoGen, a framework that synthesizes customizable humanoid loco-manipulation references through kinematically grounded video generation. KinoGen conditions a pretrained video generator on calibrated renders of the target robot and object in a simulated task instance and recovers the reference by fitting the same models to the generated two-view video, so that the reference lies in the robot configuration space by construction without a human body proxy or retargeting. Body, hand, and object priors initialize the reconstruction, and direct optimization against cross-view evidence refines it. Since an edited keyframe is simply another rendered state, a user can prescribe a goal object pose, regenerate the video around the edited keyframe, and obtain a reference that satisfies the pose exactly. Across eight loco-manipulation tasks, KinoGen yields lower penetration and foot skating than video-based baselines and preserves over 90% of annotated contacts, and its customized references satisfy every prescribed target with substantially less penetration than direct interpolation. Tracking policies trained on the synthesized references are deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid with dexterous hands. Project webpage: https://kinogen-humanoid.github.io/.

## 2. HiFun: a Hierarchical Framework for Efficient Functional Dexterous Manipulation Learning

- 作者：Linyi Huang, Guowei Huai, Weibin Liu, Shulong Jiang, Ping Tan, Weixuan Zhang, Hui Zhang, Jie Song

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T03:37:15Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19518v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19518v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



While multi-fingered dexterous robot hands have the potential to provide human-level dexterity, using them for functional manipulation remains difficult because task success depends on precise contact, coordinated arm-hand motion, and reactive recovery from execution errors. Precise contact depends on complex contact dynamics that are difficult to model accurately in simulation, motivating real-world learning. However, real-world demonstrations in the full arm-hand control space are costly and noisy because each trajectory must coordinate arm motion with high-DoF multi-finger contact. Small execution deviations can further cause slipped contact or premature force application, making recovery from off-nominal contact states important for robust execution. To deal with these challenges, we propose HiFun, a hierarchical real-world RL framework for functional dexterous manipulation tasks. Our key insight is to decouple the control complexity into a functional hand skill for precise contact execution and an arm-hand coordination policy for arm control and hand-skill activation. This design enables effective real-time human intervention for reactive recovery learning and efficient data collection with Dynamic Movement Primitive (DMP) augmentation. Across six real-world tasks on two dexterous hands, HiFun achieves 98.3% average success within one hour of online training and remains robust to dynamic disturbances. The project page is available at https://hly-123.github.io/HiFun/.

## 3. DexMimic: A Generalist Dexterous Hand-Object Tracking Policy via Value-Guided Reset Allocation

- 作者：Zhexi Luo, Zhixuan Xu, Rui Yang, Zeyu Xiao, Yiwen Hou, Lin Shao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T19:29:33Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19089v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19089v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Achieving human-like dexterous manipulation remains a central challenge in robotics. Learning from human hand-object interaction (HOI) datasets offers a promising pathway to diverse dexterous skills, yet existing physics-based approaches train specialist policies for individual trajectories, thereby limiting skill reuse at scale. We introduce DexMimic, a generalist dexterous hand-object tracking policy jointly trained across diverse HOI trajectories. Efficient joint training requires allocating each rollout to both an undertrained phase and a starting state that supports continued tracking. DexMimic realizes this insight through Value-Guided Reset Allocation, which adapts the initialization distribution online to prioritize difficult phases and select reset states with higher expected tracking returns. We further introduce a benchmark spanning training, fine-tuning, and generalization. Experiments show improved tracking success and training efficiency, faster fine-tuning, and the potential for zero-shot generalization to unseen trajectories and objects; larger training sets further improve efficiency and generalization. Finally, we validate DexMimic on a real-world dexterous manipulation platform.

## 4. EgoMatrix: Physically Faithful Dexterous Robot Demonstrations from Egocentric Human Videos

- 作者：Ziheng Wang, Yanming Shao, Ting Mao, Kaihan Chen, Yiqun Wang, Xuanye Wu, Yao Mu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T17:51:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18822v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18822v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Egocentric human videos offer a scalable alternative to robot teleoperation for collecting dexterous manipulation data. However, two challenges limit their use as reliable training data: accurate hand-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction from monocular RGB and high-quality robot demonstrations, especially visual observations. We present EgoMatrix, an end-to-end data engine that converts monocular egocentric human videos into bimanual dexterous trajectories and paired robot observation videos, with the trajectories directly replayable on real robots. It reconstructs HOI with emphasis on relative geometry and contact rather than exact metric scene recovery, then uses retargeting and reinforcement learning (RL) to reproduce the demonstrated tasks in simulation. We jointly render robot hands and objects from these simulated trajectories to produce physically faithful observations synchronized with state and control records. Rendering the same trajectories in diverse, task-compatible backgrounds expands paired training data while preserving robot-object motion and contact. Across TACO, OakInk2, and self-collected data, EgoMatrix achieves 54.84% end-to-end success in simulation versus 14.52% for the strongest baseline. Reconstruction reaches state-of-the-art performance on most metrics of the two public benchmarks. Qualitative results show more faithful hand-object interactions than prior methods, while feature-space analysis shows closer alignment with real-robot observations than GPT-6 Astra. Our project page is available at https://spikew726.github.io/EgoMatrix/.

## 5. PACT: Posture-Aligned Co-Design Technique for Hand Configuration and Retargeting in Multi-Finger Imitation Learning

- 作者：Koyo Sasazaki, Satoshi Funabashi, Pranav Ponnivalavan, Alexander Schmitz, Tetsuya Ogata, Shigeki Sugano

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-13T14:16:45Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18443v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18443v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



The fidelity of teleoperated demonstrations used for imitation learning is determined by how faithfully a robot hand can replicate the operator's posture. The posture retargeting law that the robot will execute is typically selected only once the hardware has been finalized, and conventional hand designs often neglect human-like grasping postures. We introduce PACT, an optimization based on human grasping postures that jointly optimizes the mechanical parameters of a 17-servo robot hand (link lengths, joint offsets, mounting angles, and base placements) and its nonlinear posture retargeter (a linear human-to-robot joint map, per-joint posture-IK weights, and damping). The optimization enforces full-finger correspondence (MCP, PIP, DIP, and tip of each finger) to human poses from the DexYCB dataset, and is constrained by servo interference, assembly clearances, and flat-hand protrusion, so the resulting design can be physically assembled. We fabricate the PACT Hand and validate it through teleoperation experiments and an imitation-learning demonstration. The hand reproduces all human hand keypoints with a kinematic RMSE of 3.9 mm, compared to 12.8 mm for the LEAP Hand baseline, and keeps all fingertips within 10 mm on every grasping posture, while the baseline achieves this for only 21.6% of the poses. Under matched per-joint torque limits, it lifts twice the baseline payload, 2 kg against 1 kg, and deforms a held paper cup seven times less, reaching a deformation level comparable to a human hand. It also achieves an 86% grasping success rate on YCB daily objects, compared to 64% for the baseline. Finally, as a demonstration, an imitation-learning policy trained on demonstrations collected with the PACT Hand achieved a 75% success rate at held-out placements in a can pick-and-place task. Project page: https://pact-hand-2027.github.io

## 7. Coordi-pi: Coordination-Aware Dual-Hand Action Representation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Yiming Meng, Yanming Shao, Weijie Shen, Kunyang Lin, Jiayu Sun, Ping Luo, Yao Mu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T08:08:45Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19753v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19753v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general-purpose robotic manipulation, but extending them to bimanual dexterous manipulation introduces a high-dimensional and structured action space. Existing bimanual VLA policies commonly concatenate right- and left-side arm--hand actions at each control timestep, leaving cross-hand interactions implicit within a single action vector. We introduce Coordi-$π$, a structured action representation for bimanual dexterous manipulation. Coordi-$π$ represents the right- and left-side arm--hand actions as individual action tokens and organizes them into an interleaved sequence, explicitly exposing the two sides within the action sequence. To explicitly encode hand identity and temporal synchronization in this interleaved representation, a learnable hand-identity embedding distinguishes the two sides, while sequence--control-timestep factorized rotary positional encoding (RoPE) separates sequence position from control timestep, allowing actions at the same control timestep to share a temporal coordinate while retaining distinct sequence positions. Across all five bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in DexJoCo, Coordi-$π$ achieves state-of-the-art performance under both \textit{rand-obj} and \textit{rand-full}, improving the average success rate over $π_{0.5}$ from 31.9\% to 51.3\% and from 18.3\% to 32.3\%, respectively. Coordi-$π$ also consistently outperforms $π_{0.5}$ across four real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks.

## 8. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

## 9. In-Context Guidance: Learning Inter-Task Synergies via Numerical Foundational Models for Few-Shot Multitask Optimization

- 作者：Tingyang Wei, Haofeng Wu, Jiao Liu, Zhao Wei, Puay Siew Tan, Yew-Soon Ong

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-09-22T08:01:54Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25836v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25836v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.NE



Multi-task optimization (MTO) addresses a set of optimization tasks simultaneously, often suffering from inaccurate inter-task relationship estimation under limited evaluation budgets, leading to negative transfer. This paper introduces In-Context Guidance Multitask Optimization (ICG-MTO), a novel framework that leverages numerical foundational models to improve inter-task coupling estimation in few-shot scenarios. Unlike conventional methods that rely solely on scarce observed data, ICG-MTO employs a frozen foundational model to infer auxiliary guidance through in-context learning. The framework operates through three stages: constructing an algorithm-specific in-context query from evaluated solutions, using the foundational model to infer a guidance signal characterizing predictive relationships among tasks, and translating this signal into algorithm-specific guidance for maximum-a-posteriori coupling estimation. This approach provides regularization during the early, data-scarce stages of optimization and gradually relinquishes control as task-specific observations accumulate. We instantiate the framework in multitask Bayesian optimization as ICG-MTBO, using directional fitness-class queries to guide inter-task coupling estimation, and further instantiate it in MFEA-II using decision-space-overlap queries to guide random mating probability estimation. Experiments across synthetic benchmarks and a real-world robot arm control problem, together with evaluations under different acquisition functions and evolutionary multitasking, demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of ICG-MTO for few-shot multitask optimization.

## 11. Refine Actions If Needed: Adaptive Action Chunk Refinement for Robot Manipulation

- 作者：Hanlin Liu, Zichen Zhou, Jiacheng Zhang, Liuyuan Na, Jin Jin, Guangyan Chen, Yufeng Yue, Gen Li, Fangqiang Ding

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T08:17:57Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19765v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19765v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Computationally lightweight manipulation policies predict executable actions with low inference latency, but prediction errors can limit task success. Iterative generative policies can achieve higher task success rates, but repeated network evaluations increase inference latency. Refining action proposals from computationally lightweight policies can combine these strengths, but the amount of refinement needed varies across states. We introduce RAIN (Refine Actions If Needed), a framework that predicts action errors at different refinement levels to decide whether and how much to refine each executable proposal, before performing any refinement. We train an ensemble to predict these errors by comparing action proposals and their refined versions with expert actions from offline demonstrations, without requiring explicit labels of refinement needs. Calibration balances uncertainty, computation, and temporal consistency. We evaluate RAIN on eight ManiSkill tasks and four real-robot tasks. In simulation, our policy achieves 66.6% success, approaching the best evaluated diffusion-policy success rate with 5.71 times lower policy latency. On the real robot, success increases from 55.0% with Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) to 72.5%, with comparable average successful completion time. Matched controls show that dynamic routing maintains the strongest observed fixed-refinement success level with 26.6% fewer refiner evaluations. Project page: https://rain-policy.github.io/.

## 12. Benchmarking Interaction-Oriented Aerial Mobility across Heterogeneous Environments

Aerial mobile manipulation requires unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to traverse heterogeneous environments such as indoor and outdoor domains, and connect navigation with subsequent physical interaction within the same trajectories. Existing aerial benchmarks are typically confined to single-environment settings, limiting evaluation of agents across continuous environmental transitions. When interaction is evaluated, agents are often initialized near the target, leaving the transition from navigation to interaction largely unassessed. This work introduces CrossFly, a benchmark for long-horizon interaction-oriented aerial mobility across heterogeneous environments. CrossFly contains 15K trajectories across more than 20 reconstructed scenes under a unified simulation and evaluation framework, covering Indoor, Outdoor, and Indoor$\leftrightarrow$Outdoor trajectories. A downstream manipulation case study further validates the relevance of interaction-oriented arrival to subsequent physical interaction. We hope CrossFly helps advance aerial embodied intelligence toward unified mobility and physical interaction in heterogeneous environments.

## 13. SafeDriveVLA: Navigation-Conditioned World Model Dreaming for Conflict-Aware End-to-End Autonomous Driving

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are gaining attention in autonomous driving, inheriting commonsense priors from large language models (LLMs) and widening the navigation interface from a fixed command set to free-form natural-language instructions. ## 14. Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation

- 作者：Xiaoman Li, Zhiqiang Yang, Jiayu Hu, Jipeng Kong, Yang Zhang, Jingwei Zhang, Fangzheng Yan, Chunhua Shen, Chenjia Bai

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T17:09:36Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20504v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20504v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. New rollouts are incorporated by appending them to the graph and rerunning search, without training a reward model, action-conditioned critic, or progress predictor. Across four precise manipulation tasks and three VLA backbones, GR-BC improves single-round supervised fine-tuning and reaches 91% success after a few offline iterations, without trajectory comparisons or progress labels. More details and videos are available at: https://gr-bc.github.io

## 15. AdaRSI: On-Demand Recursive Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents

- 作者：Peidong Wang, Ying Chang, Zhiming Ma, Xiaocui Yang, Shi Feng, Daqi Liu, Yuzhe Yang, Xu Cao, Chuang Wang, Jian Su, Daling Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T16:51:09Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20474v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20474v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CL, cs.RO



Pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) often fail midway through unseen long-horizon tasks. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) offers a remedy, but embodied RSI methods either refine the harness around a frozen executor and so cannot add a missing motion, or train the executor on persistent failures without asking whether a failure lies in how the executor is called or in what it can do. We ask when a self-improving embodied system should change the weights of its executor, and propose AdaRSI, an on-demand RSI framework for embodied agents. A coding agent first improves its skills and harness, then attributes persistent failures by retrying failing sub-steps from saved states, and fine-tunes only the sub-steps that remain unreliable, with demonstrations it collects itself and experience carried over from earlier tasks. Compared with fine-tuning every sub-step, it reaches slightly higher success while using only 28% of the GPU time and 22% of the self-collected demonstrations, and fine-tuning without a harness trains more yet gains far less.

## 16. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

- 作者：Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T14:25:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20255v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14042v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A longstanding goal for robot reinforcement learning (RL) is that robots learn from their own physical experience, as humans and animals do. A key obstacle to learning directly from physical experience, especially outside instrumented lab environments, is that the state of the world is usually unobservable to the learner and must be estimated from on-board sensing. Errors in that estimate corrupt both the policy input and potentially the reward signal. We study this challenge using the contact-rich task of a bipedal robot learning to score soccer goals. We estimate all key state variables on-board with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) and no external instrumentation (e.g., motion capture or overhead cameras). Because the UKF may incorrectly estimate state when the ball is unobserved, we learn a new state representation with a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) that, critically, only corrects the RSSM's latent state when the ball is observed. Building on RLPD, we introduce RSSM-RLPD and apply it to the task of a NAO robot learning to dribble a soccer ball into a goal. We show that the robot learns to score from multiple starting positions after 30 minutes of real-world interaction, reaching 100% success against 0% for a feed-forward policy under the same training budget. We complement our physical robot experiments with a simulation study and show that RSSM-RLPD learns policies that achieve a higher success rate than behavior cloning, a feed-forward, and GRU baseline. Taken together, our results show that the addition of a learned state representation to existing on-robot learning methods enables RL from real-robot data even with a high degree of partial observability.

## 19. vLLM-Omni Technical Report: A Unified Serving Runtime for Omni-Modality Generation

- 作者：vLLM-Omni Team

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-07T02:04:28Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09307v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09307v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.DC



Interaction with intelligent systems is expanding beyond text-centric chatbots and coding agents. Speech-native assistants, visual generation and editing, world-model environments, and robot action loops require models that emit text, audio, images, video, and actions. These models differ in execution pattern: multi-stage autoregressive omni and TTS pipelines, iterative diffusion or flow-matching generators, and longer-lived world-model or robot loops that carry state across steps. As a result, serving is no longer a single text decode loop, but a heterogeneous multi-stage workflow with cross-stage transfer, streaming, and session-shaped interaction.   Existing inference stacks are typically optimized for one architecture family. LLM servers deepen autoregressive scheduling and KV management, while diffusion stacks deepen denoising and parallel generation. Neither provides a shared control plane for pipelines that emit speech, pixels, or actions through separate generators, so production deployments often fall back to ad-hoc composition across disjoint runtimes.   We present vLLM-Omni, a unified serving runtime for omni-modality generation. vLLM-Omni organizes each workload as a multi-stage pipeline under a single orchestrator that admits requests, advances them across stages, and demultiplexes streaming outputs. Specialized engines and stage replicas provide compute; a connector carries heavy payloads on the data plane; and session-oriented control supports long-lived duplex, world-model, and robot workloads.   This report covers the architecture (stage-level KV paths, replica pools, multi-hardware platforms, and efficiency stack) and OpenAI-compatible and OpenPI APIs for omni, TTS, image/video, world-model, robot, and duplex workloads. We evaluate on the multimodal nightly CI on H100 (TTS and MiniCPM-o on H200), focused on Qwen3-Omni.

## 20. LongLive-Plug: Once-for-All Distillation for Video Generation

- 作者：Shuai Yang, Luozhou Wang, Wei Huang, ZhiFei Chen, Bohan Zhang, Xiao Fu, Qianli Ma, Chen-Hsuan Lin, Weian Mao, Bryan Chu, Song Han, Yukang Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-09-29T17:58:49Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38154v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38154v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Video diffusion models are increasingly developed into specialized models for diverse downstream tasks, and this development often includes a distillation stage, for example to accelerate sampling or to improve long-video generation. This stage is typically repeated for every specialized model. We introduce LongLive-Plug, a once-for-all distillation framework that learns reusable capabilities as LoRAs on a base model for training-free, plug-and-play deployment to compatible downstream models. These capabilities include single-pass classifier-free guidance, few-step sampling, and long-context error correction for autoregressive generation. The adapters remain reusable even when downstream models add conditioning branches, expand output channels. Despite training at a fixed guidance scale, our dedicated CFG LoRA provides text guidance control through its inference weight. Combining it with a few-step LoRA simultaneously preserves few-step generation and CFG controllability on downstream tasks. We verify training-free deployment on 54 downstream models across three backbone families and eight task categories, including world modeling, robotics, editing, and multimodal generation. The approach may support additional compatible models. Each capability can thus be distilled once per backbone family and reused without per-target retraining.
