## 1. Catch It All!: Generalizable Dynamic Catching with a Dexterous Hand

- 作者：Arth Shukla, Kehlani Fay, Huaze Liu, Darin Djapri, Alexiy Buynitsky, Nicklas Hansen

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39044v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Catching arbitrary objects requires generalization to novel projectiles and real-time adaptation to unpredictable contact dynamics under noisy visual observations. This paper presents Catch It All!, a sim-to-real catching framework for humanoid robots that generalizes and adapts to unseen objects with variable physical properties for underhand and overhand catches, using only one egocentric camera. We learn policies which generalize to unseen objects with varying geometry, deformability, elasticity, and physical properties. Effective grasps which adapt real-time to object poses and shapes are learned through careful grasp reward design. With only one egocentric camera, Catch It All! successfully performs underhand and overhand catches on 9 unseen objects not seen during training, including low-rebound plushes, semi-deformable toys and chip bags, and rigid-elastic objects with high rebound on contact.

## 3. A fully articulated humanoid dexterous hand with human-level joint structure enabled by linkage-based actuation

- 作者：Jihao Li, Jianguo Wang, Haotian Guo, Yanzhe Wang, Guodong Lu, Huixu Dong

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-27T07:53:51Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36789v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36789v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Humanoid dexterous hands are a core component of embodied intelligence, enabling robots to translate high-level intent into rich and adaptive physical interaction. However, existing designs remain constrained by a persistent trade-off among anthropomorphic kinematic complexity, mechanical robustness, load capacity and control simplicity, limiting dexterous manipulation in unstructured environments. Here we present Torch-Hand, a fully articulated humanoid dexterous hand that replicates the joint structure and motion hierarchy of the human hand with 21 degrees of freedom (DOF), comprising a highly articulated five-DOF thumb and four DOF in each of the remaining fingers. The differentiated thumb integrates multi-axis CMC motion to provide broad opposition and flexible contact orientation relative to the four fingers. Unlike tendon-driven or hybrid actuation approaches, Torch-Hand adopts an entirely linkage-based transmission architecture. By integrating screw-linkage actuation, the hand achieves high grasping force and substantial payload capacity while maintaining a compact anthropomorphic form. Its actuation architecture is optimized for control tractability, with all actuators integrated within the palm and arranged in a serial-robot-like configuration that establishes a direct correspondence between motor motion and joint kinematics. This architecture mechanically decouples functional DOF to reduce unintended inter-joint motion propagation, while allowing independently addressable DOF to be actively coordinated into coupled multi-joint motion patterns during complex manipulation. Comprehensive experiments assess reachable workspace, coordinated fingertip trajectories, thumb opposition, 33-grasp taxonomy coverage, grasping force, payload capacity and task-level manipulation. These results establish Torch-Hand as a robust hardware foundation for dexterous embodied manipulation.

## 5. From Teleoperation to Long-Horizon Dexterity: Evaluating Visuomotor Skills and Offline Proxies for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Ammar N. Abbas, Aayush Jain, Prashanth Viswanath, Nivesh Gadipudi, Giulia Vilone, Jim Gibbons

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T18:07:37Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40828v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40828v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG, eess.SY



Imitation learning policies are intended to make robotic manipulation robust to variations in object pose, appearance, and environment that challenge pre-programmed motion. However, real-world performance remains difficult to predict, as conventional optimization signals such as validation loss show no consistent relationship with robot success. To identify the design choices that govern real-world performance, 281 imitation-learning policies spanning seven policy families were evaluated in approximately 1,700 real-robot trials of RJ45 connector grasping and insertion using a five-fingered robotic hand. Controlled ablations showed that observation and action representations dominated conventional optimizer and sampling choices. For point-cloud policies, region-of-interest cropping, representation in the robot-base frame, and normalization within the point encoder each improved success by 70-100 percentage points. For the vision-language-action policy, absolute rather than relative action prediction improved insertion success by up to 70 percentage points. Skill decomposition, multi-view perception, and recovery demonstrations were associated with the strongest-performing systems, although their effects were not independently isolated. Of 67 offline metrics evaluated, only five measures of predicted-motion smoothness and directness reliably correlated with insertion success (r = -0.70 to -0.80), and only within a single policy family. These results identify representation choices as key determinants of real-world imitation-learning performance and show that offline metrics can shortlist policies, but cannot replace real-robot evaluation.

## 8. How Long, Not How Close: A Learned Temporal Metric for Planning in Latent World Models

- 作者：Lama Moukheiber, Haotian Xue, Yongxin Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-04T06:15:22Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04988v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04988v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.LG



Latent world models plan by rolling a frozen predictor forward under candidate action sequences and ranking the candidates by the latent distance between their imagined end state and the goal. However, this ranking breaks down when the goal lies several plans away, because the latent distance measures how closely an end state resembles the goal rather than how far it remains from reaching it. To address this, we propose TEMPO, a temporal-distance planning objective that leaves the world model untouched, learns only from the recorded trajectories already used to train it, and adds negligible cost to the planner's search. TEMPO learns a small map of the frozen latent in which the distance between two states of an episode reflects the number of environment steps between them, and blends this distance into the planner's cost. It requires no rewards, policies or success labels and, being a cost rather than a model, applies to frozen world models with one latent vector per state that plan by a latent distance. We evaluate TEMPO on eleven simulated environments (e.g., maze navigation, tabletop pushing, robotic arm control and three-dimensional manipulation) with the LeWM and PLDM planners. With a small MLP that adds at most 0.3% to a plan's arithmetic, TEMPO improves both planners at every goal distance, including the one-plan setting of their evaluations, raises LeWM from 36% to 99% on TwoRoom three plans from the goal, and remains competitive on a broad range of 2D and 3D navigation, reaching and manipulation tasks.

## 12. When the Simulator Fails, Not the Policy: Auditing Contact Artifacts in Simulated Robot Benchmarks

- 作者：Alex Zhou, Junhao Wu, Xueyang Zhou, Yixiao Huo, Hongcheng Gao, Di Zhu, Zhenghao Zhang, Yongchao Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-30T15:13:11Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41857v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41857v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Simulation benchmarks score robot policies by success rate, and every failed episode counts against the policy. Many of these failures are the simulator's fault, not the policy's; we find the cause and fix it. Testing a vision language action model and a world action model in simulation, we find that the policy often places the object and the simulator then ejects it, and that the benchmark's own scripted demonstrations fail the same way. We present SimuGuard, an auditing plugin for LIBERO, RoboTwin, RoboCasa and RoboDojo. It leaves the evaluation unchanged. At every physics step, it records the state and flags an object that starts moving at a contact faster than the robot's contact or gravity account for. It replays a recorded episode bit for bit, and it reruns the episode with one simulation parameter changed. With it we trace these episodes on RoboTwin to the collision detection of the physics engine: for geometry one to two centimeters apart, the engine reports a penetration of about two centimeters, and the solver throws the object out at several meters per second. The cause is one constant: the distance algorithm discards triangles below a fixed area threshold, which valid triangles fall below at the centimeter scale. With that constant changed and nothing else, episodes with an artifact fall from 12 in 100 to none, and the success rate on five container tasks rises from 89% to 98%. Success rates in simulation thus count failures that the simulator causes. Benchmarks on PhysX 5.3.1 or earlier should rerun in a repaired engine. Where the engine cannot change, they should report the success rate together with the range it spans when every flagged episode fails or passes: 75.8% to 98.5% around a reported 83.1% for LingBot-VA.

## 13. VLA Policies Do Not Know When Not to Act: Unintended Task Execution and Start Suppression

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-30T11:45:50Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41609v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41609v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



A robot should act only on an instruction from a person or on an intent that the robot explicitly represents and generates. Unintended actions taken without such an instruction can cause risk or inconvenience to people and the environment. We examine how recent vision-language-action (VLA) policies respond when the instruction is missing or meaningless. We apply a paired protocol with empty, neutral, nonsense, infeasible, and wrong-task instructions to six model checkpoints in LIBERO-Goal simulation. Holding the initial state fixed and varying only the instruction, we ask three questions: whether the robot starts to move, which task it completes, and what the instruction determines. The fine-tuned policy never completed a task on non-task inputs, but it did not reliably keep the robot still, and it failed to suppress the start on infeasible instructions.

## 14. SACQ: Structure-Aware Codebook Quantization for Ultra-Low-Bit Vision-Language-Action Models

- 作者：Haesung Lee, Insu Han

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-30T08:19:50Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41408v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41408v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.RO



Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable capable robot behavior, but their large memory footprint limits deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. Weight quantization is a natural remedy, yet post-training quantization (PTQ) recipes inherited from large language models degrade when applied to VLAs at ultra-low precision. Existing low-bit PTQ methods often fail to explicitly account for the structure of downstream operations and allocate precision without considering its effect on the policy actions. We introduce SACQ, an ultra- low-bit weight-only vector-codebook quantization framework for VLAs. SACQ combines (1) structure-aware codebook fitting with role-specific quadratic metrics that reflect input statistics and downstream sensitivity; (2) action-aware mixed-bit allocation based on the increase in final-action error as individual layers are compressed; and (3) two-stage post-quantization refinement that reassigns codeword indices with second-order error compensation and calibrates codebooks and row-group scales against the decoded action. Using GR00T N1.7, SACQ achieves 93.85% average success across four LIBERO suites at 1.5 bits per language/action weight while reducing model memory by 4.83x. With INT8 activation quantization and low-precision attention, it retains 93.55% success while running 1.51x faster than BF16. On SimplER, SACQ further achieves 63.76% and 52.27% success under Visual Matching and Variant Aggregation at 2 bits per language/action weight, compared to 58.90% and 53.45% for BF16, respectively.

## 15. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 16. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

- 发布时间：2026-10-14T14:25:46Z

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

These models differ in execution pattern: multi-stage autoregressive omni and TTS pipelines, iterative diffusion or flow-matching generators, and longer-lived world-model or robot loops that carry state across steps. As a result, serving is no longer a single text decode loop, but a heterogeneous multi-stage workflow with cross-stage transfer, streaming, and session-shaped interaction.   Existing inference stacks are typically optimized for one architecture family. LLM servers deepen autoregressive scheduling and KV management, while diffusion stacks deepen denoising and parallel generation. Neither provides a shared control plane for pipelines that emit speech, pixels, or actions through separate generators, so production deployments often fall back to ad-hoc composition across disjoint runtimes.   We present vLLM-Omni, a unified serving runtime for omni-modality generation. vLLM-Omni organizes each workload as a multi-stage pipeline under a single orchestrator that admits requests, advances them across stages, and demultiplexes streaming outputs. Specialized engines and stage replicas provide compute; a connector carries heavy payloads on the data plane; and session-oriented control supports long-lived duplex, world-model, and robot workloads.   This report covers the architecture (stage-level KV paths, replica pools, multi-hardware platforms, and efficiency stack) and OpenAI-compatible and OpenPI APIs for omni, TTS, image/video, world-model, robot, and duplex workloads. We evaluate on the multimodal nightly CI on H100 (TTS and MiniCPM-o on H200), focused on Qwen3-Omni.

## 19. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2008-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.

## 20. DexUMI: Using Human Hand as the Universal Manipulation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Mengda Xu, Han Zhang, Yifan Hou, Zhenjia Xu, Linxi Fan, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2025-09-19T19:43:41Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.
