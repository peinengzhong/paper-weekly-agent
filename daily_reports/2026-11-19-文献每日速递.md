## 1. HandWorldBench: A Unified Benchmark for Hand-Centric World Models

- 作者：Zhaolu Wang, Jiacheng Xu, Lihong Wang, Shuqing Wang, Jianjie Fang, Ziyou Wang, Xvyuan Liu, Rongze Tang, Yiding Ma, Xin Zhang, Jiawei Chen, Wei Wu, Chen Gao, Yong Li, Zhibo Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T16:47:46Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24731v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24731v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.CV



Recent advances in world models are opening new possibilities for fine-grained embodied tasks, with hand-object interaction offering a particularly demanding setting. Modeling these interactions involves capturing how hand actions change objects and how the resulting states persist over time. Yet systematic evaluation tailored to these hand-centric capabilities remains underdeveloped. To address this gap, we introduce HandWorldBench, a unified benchmark that systematically evaluates hand-centric world models across human hands, simulated dexterous hands, and real dexterous hands along six dimensions: visual quality, hand fidelity, appearance consistency, instruction following, physical consistency, and temporal coherence. To construct the benchmark, we develop a data curation pipeline that extracts hand-object interaction clips and screens them for quality, yielding 20,500 high-quality egocentric clips from over 600 hours of footage. We then manually select 500 clips covering seven task families for evaluation. Benchmarking eleven models under a unified protocol reveals that stronger hand-pose control does not consistently translate into coherent object-state evolution and persistence or robust cross-embodiment performance, with several specialized hand-control models underperforming general-purpose text/image-to-video generators on dexterous-hand tasks.

## 2. Ego2X: Contact-aware Transfer from Egocentric Human Data to Any Robot

- 作者：Xinke Lyu, Bowen Yang, Zhicheng Wang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T09:48:30Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24138v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24138v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Embodied models depend on real interaction data, yet collecting it on physical robots is slow and labor-intensive. Recent ego-to-robot pipelines retarget first-person view human videos to robot data via transferring hand and arm motion. They judge fidelity mainly by visual appearance. However, trajectories often fail in terms of physics: fingers hover beside objects, and grippers close on empty space. We argue that how the object moves and the contacts that drive the motion count. Thus, we present Ego2X, an object-centric framework that converts monocular egocentric video into physically executable robot data across dexterous hands and parallel grippers. Ego2X centers on CORE (Contact-centric Object-interaction REpresentation), which records each contact on the object by its surface anchor, as well as relative force, grasp mode, and interaction state. Throughout the pipeline, CORE corrects hand-object drift via object-relative constraints, steers inverse kinematics toward contacts the robot must realize, and preserves held objects during arm erasure and robot compositing. On HOT3D, contact-driven correction reduces in-contact hand depth error by 33.7%, while on EgoDex, contact-driven retargeting realizes 62.9% of annotated contacts, surpassing the official HuRo pipeline (27.9%). In RoboTwin 2.0, post-training X-VLA on Ego2X episodes alone, without simulator demonstrations, raises task success rate from 40.5% zero-shot to 67.0% across 200 paired scenes. We will publicly release our conversion pipeline and CORE-annotated dataset for dexterous hands and parallel grippers.

## 3. 3DTouch: A Tactile-aware VLA Model with 3D Geometric Relations for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Xin Zhang, Shiqi Zhang, Siyuan Cao, Juncheng Cai, Yedong Shen, Yuxuan Gao, Dianlong Wen, Kaixue Long, Lu Zhang, Yao Li, Jiajun Deng, Yanyong Zhang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T06:51:53Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23961v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23961v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



In contact-rich dexterous manipulation, occlusion by the fingers and interacting objects obscures local geometry, preventing visual observations from fully capturing contact locations. Visually similar scenes may also differ subtly in the relative positions of interacting objects, leading to different contact states. We propose 3DTouch, a tactile-geometric conditioning approach for vision-language-action (VLA) models. 3DTouch constructs a shared 3D visuo-tactile representation by expressing RGB-D scene points and dense five-finger tactile measurements in the robot-base frame, preserving each taxel's spatial location and force vector. Hand-object and object-object relations derived from hand landmarks and pose-aligned reconstructed surfaces explicitly describe the relative arrangement of interacting entities. Together, these representations condition the VLA action expert on spatially localized contact forces and relative surface geometry, providing explicit cues for contact adjustment and object alignment. We evaluate 3DTouch on five real-world contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks using a UR7e robot arm equipped with an XHand dexterous hand. 3DTouch improves the average success rate by 42 percentage points over the corresponding VLA baseline and by 20 percentage points over the strongest evaluated alternative. Ablation studies reveal the complementary benefits of scene geometry and spatial tactile feedback, with further gains from shared spatial grounding and explicit geometric relations.

## 4. TapTacTouch: Shape Completion from Sparse Tactile Contacts with Geometric Priors

- 作者：Yoobin Shin, Jaeyoung Lee, Jieun Kang, Hyun Song, Woojin Kim, Andrew Jaeyong Choi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T10:42:58Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24207v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24207v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



3D object reconstruction is essential for robotic perception and manipulation, yet conventional approaches predominantly rely on visual observations that can be limited by occlusion or unavailable in visually constrained environments. In this work, we propose a vision-free, training-free, and category-agnostic framework for reconstructing 3D object meshes from sparse tactile contacts without relying on pretrained shape models. A 6-DoF robotic arm equipped with a 20-DoF multi-finger robotic hand collects tactile observations using 3x3 pressure sensor arrays mounted on the finger pads. The acquired tactile measurements are transformed into 3D contact constraints through robot kinematics and integrated into a multi-scale Gaussian Process Implicit Surface (GPIS). To address sparse and incomplete surface observations, the proposed method incorporates contact confidence weighting, weak pseudo-surface constraints, and an observation-driven geometric prior to preserve local surface geometry and provide structural guidance in unobserved regions. Real-world experiments on six objects achieve a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 1.610, an Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) of 7.738, and an F-Score of 95.00, with reconstruction completed in an average of 49.99 s per object. These results demonstrate that sparse tactile sensing can provide sufficient geometric information for continuous 3D object reconstruction without visual or semantic information.

## 5. Porosification of the dielectric layer in alternating-current electroluminescence devices enables high-resolution tactile imaging

- 作者：Mingwei Gu, Licheng Li, Zeyuan Zhou, Ying Yang, Zixiang Nie, Xingyu Ma, Junshuai Chen, Lin Zheng, Jinhui Gu, Jia-Sen Xie, Ziling Wei, Mengxing Huang, Jin Ge

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-14T12:47:44Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19384v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19384v1.pdf

- 分类：cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.optics



Vision-based tactile sensing enables high-resolution contact perception, yet bulky optical architectures consume valuable space and constrain the functional design of dexterous robotic hands. Here we introduce a self-emissive tactile imaging strategy based on alternating-current electroluminescence (ACEL) devices, in which a compressible porous dielectric encodes contact pressure into spatial emission patterns without external illumination or discrete sensing pixels. We identify a nonlinear capacitance-dependent emission window that provides a basis for tunable pressure-to-light conversion. Pore-size engineering further suppresses local emission deficiencies, reducing spatial intensity variation by nearly ninefold and enabling faithful imaging of contact features down to 100 μm. The continuous electroluminescent interface also reproduces complex contact patterns without computational reconstruction of electrical fields. Direct lamination of the ACEL device onto a complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) image sensor is expected to enable compact contact imaging in an assembly less than 5 mm thick, substantially thinner than conventional vision-based tactile sensors. This approach could reduce the space required for high-resolution tactile imaging, offering greater design freedom for dexterous robotic hands.

## 8. SHAPE: Discrete Supervision and Parallel Continuous Streams for Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Human Data

- 分类：cs.RO



Egocentric human recordings are far cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, promising stronger pretraining and direct skill learning without task-specific robot data. Yet humans and robots differ in morphology and kinematics, so human recordings lack the joint positions and actuator commands that supervise robot policies, providing only retargeted end-effector (EEF) actions. This raises two questions: how human data should supervise the policy, and how a dexterous robot should read out actions at deployment. ## 9. Towards Pose-Robust Few-Shot Vision-Language-Action Models

- 作者：Wenhao Li, Zhongyi Han, Yuhao Wei, Liang Han, Yilong Yin, Liqiang Nie

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T12:54:16Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24360v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24360v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV



Fine-tuning a vision-language-action (VLA) model on a few dozen demonstrations is the standard way to put a manipulation policy on a robot, yet the resulting policy silently assumes that the camera and the robot base stay exactly where they were during data collection: a bumped tripod or a base re-docked a few centimetres away makes it reach for the wrong place. We show that both failures are one phenomenon, a change of the camera-to-base extrinsic that the policy has memorised, and that the robot carries everything needed to undo it. We introduce WACO, a world-anchored canonicalization framework for VLA models: the arm, placed by forward kinematics at the measured joint angles, serves as a metric calibration target, and the static scene serves as the world anchor that tells a moved camera from a moved robot. From a single RGB-D frame, WACO estimates both shifts without ground-truth extrinsics or extra data, canonicalises the image to the calibration viewpoint and the state, action and start pose to the calibration base frame, and fine-tunes the unmodified policy under the artefacts of its own estimator. We also introduce LIBERO-PoseShift, the first benchmark for RGB VLAs with graded camera, base and compound shifts. Across LIBERO-PoseShift, the LIBERO-Plus camera track and four real-world tasks on a UR5e robot, WACO consistently outperforms the standard few-shot recipe and prior viewpoint-robust methods, raising shifted success by 13.2 points in simulation and by 36.7 points on the real robot, recovering 90% of the simulation gap to a ground-truth oracle and 87% of the real-robot gap, and establishing a new state of the art among methods that use no perturbed training data, with no test-time calibration. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/WACO/.

## 10. WAM-Policy: World Action Models with On-Policy Distillation for Quadrupedal Loco-Manipulation

- 作者：Junlin Long, Xu Deng, Yiran Wang, Yue Yang, Yang Zhao, Zeyu Zhang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T08:03:53Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24011v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24011v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV



Arm-free quadrupeds can perform loco-manipulation, walking on three legs while manipulating with a front leg, yet no existing policy for it follows language instructions. World action models (WAMs) have emerged as effective language-conditioned manipulation policies, but are pretrained for robot arms and sample slowly. WAM-Policy turns one WAM into a quadrupedal loco-manipulation policy via two stages. The first, Cross-Morphology Policy Adaptation and Acceleration, adapts the arm-centric WAM to quadrupeds and distills the transferred policy into a student with one video and two action steps by on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). This student, however, is bounded by its teacher, which only imitates demonstrations. The second stage, Task-Specific Improvement and Merging, therefore tunes the teacher by reinforcement learning (RL) and merges the slow specialists into one few-step student by multi-teacher on-policy distillation (M-OPD). On QLM-Bench, ten simulated tasks with 1,000 demonstrations synthesized without teleoperation, the transferred policy reaches 77.0%, and the OPSD student keeps 76.7% while sampling 14 times faster. Task-specific RL and M-OPD lift it to 85.0%. We also deploy the OPSD student on a real Unitree Go2 Air. WAM-Policy thus achieves a stronger quadrupedal policy at a fraction of the inference cost.

- 分类：cs.RO



Tactile feedback helps visual manipulation policies respond to local contact, but changes in tactile observations do not always require corrective motion, and sustained corrections do not always require new tactile features. We introduce TacPilot, a tactile residual framework that separates representation updates from action intervention around a frozen visual policy. TacPilot uses a perception gate to determine when tactile features need refreshing and updates only affected regions while reusing unchanged features. ## 15. Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

- 作者：Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-05T15:05:07Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08144v2

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08144v2.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.SE



Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it.   On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

## 17. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 18. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2008-01-01T00:00:00Z

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.

## 19. DexUMI: Using Human Hand as the Universal Manipulation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

- 作者：Mengda Xu, Han Zhang, Yifan Hou, Zhenjia Xu, Linxi Fan, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2025-09-19T19:43:41Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=EkigXMH9Ik

- 分类：CoRL 2025 Workshop Dexterous Manipulation Spotlight, Dexterous Manipulation, Learning from Human, Imitation Learning



We present DexUMI - a data collection and policy learning framework that uses the human hand as the natural interface to transfer dexterous manipulation skills to various robot hands. DexUMI incorporates hardware and software adaptations to minimize the embodiment gap between the human hand and various robot hands. The hardware adaptation bridges the kinematics gap with a wearable hand exoskeleton. It allows direct haptic feedback in manipulation data collection and adapts human motion to feasible robot hand motion. Our software adaptation bridges the visual gap by replacing the human hand in video data with high-fidelity robot hand inpainting. We demonstrate DexUMI's capabilities through comprehensive real-world experiments on two different dexterous robot hand hardware platforms, achieving an average task success rate of 86%.

## 20. Arm Robot: AR-Enhanced Embodied Control and Visualization for Intuitive Robot Arm Manipulation

- 作者：Siyou Pei, Alexander Chen, Ronak Kaoshik, Ruofei Du, Yang Zhang

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2024-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=iyTHgxFL7B

- 分类：CoRR 2024



Embodied interaction has been introduced to human-robot interaction (HRI) as a type of teleoperation, in which users control robot arms with bodily action via handheld controllers or haptic gloves. Embodied teleoperation has made robot control intuitive to non-technical users, but differences between humans' and robots' capabilities \eg ranges of motion and response time, remain challenging. In response, we present Arm Robot, an embodied robot arm teleoperation system that helps users tackle human-robot discrepancies. Specifically, Arm Robot (1) includes AR visualization as real-time feedback on temporal and spatial discrepancies, and (2) allows users to change observing perspectives and expand action space. We conducted a user study (N=18) to investigate the usability of the Arm Robot and learn how users perceive the embodiment. Our results show users could use Arm Robot's features to effectively control the robot arm, providing insights for continued work in embodied HRI.
