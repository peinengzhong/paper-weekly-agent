## 1. DexLatentWorld: 3D Cross-Embodiment Latent Action World Models for Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation

- 作者：Vinay Udit Mohan, Xuchuan Huang, Rolandos Alexandros Potamias

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T16:05:37Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30907v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30907v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG



World models facilitate planning and control in robotic manipulation by enabling agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions. However, they typically rely on action-labelled interaction data that is costly to acquire and difficult to scale. These models are also often tied to the specific embodiment they were trained on, limiting transferability to previously unseen robot morphologies. We introduce a 3D latent-action world model that learns continuous action representations directly from point-cloud observations of retargeted human manipulation demonstrations. A contrastive objective is used to encourage consistent representations of motion across embodiments. The resulting unified latent action space serves directly as the search space for model-predictive control, where sampled latent actions condition particle-based future predictions. Executable robot commands are then recovered from the predicted geometric motion without requiring an embodiment-specific action decoder. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that the learned latent actions exhibit controllable and compositional motion, transfer to unseen robot embodiments, and support zero-shot manipulation with unseen gripper geometries. Following task-agnostic adaptation, our model achieves 90%, 92%, and 60% success on reaching, grasping, and placing, respectively, compared with DexWM's reported 72%, 58%, and 28%. Finally, we demonstrate zero-shot real-world manipulation with an unseen dexterous hand, achieving an 86% success rate across 50 trials on five diverse tasks involving pushing, grasping, and lifting everyday objects. These results highlight the potential of geometry-grounded latent actions as a unified, transferable interface for world-model-based robotic manipulation. Project materials, including demonstration videos and source code, are available at https://vinaymohan0409.github.io/DexLatentWorld/.

## 2. FunGrasp-Zero: Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Policy for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Functional Grasping

- 作者：Yanhan Lin, Yuliang Wu, Yihao Guo, Wei-Jin Huang, Jianwen Li, Jiawei Tang, Xiaolong Huang, Yuquan Wang, Ancong Wu, Wei-Shi Zheng

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-22T03:39:52Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29195v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29195v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous functional grasping is critical for robotic tool use, yet current dexterous hands exhibit substantial morphological diversity. This hardware heterogeneity makes cross-embodiment functional grasping a fundamental but largely unexplored problem. Existing functional grasping methods use retargeted hand poses as guidance, tying task representation to a specific embodiment and failing to explicitly capture object-centric functional contact intent, limiting generalization across hands. To address this, we propose FunGrasp-Zero, a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping. Instead of relying on embodiment-specific joint configuration, FunGrasp-Zero captures functional intent via an object-centric, cross-embodiment-consistent contact map, adapted to heterogeneous hands through morphological mapping. We construct a hand-object potential field from mapped contact regions, yielding attractive and repulsive guidance vectors. A Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Network (FC-GAT) leverages these vectors to coordinate multi-finger motion across hands, while a potential field reward guides fingers toward target regions and penalizes non-target collisions to enforce functional alignment and stability. We further establish X-FunBench for cross-embodiment functional grasping, evaluating both physical grasp success and functional intent preservation across hands. Evaluated on X-FunBench, our unified policy, co-trained on four embodiments, achieves 86% average grasp success rate across five unseen hands on novel objects. Deployed on four unseen physical hands (Leap, Inspire, Sharpa, Wuji) for novel object grasping, the policy achieves 80% average success rate without fine-tuning. To our knowledge, FunGrasp-Zero is the first RL-based framework to achieve zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping across unseen hands in both simulation and the real world.

## 3. Task2Hand: A Contact-Grounded Skill for Parametric Dexterous-Hand Morphology Design

- 作者：Haoyang Wang, Weibo Gao, Zhengxiong Li, Lingfeng Tao

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-20T18:48:12Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27967v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27967v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Dexterous hand design demands repeated expert work across geometry, robot-description code, collision debugging, grasp initialization, and policy training. We present Task2Hand, a Skill: a specification package that turns a functional manipulation brief into a parameterized hand model and routes every candidate through cost-ordered contact, dynamics, and learning gates. Each gate returns a status and an evidence record; on failure, the Skill records the earliest blocker, revises one bounded parameter group, and reruns the cheaper gates. The agent generates, validates, and trains autonomously within declared bounds, and escalates trade-offs, plateaus, and conflicting evidence to a human who sets the next design direction. An evidence-driven design trajectory for bidirectional in-hand manipulation yielded SAGE Hand (Skill-Aware Generative Embodiment): four fingers, 16 actuated DoF, an integrated guiding palm, compact roots, cylindrical tips, and a collision-cleared opposable thumb. Under a matched training protocol, SAGE Hand reaches 45.6% task success on a hexagonal prism against 37.3% for the LEAP hand, and 70.8% on a bicolor cylinder against 51.6%. Dense simulated tactile input more than halves the cylinder drop rate. In a controlled iteration study, the Skill path preserves capture at 164/165 trials while the path without the Skill degrades to 14/165, confirming that the gates act as a regression guard during refinement. Accepted candidates pack with URDF/MJCF, metrics, checkpoints, and a provenance manifest.

## 4. DexDuet: Learning Contact-Preserving Bimanual Dexterity from Human Demonstrations

- 作者：Haoran Zhang, Haokai Ding, Han Zhou, Zuxing Lu, Heng Zhang, Xingxing Zuo

- 来源：arXiv

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30495v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



Human demonstrations provide a rich source of bimanual dexterous skills, yet transferring them to robot hands remains challenging. Existing methods primarily optimize object trajectory tracking, often overlooking how human hands interact with the object. Even with accurate tracking, robots may contact incorrect object parts or slide their fingers, resulting in unstable manipulation. We present DexDuet, a framework that learns contact-preserving bimanual dexterity from a single human demonstration. DexDuet employs two interaction rewards to preserve human hand-object configurations and contacts. To enable coordinated manipulation, we factorize the bimanual policy into two weight-sharing per-hand policies that condition on each other's states. Across four benchmarks spanning three simulators and five robot embodiments, DexDuet improves object-tracking ADD-AUC by 11 to 41 points over the corresponding baselines while consistently achieving better contact preservation. Notably, among 122 paired runs with comparable object-tracking accuracy, DexDuet preserves the human's contacts better in 117 cases. Code and videos are available at https://rcl-robotics.github.io/Dexduet.io/

## 6. TapTacTouch: Shape Completion from Sparse Tactile Contacts with Geometric Priors

- 作者：Yoobin Shin, Jaeyoung Lee, Jieun Kang, Hyun Song, Woojin Kim, Andrew Jaeyong Choi

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-18T10:42:58Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24207v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24207v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO



3D object reconstruction is essential for robotic perception and manipulation, yet conventional approaches predominantly rely on visual observations that can be limited by occlusion or unavailable in visually constrained environments. In this work, we propose a vision-free, training-free, and category-agnostic framework for reconstructing 3D object meshes from sparse tactile contacts without relying on pretrained shape models. A 6-DoF robotic arm equipped with a 20-DoF multi-finger robotic hand collects tactile observations using 3x3 pressure sensor arrays mounted on the finger pads. The acquired tactile measurements are transformed into 3D contact constraints through robot kinematics and integrated into a multi-scale Gaussian Process Implicit Surface (GPIS). These results demonstrate that sparse tactile sensing can provide sufficient geometric information for continuous 3D object reconstruction without visual or semantic information.

## 7. OVOGO: Offline Value-Guided On-Policy Generative Optimization for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models

- 作者：Rongzheng Yang, Jiaming Guo, Yuxuan Guo, Zihao Zhang, Yunkai Gao, Siming Lan, Shaohui Peng, Rui Zhang, Tianyun Ma, Xing Hu, Yunji Chen

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T11:04:04Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30477v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30477v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.AI



Flow-based vision-language-action models can benefit from robot experience collected during deployment, but repeatedly acquiring new physical interactions for policy improvement is costly. Offline reinforcement learning provides a natural way to reuse collected experience, while flow-based policies offer an additional opportunity because fresh generation trajectories can be cheaply resampled from the current policy at replay observations. This motivates separating offline value learning from on-policy optimization of the generative process. We propose OVOGO, an offline reinforcement learning framework that realizes this separation for flow-based VLAs. A central challenge in this formulation is to learn a critic that remains grounded in logged robot experience while providing useful value signals for newly generated actions. OVOGO introduces a Return-Anchored Ranking Critic that combines in-sample distributional value learning with guarded local ranking, improving discrimination among generated actions without assigning them artificial return targets. The frozen critic then provides group-relative value signals for selected-transition optimization of fresh action candidates sampled from the current flow policy. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, DexJoCo, and LIBERO, as well as a real-robot dexterous manipulation task, show that OVOGO improves the initial VLA across all four settings and achieves higher success rates than the evaluated offline adaptation and value-guided baselines. These results suggest that offline robot experience can be repeatedly reused for value learning while inexpensive generative sampling supports policy optimization, providing an effective approach to experience-based post-training of flow-based VLAs.

## 9. Skill-Adaptive Noise Scheduling for Diffusion Policies

- 作者：Woo Kyung Kim, Gwangpyo Yoo, Eunyoung Park, Honguk Woo

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T08:25:56Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30264v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30264v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Recent advances in diffusion policies have demonstrated strong effectiveness when integrated into hierarchical skill-based learning frameworks, leveraging offline data to capture multimodal and temporally abstracted behaviors. However, prior approaches have rarely considered the diffusion process as a learnable component, adopting fixed noise schedules applied uniformly across skills, regardless of their distinct behavioral contexts. Such a design fundamentally limits the achievable performance, particularly for contact-rich and dexterous manipulation tasks where different skills exhibit distinct couplings between action dimensions. In this paper, we present a skill-adaptive noise diffusion framework (SaND) which introduces a learnable, skill-conditioned noise schedule that modulates the diffusion process per action dimension, adapting the noising dynamics to each skill. To prevent the learned noise schedule from collapsing into trivial solutions, we employ an auxiliary inverse objective that reconstructs the skill embedding from the Legendre coefficients of the derivative of the noise schedule, encouraging the schedules to remain skill-discriminative. This formulation naturally enables adaptive control over the denoising steps for each skill, where noise schedules are clustered in the Legendre coefficient space, and the appropriate number of denoising steps is determined at the point where reconstruction error abruptly increases. At inference, we identify each skill embedding's cluster from its noise schedule and apply the corresponding number of denoising steps, dedicating more compute to skills requiring fine-grained control and less to simpler ones. In simulation and on real robot hardware, SaND outperforms existing skill learning and planning baselines on contact-rich and dexterous manipulation tasks, achieving higher success rates while requiring fewer denoising steps.

## 14. FATCAT: Fault Injection Attacks Targeting Chip-level Alignment

- 作者：Peter Horvath, Ilia Shumailov, Zhuoran Liu, Lukasz Chmielewski, Lejla Batina

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-11-23T18:01:58Z

- 分类：cs.CR, cs.AI



Model alignment is the primary safety and security mechanism for enforcing refusal of malicious prompts in modern foundation models; yet, existing adversarial evaluations implicitly assume underlying hardware integrity. In this paper, we present FATCAT, the first physical fault-injection attacks capable of eliminating refusal in modern large language and vision-language models. Specifically, we present a non-invasive electromagnetic (EM) fault injection attack targeting GPU-accelerated Systems-on-Chip with physical access, requiring neither software execution privileges nor model-layer access. ## 18. Event2Reward: Learning Robotic Rewards from Progress Relations

- 作者：Junnan Nie, Zitian Fan, Jiachen Zhang, Junyi Lao, Likui Zhang, Chenghao Liu, Xiaoyang Lu, Yunbo Wang, Yixue Zhang, Linbo Zhai, Jiayi Li, Mengzhe Wang, Xichang Wu, Bin Wang, Chang Liu, Tianle Zhang, Songfang Huang

- 来源：arXiv

- 发布时间：2026-10-29T02:34:14Z

- 论文链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39212v1

- PDF 链接：https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39212v1.pdf

- 分类：cs.RO, cs.LG



Learning task progress from robot demonstrations provides a practical way to construct dense shaping rewards for robotic reinforcement learning. A common approach uses normalized demonstration time as progress labels, implicitly assuming monotonically increasing task progress. During policy exploration, however, actual task progress can stall or even regress. Such models may mistake regressions for progress, assigning positive shaping rewards to actions that cause progress loss. Real suboptimal and failed executions offer corrective experience, but assigning absolute progress values to their states is difficult. Our key observation is that stalls, regressions, and recoveries establish local relative progress relations even when absolute progress is unknown. We introduce Event2Reward, which uses normalized temporal labels to anchor the progress scale and these within-trajectory relations to constrain local changes, combining absolute and relative progress supervision. Its progress predictions serve as potentials for reward shaping. We curate an event-annotated dataset from approximately 100K raw real-robot execution records, covering successful, suboptimal, and failed executions, without requiring framewise absolute progress labels. Under the same training budgets, Event2Reward improves average policy success by 18.16 percentage points over a baseline using only sparse task rewards across 40 tasks in four LIBERO suites. On real robots, average success after 30 minutes of wall-clock training reaches 60.0% on three precise insertion tasks and 90.0% on three object manipulation tasks, exceeding the sparse-reward baseline under the same budget by 36.7 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies further show that absolute progress supervision and relative progress relations are complementary, with joint supervision yielding better downstream policy learning.

## 19. A new anthropomorphic robotic hand

- 作者：Immanuel Gaiser, Stefan Schulz, Artem Kargov, Heinrich Klosek, Alexander Bierbaum, Christian Pylatiuk, Reinhold Oberle, Tino Werner, Tamim Asfour, Georg Bretthauer, Rüdiger Dillmann

- 来源：OpenReview

- 发布时间：2008-01-01T00:00:00Z

- 论文链接：https://openreview.net/forum?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- PDF 链接：https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qF7gyHc5Z3

- 分类：Humanoids 2008



This paper presents the new robotic FRH-4 hand. The FRH-4 hand constitutes a new hybrid concept of an anthropomorphic five fingered hand and a three jaw robotic gripper. The hand has a humanoid appearance while maintaining the precision of a robotic gripper. Since it is actuated with flexible fluidic actuators, it exhibits an excellent power to weight ratio. These elastic actuators also ensure that the hand is safe for interacting with humans. In order to fully control the joints, it is equipped with position sensors on all of the 11 joints. The hand is also fitted with tactile sensors based on cursor navigation sensor elements, which allows it to have grasping feedback and the ability for exploration.
